## The game (core; detail in GDD §01, which wins on conflict)
- **What:** single-player real-time tactics with social infiltration, PC, Unity. You command a cell of four (Scout watches, Fixer turns and calls, Tech loops cameras and reroutes patrols, Enforcer takes and fights). The city carries out your plan without knowing it: you turn ordinary people and time their small acts into one plan. Arc: one person → networks → districts.
- **Loop:** Watch → Learn → Turn → Time → Take → Fight → Inherit. Every order shows its full price ($, time, Trail, Alert risk) before you commit. Terms: Trail (campaign evidence from traceable acts), Alert 0–3 (security now), Standing (Hero ↔ Menace per district, starts leaning Hero; moved by seen or found acts, public fights and word of mouth), Heat (Trail + Menace), Talker (gives you up when questioned).
- **Pillars**, testing every feature: subvert people · orchestrate the city · live with it. Tiebreakers in order: readable > rich · people > powers · systems > scripts (nothing hand-authored) · preparation > improvisation · commander > avatar · exteriors only.
- **World:** an alternate history, "a 1950s world that discovered atomic power centuries ago": 1950s style, technology centuries ahead. No thinking machines or networks: robots are clockwork-smart on fixed routines, records are paper and microfilm, cameras are closed-circuit and watched by people, calls are by voice. Information moves through people, so people are the weak point. An abundant city that fears disorder: optimistic surface, coercive underneath. Design test for every object: 1950s style, centuries-ahead tech (crossings have floating signal eyes and light set in the road, never a zebra).
- **The Handover** (GDD §07) is the capstone and the reference every generated mission must match: take Councillor Vale alive at shift handover outside the Civic Records Office, by the front door (Inez the waitress) or at the pickup (the driver). Keep it; its timings and placements may move with the world.
- **Fixed limits:**
  - camera above the street, never first person: play zoom 14 at 38°, opening yaw 30, turned in quarter steps with Q/E (the Records Office front is played at 210); characters 40–60 px at 1080p
  - exteriors only: interiors are timed off-screen actions
  - levels: street 0, deck 9.43 m, sky concourse 24 m
  - 60 fps at play zoom on the 15-inch MacBook Air M4
  - no AI at runtime: content is made at build time, the same orders give the same result, and every run replays exactly
  - rules come only from Keel's rulebook: unknown = reject
- **Look:**
  - Bright retro-future. References: the 26 Sep paintings (`briefs/008/concepts/`) for street level, the AtomicAge and Blocks sheets for the skyline.
  - Materials: cream stone, seafoam bands, chrome. Brass is a thin line (SF-21), never a large surface.
  - Signals (cones red, guard amber, rivals violet) stay readable by drawing unlit and emissive, each with its own shape or motion cue. They don't own their colours: red, amber and violet are free for the world and props (Sky, 2026-10-16, which dropped the crew's ΔE ≥ 20 colour reservation).
  - **Concept art first:** look decisions come from concept art made from an open brief (style contract `concept_prompt`), not from palette tables. Show Sky pictures.
  - People prompts carry no date (a strong 1950s ruined the character concepts). The cell are visitors from a nearby timeline: sharper, no deco or 1950s tailoring.

## Layout

The world holds buildings, structures, street furniture and bays only; no destinations, routes or cast.

Recorded runs replay through the same overlay from their pinned block.
- `contracts/style/`: style contract (machine copy of GDD §15).
- `tools/plan/`: plan checker + CAD sheet. `bun run plan` exits 1 on any failed rule; sheet → `out/plan/`.
- `tools/world/`: world generator + export (`bun run world` → `out/world/harbour-district.world.json`): masses, frontages, levels and connectors, megastructures, street furniture, kit refs; deterministic, seeded by id; exits 1 unless the plan passes.
- `tools/kit/`: `bun run kit`, the kit catalogue and the hash-checked copy of adopted pieces into `unity/Assets/Suborned/Kit/` (git-ignored). `tools/decokit/`: the Blender DecoKit generator (`bun run decokit` → `out/decokit/`).
- `tools/levels/`: the A04 transit pipeline (`bun run levels transit`, `levels:replay`); runs recorded in `runs/levels/`. The submitted copy is the `assignment-04` branch: never change that branch. `tools/gen/`: the run machinery the pipelines share (spend ceiling, continue, replay and verdict, pinned kit and look, ledger, git state).
- `tools/crew/`: crew card generator.
- `tools/style-agent/`: the dialogue style loop (brief 054): Fable writes a conversation from its brief, Motive scores it (SCORE + REASON), Fable revises until 10/10 or its limits. `bun run style:briefs`, `style:run`, `style:chain`, `style:replay`; live runs need `SUBORNED_LIVE=1`. Runs go to `runs/style-agent/`. See its README.
- `paid/` (git-ignored) + `tools/paid/`: all paid-API output (Runware and the like) goes only here, through the helper `tools/paid/paid.py` (a pass needs an explicit budget: no budget, no call; it prints the estimate and stops at the budget), viewed with `bun run paid` (builds `paid/index.html`; opens nothing). Free Codex stills go in the same job folders (`codex/`), at $0. One folder per job `paid/<brief>-<slug>/` with the media, an append-only `ledger.jsonl` (every call: time, model, task, cost, file, prompt, refs, seed, sha256, status, pass, `used_in`) and `passes.json`; the rollup without media, `tools/paid/spend.json`, is committed (brief 053).
- `tools/rag/`: build-time generation core (ported from Assignment 4). Corpus = agent GDD by §, human GDD by heading, plans by part → SurrealDB (HNSW + BM25, RRF) via `compose.yaml` + `services/embeddings`. `runAgent` runs a crew member tool-less (card body as system prompt, only StructuredOutput) and saves card, retrieval, prompt, schema, transcript and output per call. `bun run up`, `ingest`, `retrieve`. Model calls are opt-in, never in tests. One database per corpus hash, so machines and trees can share one server without clobbering each other; an ingest of a corpus already indexed is reused. The Mac Studio can serve the Air over Tailscale (`.env.example`; `bun run doctor` checks it).
- `unity/`: Unity 6000.6.2f1 URP project. Unity MCP via `.mcp.json`. Only Latch edits scenes. `Assets/Scenes/HarbourDistrict.unity` is built from the world export by `HarbourWorldBuilder` (`Assets/Suborned/Editor/World/`) and git-ignored; never edit it by hand.
- `briefs/`: objectives Aster hands out (objective · owner · done-check · evidence · next owner).
- `out/`: generated, git-ignored. Evidence that must ship (JSON, manifests, reports) goes in a committed folder, never only in out/. Screenshots, renders and captures under `briefs/` are git-ignored and stay on disk; only concept art and images a tool reads are committed (the keep list is in `.gitignore`). Scratch goes under `out/scratch/<agent>/` (a probe, a player copy, a tree copy, a round of shots: never `out/<agent><n>/` or a copy of the player in your own folder); the batch's shots rotate in `out/batch/shots` (+ `shots-prev1/2`); a `KEEP` file protects a folder; `bun run storage` reports sizes and leaks, `bun run storage --prune` is a dry run until `--yes` (brief 050).

## Rules
- No commits or deploys unless Sky asks.
- Paid APIs are real money (Sky, 2026-10-23: "just be economical"): Runware, fal, Tripo, World Labs and other paid APIs are charged to Sky. Economical by default: the cheapest model that does the job, few takes, small passes, stopping when a shot is good enough. Every call goes through the paid-assets helper into `paid/` with its ledger, so Sky can audit spend and output in the viewer (brief 053). A pass estimated over $10, or anything that raises a pass's budget, needs Sky's OK first. Subscription Claude calls are exempt. **Stills come from the Codex CLI's image generation first** (Sky, 2026-10-23: "should be using chatgpt cli imagegen as that's free"; it's on Sky's ChatGPT plan): `python3 -I tools/paid/paid.py codex <job> <pass> --name N.png --prompt ... [--ref IMG]`, logged at $0 in the same ledger and viewer. Runware is only for what Codex can't make (video, music, 3D input) or a still Codex has failed at, and say why.
- Crew agents share one working tree: never `git stash`, `checkout`, `reset` or `clean`. To compare with the last commit, use `git diff` or `git show HEAD:<path>`.
- Design changes are discussed with Sky before the GDD is edited; a GDD change is a new version, then `bun run crew`.
- The plan is the source; the sheet and the Unity graybox are derived from it. Edit the plan, never what it generates.
- Agents never drive the browser pane. Verify with the plan checker, `bun run typecheck`, Unity compile and console, screenshots.
- bun · rg / ast-grep · reports as self-contained `.html` · scratch output under `out/`.
- Kill processes by PID only, never `pkill -f <pattern>`: a loose pattern kills other agents' work (Keel's `pkill -f cat` took down Unity, 2026-10-22).
- Code first, test once (Sky, 2026-10-22): when a change that affects UX or play arrives while a build is being tested, stop the testing and roll the change in; run the full batch, shots and review only once the build's content is settled.
- Checks stay under 10 minutes (Sky, 2026-10-22): run the suite with `bun run test` (parallel), not plain `bun test`; play many scripts with `suborned-sim batch` (one process, every core), never one process per script. Anything over 10 minutes: say why and get it sped up (brief 042).
- No load tests, player batches or heavy sweeps while Sky is playing (Sky, 2026-10-22; brief 048): `bun run batch` waits ("Sky is playing; waiting", `--wait-timeout`, `--force`); start any other load through `bun run guard <command>` (tools/game/playing.ts).
