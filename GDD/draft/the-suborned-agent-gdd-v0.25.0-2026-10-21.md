## §01 GAME + CORE (loaded first by every agent)
game: single-player real-time tactics + social infiltration · PC · Unity · camera above street, never first person · exteriors only (interiors = timed off-screen actions; shops, bars and workshops are frontages) · multi-level exteriors: street 0, deck 9.43 m, sky concourse 24 m (sky rail platforms) · mission = 1 block, 8–12 min, live clock
fantasy: commander. The city carries out your plan without knowing it: turn ordinary people, time their small acts into one plan. You plan on the map; you may drop into one agent to walk the plan through at street level [lab] (§10).
arc: one person → networks → districts. Every system adds to or tests that growth.
look: bright retro-future (pastel cream + peach stone, saturated enamel accents, lagoon water, coral transit, chrome; skyline of towers, rockets, planets). Optimistic surface, coercive underneath.
world: an alternate history, not a forecast: "a 1950s world that discovered atomic power centuries ago". Culture and style are 1950s, technology centuries ahead: robots, trams and the rest are 1950s in style and far advanced inside. Not "the 1950s imagining the year 2000". People wear the city's space-age couture, never 1950s period dress (§15 people).
- atomic power made energy, travel and building cheap (sky rail, skyports, towers, liners): everything physical is abundant.
- thinking machines and networks never came: no networked computing; robots are clockwork-smart and follow fixed routines; records are physical (microfilm, punch cards, pneumatic tubes, stamped forms). Information moves through people and paper, so people are the weak point: why the game is about suborning them (pillar subvert people; tiebreaker people > powers).
- robots look futuristic and think old-school: a machine sees only the way its lens or face points, never all round (§05).
- the city fears disorder most: an abundant, orderly city runs on routine and paperwork; hence optimistic surface, coercive underneath.
- design test for every object: 1950s style, centuries-ahead technology (crossing signals float, markings need not be paint).
- reads: cameras are closed-circuit, watched by people · a watcher's log is a physical record that investigators pull · calls are voice between people (telephone, radio) · "networks" in the arc and transit = people and lines, never computers · "no AI at runtime", fight AI and AI chatter are build terms, not the world.
pillars (test):
- subvert people: does knowing more make a person cheaper, quieter or more loyal?
- orchestrate the city: does it open or close a window the player can see coming?
- live with it: will the player meet this choice again?
tiebreakers, in order: readable > rich · people > powers · systems > scripts (nothing hand-authored) · preparation > improvisation · commander > avatar · exteriors only
pace: real-time play is the default; pause is a tool, not the mode. You still command from above and prepare before you act. The rules show in the street, not in text: the systems stay deep, and the player reads them from people, faces, rings, lines and locks (§10). Fights are Syndicate-simple: hit chances, never aiming.
terms: Trail = campaign evidence from traceable acts, drives investigations · Alert 0–3 = security response now (routine, inquiry, search, lockdown) · security level S 0–4 = how hard a district answers an alarm (lax, routine, watchful, guarded, swarm): the response dial (§05) · Standing = Hero ↔ Menace per district: acts seen or found, a public fight once by opponent, and word of mouth between missions; starts leaning Hero (+2) · Heat = campaign T + Menace (Menace = how far Standing is below 0), read at a mission's start: it schedules fights and sets how fast watchers notice a loiterer (§05, §06) · bravery (helped → Fighter) / resentment (coerced → Informer) · Talker = fear + resentment > bravery + loyalty, gives you up when questioned · left behind = an agent not in the getaway car when it leaves: captured or walks out (§06, §08)
house rules:
- build only against Keel's rulebook (each event: needs, changes, witnesses). Unknown = reject.
- no AI at runtime. Content generated at build time. Same orders → same result. Any run replays exactly from start state + orders (a driven agent's moves are orders too).
- a rule behind a playground or fight-lab switch [lab] is not the game: it runs only when a run's start turns it on, and with it off every run replays byte-identical.
- handoff = inputs · done-check · next owner.
- only Latch edits Unity scenes. Price paid runs before starting.
- models and textures ship at full detail: no decimation, triangle caps, slim copies or texture downscaling without Sky's sign-off (§12).
- escalate to Sky (via Aster) only: pillar change, new paid spend, release, destructive action, unresolved conflict.

## §02 LOOP + RESOURCES
mission: Watch → Learn → Turn → Time → Take → Fight → Inherit
campaign: turned people pay cash/arms/tips → turn more people (snowball). Contacts grow braver or bitter. T + Standing set pushback.
| resource | scope | drives | from |
| Cash | campaign | fees, debts, bribes, turning | sources (skimming clerk, bookmaker) |
| Arms | campaign | winning fights, arming joiners; how many of your people carry a gun this mission (one sidearm per point; the Enforcer's carbine is the cell's) | sources (quartermaster, hijack) |
| Assets | campaign | access, income, fight allies | Fixer, introductions |
| Time | mission | actions 1–62 s (Board 1 s, a kill 3 s, a knockout 6 s, Watch 20 s); events don't wait | — |
| Trail | campaign | investigations; part of Heat | traceable acts: threats, visible payments, people hit by gunfire, people taken down (knockout +4, kill +6, charged when a report is confirmed or the victim gives a statement), missing persons (+2), an agent captured (+4), a getaway seen by security (+1) |
| Alert 0–3 | mission | security response now | reports that land (a witness, a camera, a missed check-in, a missing person, a loiterer), found faults, shots |
| Security level S 0–4 | district | the response's size and speed | the district's base, its open cases, the mission (§05) |
| Standing | district | who agrees, crowd behaviour | acts seen or found, public fights, word of mouth (§04) |
| Heat | campaign | how often fights come (§06); how fast watchers notice (§05) | campaign T + Menace, read once at a mission's start |
rule: every order shows its full price before commit ($, time, T, A risk, who would see it). In real time: one line per way on the hover chip and on the ring at the target, the move ring on the ground, Alt for the breakdown, the going-loud card (§10).
travel [S]: boarding a tram or sky rail is fast travel round the city: board at any stop or station, ride to any other on the network. Price = time only: wait for the next service + the ride, both from the timetable. No fare.
chain: Handover route A is itself a chain (watch café → clear Inez's debt → loop camera → her call pulls the guard → take Vale). Each person sees only their part; city investigates backward, link by link [S]. Later operations chain more links across a district [L].

## §03 CELL
| op | verbs | notes |
| Scout | Watch, Listen, Mislead | watches from furthest (30 m), so the Scout alone can watch everything from outside every cone; Listen reveals need and temperament; false tips send rivals away; fires as untrained |
| Fixer | Turn, Call, Watch | offer/pay/help/threaten; calls contacts in on cue; the face witnesses remember; watches at 12 m; fires as untrained |
| Tech | Loop, Reroute, Watch | blinds surveillance, misdirects patrols; tethered while a loop holds (no move, no Watch); watches at 24 m; fires as untrained |
| Enforcer | Enter, Take, Takedown, Fire, Watch | the only op who knocks out or kills in silence (§06) and who carries Vale; carries the carbine; watches only at 3 m |
watch [S]: every agent can Watch people, cameras, the patrol and containers: 20 s, $0, 0 T, no Alert of its own, and every watcher learns the same facts (a routine, a watcher's sight (its cone) and check-in marks, a container's round, an affair seen together). Reach is the agent's skill, on a clear line at eye height, people and crowds blocking: Scout 30 m · Tech 24 m · Fixer 12 m · Enforcer 3 m. A Watch out of reach walks first to the nearest spot in reach with a line (never chosen by cones the cell hasn't learned). The patrol's routine needs 40 s of watched time, two agents watching at once counted once. Listen (42 s, 3 m) and Mislead (44 s) stay the Scout's. So the opening recce splits the cell, and each agent must stand where it isn't watched (§05 attention).
all ops [S]: any op can carry a sidearm (one per Arms point) and Fire (§06); F draws and holsters. Every op can run (double-click, or G on a moving op) at 2.8 m/s, twice the walk, and is noticed (§05). Any op can carry a body (the Enforcer at the walk, 1.4 m/s; the others at 0.7 m/s), put it down (1 s), restrain a living one (8 s) and stash it (§05 bodies). Any op can Board the getaway car and Go (§06 getaway). Nobody runs while carrying Vale, a downed op or a body, while tethered or mid-action; nobody fires while running or carrying, and the Enforcer doesn't fire while he holds Vale.
founding cell [S]: the training cell starts sworn, not fearless: bravery 2 (accomplice), loyalty 3 (sworn), fear 0, resentment 0. Recruits keep the scales they were turned with. Your own people answer to the same Talker test as anyone (§04, §08 captives): a recruit you pressured talks first.
training: full cell. campaign: starts Fixer only; recruit the rest by turning people with the skill [S].

## §04 PEOPLE
person = routine + need + temperament + memory. Knowledge lowers price; standing decides who listens.
approaches (Inez, training) [B]:
| approach | requires | $ | time | T | leaves |
| Ask cold | — | 0 | 25 s | +1 | remembered refusal: later offers +$10,000, +1 T |
| Pay | routine known (Watch 20 s [S], any agent) | 70,000 | 50 s | +2 | traceable payment; neutral |
| Help (clear debt) | need known (Listen 42 s) | 40,000 | 50 s | +1 | gratitude; bravery can grow |
| Pressure | need known | 0 | 35 s | +3 | fear; resentment grows; can identify Fixer |
purse: pay + driver = 110,000 > 100,000; help + driver = 80,000 fits → learning buys the second route.
private [S]: Ask, Pay, Help and Listen have no witnesses: a conversation is private, so onlookers learn nothing, and they add no witness Trail, no Standing, no incident and nothing told along ties. The approach's own price stands (its Trail, time and cash; Pay is still traceable money; a Loyal subject still reports an approach). Pressure is not private: a threat is seen, remembered and passed on. A conversation never reads as loitering (§05 attention).
temperament [S]:
| type | responds to | Hero | Menace |
| Idealist | Help; refuses bribes | volunteers free; bravery fast | refuses; may report |
| Mercenary | Pay | overcharges | discount; sells you to highest bidder |
| Fearful | Pressure | hesitates; needs Help ×2 | folds free |
| Loyal (institution) | trust over time | turns after repeated Help | reports any approach |
Hero and Menace campaigns turn different halves of the city = replay value.
bravery [S]: Informant → Helper → Accomplice (hides op, drives) → Fighter (joins armed). +1 step per job completed unexposed.
resentment [S]: Compliant → Resentful → Informer (sells a route → ambush scheduled later). +1 step per pressure or exposure.
talker [S]: questioned, a person gives up known facts if fear + resentment > bravery + loyalty (deterministic). Facts: saw the Fixer's face, saw the Enforcer's face (a witness of a takedown, or a victim who saw him coming), knows cell car, saw where/when. A captured agent knows more: every agent's face, the cell's car, the mission's place and time, and every contact the cell has turned (§08). A person who reported volunteers place and time. Brave helped contact lies → nothing or false lead.
scales [S]: every number reads as a word: bravery informant·helper·accomplice·fighter (0–3) · resentment compliant·resentful·informer (0–2) · fear calm·uneasy·afraid·terrified (0–3) · loyalty none·obliged·trusting·sworn (0–3). Loyalty in the talker test = loyalty to the cell, 0 at start; loyalty to an employer or the civic order is the Loyal temperament. Start: everyone 0, except Idealist bravery 1 (helper), Fearful fear 1 (talks when questioned until helped) and the founding cell (§03).
wrong approach [S]: an approach that doesn't suit the temperament still works one step worse (one more Help, or a higher price; Keel sets the numbers); an approach the temperament refuses (Pay to an Idealist) fails and leaves a remembered refusal.
preview: an order always shows its price; whether it will work shows as "?" until the temperament is known: Watch gives where and when, Listen gives who they are.
needs [S]: each person has one need, a kind and a record.
- kinds are a short closed list of mechanics: debt · cash · housing · papers · kin · care · protection · advancement. A kind decides which approaches act on it, what it requires (kin: a family tie; protection: a rival tie; advancement: work; checked in the data) and how Help pays; care is met by Help only. A new kind is a rules change: the model may propose one, Sky accepts it; the model never adds one (unknown = reject).
- records are the open part ("Pawn ticket", "Clearance order", "Brother held"): as many as the world needs, written by the model per area at build time and bound by code to real people and places (§09). A record changes play only through the fields rules read: size (sets Help's price), holder (the person or office that could meet the need without your money) and subject (read by tie and arrest events); every other word is its label. Two records never differ only in wording: each differs in size, holder or kind. The city is abundant, so most needs are paper, permission, status, place and people, not money.
- favour route [S]: once the cell has turned a need's holder, Help can be a favour from them instead of cash: $0, +1 T, once per holder per mission.
- Intel shows the kind's word while the need is only told ("Debt") and the record's label once Listen has run ("Bureau loan"); Alt draws a line to the holder.
- secrets are separate: an affair comes from a lover tie; other secrets are records with their own labels.
- a met need is replaced between missions, drawn from the person's own stream (id + mission number); a turned contact tells it free in the debrief, anyone else needs Listen again. Needs born of the cell's own harm (a family left in need by a killing, a relative held after an arrest) are Help-only [L].
ties [S]: family ≤ 4 · workmate ≤ 3 (same employer) · regular ≤ 3 out, 12 in (their routine stops at the unit while its staff are on shift) · debtor ≤ 2 owed, 6 in · rival ≤ 1 (same trade or employer) · lover; 1–8 per person, nobody isolated, nobody a hub. Introductions travel only along family, workmate and regular ties.
routines [S]: people follow baked routines (§09); two shapes are general rules, never written for one person:
- rounds: a server who stands a door with a terrace of tables beside it works the tables. She rests at her post 24–36 s, then goes to the seated table that has waited longest (a new party is greeted 10 s after it sits, a seated table is visited again every 75–105 s; most overdue first, then nearest), stands 130 cm from it facing it for 12–18 s, and walks back at 1.4 m/s; never to an empty table, and never before 0:30, so a mission opens with her at her door. While one of the cell speaks to her (Listen, Ask, Pay, Help, Pressure) she stands still, from the order to its landing + 3 s, and an order inside those 3 s extends the hold. At Alert 2, a shot, a body found or the final fight she walks back and stands her post for the rest of the mission. A Watch learns the round, so the Listen window and the quiet corner of the far tables can be planned.
- partners: two people tied as lovers who meet stop, face each other and stand together instead of passing. Summoned (§07), he walks his usual route and stops at her free side: the first point on a 1.2 m ring round her post, nearest to her side first, with nobody within 1 m of it; he faces her, she faces him, he stays 60 s, they hold 2 s and he goes. By chance: two lovers both out on the street, neither on a post, who come within 8 m on a clear line each walk to the other's side and stand facing 20 s, once a mission per pair.
knowledge [S]: a person knows a fact only if they witnessed it (clear sightline within 13 m, §05) or were told it along a tie: face to face within 20 m, or by phone to a tie indoors (calls are voice, §01); a told fact is never told on. At the start nobody knows anything about the cell, and the cell is known by face only (no alias [L]). A keeper knows the needs of their workmates and regulars (§08 tips). Facts travel along ties, never opinion; the one feeling that travels is the word of a person the cell helped or harmed, between missions (standing, below).
restricted [S]: guards, patrol officers and investigators are Loyal or Mercenary; councillors, watchers, pawnbrokers and credit bureaux are never Idealist. Every trade and role carries ≥ 2 temperaments; no temperament over 60 % of a cast row; a unit of ≥ 3 staff carries ≥ 2.
between missions: promoted (new access) · arrested (lost unless freed) · introduces a friend (new contact) · comes home after a knockout and gives a statement (unless quieted) · grieves a found killing (a turned contact takes the saw-violence move; other family, lover and workmate ties fear +1) · worries for a missing person (those ties fear +1) · tells their ties how the cell treated them (standing) · a captured agent is questioned (§08). Debrief reports each with cause.
standing [S]: per district, an integer; Hero band at +3 or more, Menace band at −3 or less. Every district starts at +2, leaning Hero: if nobody sees you, the city assumes you are good. One known kindness reaches the band.
- in a mission only acts seen or found move it: −1 per seen incident of a threat (Pressure), violence, a drawn weapon, a takedown or a carry; −1 for a found killing, even unseen; a missing person 0.
- a public fight (city people within 30 m of any shot; it ends 30 s after the last shot, or with the final fight, which is judged when it ends or when the getaway car leaves) moves it once, when it ends, by opponent: fired on the guard, patrol, officers or robots −1; fired first on rivals the district does not fear −1; broke rivals the district fears +1; anything else 0, including leaving before feared rivals break. How the public sees a mission's rivals is mission data (the Handover's: feared); a district default comes with the campaign. A capture moves nothing: the public judges the fight, not who was left.
- between missions, word of mouth: each person the cell Helped tells their ties (+1); each it Pressured or knocked out tells of the harm (−1); once per person, not per tie; helped and harmed counts as harmed; at most ±2 per district per mission. The word is a feeling, not a fact: no face, place or time, nothing for investigators.
- drift: a district nothing moved this mission moves 1 step back toward +2, from either side.
Hero: help people (their word), shield civilians, drive off feared rivals in public, free arrested locals. Menace: shoot in crowds, public threats, harm bystanders, fight the police, a killing found; a rampage turns the whole city against the cell [L] (§05). An unseen threat moves Standing only through the victim's word. Neither end loses.
| | Hero | Menace |
| turning | slower, dearer, loyal | faster, cheaper, betrayal risk |
| crowds | join fights, hide ops | obey from fear, report, scatter under fire |
| fights | fewer; police hesitate in crowds | more often; harder |
| street | graffiti, cheering, free tips | wanted posters, averted eyes, empty pavements |

## §05 PRESSURE
urgency = scheduled events with reasons, never an arbitrary countdown. Each mission defines its closing event.
security: the door guard's routine (café stop pre-shift, to see Inez) [B] · every staff post (guards, patrol officers, watchers) checks in by voice every 90 s from the start of its shift, and at its end; a missed mark is a report that lands at the mark, Alert 1 [S] · cameras: fixed cones, blocked by roofs/parked vehicles, on a screen a person scans every 20 s [S] · the player sees a watcher's cone only once the cell has watched it; a watcher not yet watched shows "?" and still sees you (§03, §10) [S] · patrol sweep, breaks to gunfire within 30 m [B] · the response comes by the dial (below); the district seals the level's seal time after the later of a confirmation at Alert ≥ 2 and the response on the street (2:25 at S1 and S2) [S].
rivals [B]: same target, own schedule. False tip: away 3:50. Gunfire: arrive within 16 s.
| A | security does | raised by |
| 0 routine | follows schedules | — |
| 1 inquiry [S] | robots check the spot; patrol tightens | a landed report of a found knockout, a woken victim, a noise, a missed check-in, a missing person or a loiterer; a found fault; once per incident |
| 2 search [B] | the response unit and robots to the place; the search opens stashes within 30 m | a 2nd incident; a landed report of a found kill or a carry seen; any gunfire at once (sets A ≥ 2; no per-shot +1 A) |
| 3 lockdown [B] | doors lock (S2 and up); cordon units at the district mouths; district seals | a landed report of a takedown seen; gunfire or violence seen by a civilian at once |
alert rule [S]: silent acts (a takedown, a carry, a body, a missing person, a loiterer) raise Alert when a report lands, never at sight; gunfire at once, because security hears it. Witness Trail and Standing still move at sight. A knockout found or woken never raises Alert above 1 (a takedown seen is 3 on landing, knockout or not). One takedown is one incident and one dispatch, however many find it.
sight [S]: machines (cameras, the patrol robot, the response's robots) see only through their cone: behind a lens or a robot's face is safe, even close up. People (guards, officers) see through their cone and also sense anyone within 3 m on a clear line, whatever their facing.
attention [S]: watchers notice loiterers. Every security watcher on the street keeps a clock per agent: each live camera (not looped, not blocked), the door guard (not the cell's ally), the patrol robot and the response's officers and robots. Civilians and rivals keep none.
- each second: in the watcher's sight, standing still, idle or Watching → +1 s · in sight in any other act (Listen, a turn, Call, Loop, Reroute, Mislead, Enter, Take, a takedown, a carry, restraining, stashing) → held; the act's own seen-rules apply · moving, or out of sight → −1 s, to 0, or to the wary mark once wary.
- the posted guard's 3 m sense counts for the clock; a takedown's awareness at the order keeps his cone, so a blow from his blind side still lands, but lingering beside him first does not.
- noticed: a warning only · wary: for the rest of the mission; the clock never drains below the wary mark again, and a wary person is aware of that agent at a takedown (unarmed he struggles and screams; the armed guard beats it and draws, A ≥ 2) · report: a loiterer reported, once per agent per watcher per mission: a found fault, A ≥ 1, no Trail, logged as a sighting; a camera's lands at its watcher's next scan + 3 s, a person's or a robot's by radio after 3 s; a watcher downed before it lands drops it; the dial dispatches its Alert-1 response (S2: 2 robots).
- the marks scale with the cell's Heat, read once at the mission's start (the start state's heat, else the mission's start.heat, else 0):
| Heat | band (the briefing's word) | noticed | wary | report |
| 0–2 | unknown | 8 s | 20 s | 45 s |
| 3–5 | noticed | 6 s | 18 s | 35 s |
| 6–9 | wanted | 5 s | 15 s | 25 s |
| 10+ | infamous | 3 s | 12 s | 18 s |
- wary comes at or under 20 s in every band, so a whole Watch inside a sight line always makes the watcher wary; only an infamous cell is reported (18 s) before a Watch ends, so a famous cell can't Watch from inside a sight line at all.
- each watcher shows its stage on the street (§10): a camera's lens, a robot's face, a person's head. A cue shows only once an agent is in its sight, so it never draws a cone the cell hasn't learned.
witness [S]: clear sightline within 13 m of a traceable act → +1 T per incident, moves standing. Turning is private (§04): Ask, Pay, Help and Listen have no witnesses. A turned witness (loyalty ≥ 1) adds no witness Trail for a takedown or a carry and makes no report. Crowds also block guard/camera sightlines.
reports [S]: a fact reaches security only through a person (no networks, §01). Whoever notices a takedown, a carry or a body reacts by the first row that holds: (1) a person turned for this very act (the driver who brought the car): never its witness · (2) staff: radio from where they stand (lands in 5 s) · (3) kin of the victim (lover, family): report at once · (4) turned (loyalty ≥ 1): keep quiet · (5) an Informer: report · (6) terrified: flee, never report · (7) Loyal: report · (8) Idealist: walk over to check the body, then report · (9) Mercenary: keep quiet until Alert 2, then report · (10) Fearful: flee an act, avoid a body, tell a tie. A reporter walks to the nearest report point (a staffed door, or a call box), running after an act and walking after a body, and calls (6 s); the report lands. A live camera's lands at its next scan + 3 s; a robot's in 4 s. A reporter taken down before the report lands drops it. Whoever quiets, avoids, flees or reports also tells one tie: face to face within 20 m, or by phone to a tie indoors. No Tech action cuts a call box.
call boxes [S]: stand where a real city puts them: on the pavement at a corner, in sight of the junction (6–25 m from it, bigger streets first), outside stations, stops and civic doors, never narrowing the walk; every street point is within 40 m of one (R-REPORT, §09).
bodies [S]: civilians see bodies; bodies don't disappear, and the Enforcer has to take them to a quiet place. A person knocked out or killed stays where they fell until moved; kills are shown, not cut away. An open body is found by anyone within 13 m on a clear line and by watchers in their cones. Only a stash hides one: a container (5 s on the street, seen as a carry; holds 1) or a service door (10 s, off-screen; holds 3). A container is emptied on a round every 15:00, its time learnable by Watch, in the mission only. A knocked-out victim wakes after 2:00, staggers 4 s and reports the assault (Alert 1; his facts become leads); hidden, he calls out every 10 s, heard within 20 m; restrained, he is silent until freed. On landing: a found knockout 1, a found kill 2, a carry seen 2, a takedown seen 3. A found body, knocked out or killed, brings a unit of officers at any Alert and holds the scene: the door the body lies at and every door within 13 m in sight of it refuse Enter and any exit until the unit stands down (1:00 after the last alarm at Alert 1, 3:00 at Alert 2, never at Alert 3). A cancelled Enter comes out by a door no unit holds; with every door held, the Enforcer waits inside until one is free. A body moved before the unit arrives makes the report unfounded: Alert stays, no seal. At Alert ≥ 2 the unit's search opens every stash within 30 m (10 s each). Preparation earns the right to leave a body: with the camera looped and the patrol rerouted, nobody may find it in time.
suspicion [S]: a person who expects someone and finds them missing grows suspicious: one due to meet a lover, family member or workmate at a stop they share (from routines, never authored) who is there without them raises a missing-person alarm after 20 s (Alert 1). A turned waiter says nothing; a person being Taken reports nothing.
response [S]: a dial, data per district and mission, not code, so the play area can be swarmed with robots to temper the game. S = the district's base (data in the plan) + ⌊open cases ÷ 2⌋ (at most +2) + the mission's bonus. The Handover runs at S2.
| S | name | dispatch | Alert 1 sends | Alert ≥ 2 sends | +1 unit every | unit cap | seal | Alert 3 adds |
| 0 | lax | 20 s | 1 robot | 1 unit | — | 1 | 3:00 | — |
| 1 | routine | 12 s | 1 robot | 1 unit | — | 1 | 2:25 | — |
| 2 | watchful | 10 s | 2 robots | 1 unit + 2 robots | 40 s | 3 | 2:25 | +1 unit, doors lock |
| 3 | guarded | 8 s | 2 robots | 2 units + 2 robots | 30 s | 4 | 2:00 | +1 unit, doors lock |
| 4 | swarm | 6 s | 3 robots | 2 units + 4 robots | 20 s | 6 | 1:30 | +2 units, doors lock |
A unit is 2 officers; a robot is an armed patrol unit, and robots count against the 30-combatant cap. An alarm sends the response to the scene, then to where the missing person's post, car and close ties are meant to be now, then to the alarm-raiser's ties (up to 3 each); robots go only to places within 60 m of where they start. Fighting out is a last resort, not a route.
rampage [L]: an escalation ladder stands above the dial. Being seen killing civilians climbs it fast, and at its top the city defence force sends armed flying drones (mini-guns; clockwork-smart, on fixed routines) against the cell. Escape there is near impossible most of the time, never scripted impossible: a cell strong enough may fight the drones, but the city has turned against it (Standing deep in Menace, contacts refuse): it has failed to suborn the city. Rules, AI and art come when the item opens.
running [S]: a runner is noticed where an act would be: people with a clear line within 13 m, and the cones of the guard, patrol, live cameras, response unit and rivals. People remember where and when (and the Fixer's face if he ran): a lead, no T, no standing. Security that sees a runner counts a found fault: A ≥ 1, once per run; a guard who saw a runner no longer fights at your side. Running is free once a fight is on, the run is loud, or the op is under fire. The police never need to win a foot race: Vale can't run, so the getaway is decided at him (§06).
drawn weapon [S]: seen by security → A ≥ 2 and that watcher engages (reacts 2 s, then fires); seen by people within 13 m → one witnessed incident (+1 T, A ≥ 1), and they remember the face; unseen costs nothing.
alarm inside [S]: once A ≥ 2 the target's building hides them: Enter +30 s (Handover 38 → 68 s).
levels [S]: street, deck and sky concourse are all exterior. Sightlines and the 13 m run in 3D: a slab blocks sight straight up and down, an open edge does not. People change level only by stair, ramp, escalator or lift (time from the plan). Watchers stand per level; a station watcher covers its concourse.
watchers [S]: cameras and civic robots stand at key places on the district plan (stations, stops, gates, squares). Any op in a watcher's sightline is logged with place + time, act or no act. Once a Trail is open, investigators can pull the log: each sighting is a lead (place + time). Hot (Heat over threshold, Motive sets it) or Menace standing in the district: a sighting counts as a witness report (Alert table).
investigators [S][L]: on foot question ~every 20 s; cars move fast, stop to question; count rises with A and T. Pick civilians near last incident by sightline + distance, not luck. Useful answer = fact with place + time → go there, question again; ~60–90 s per hop. A hunted car (§08) is a lead they follow.
visible: questioning icon, dotted line joining held leads, unit following it.
counters: keep moving or stand out of sight (sightings go stale, attention cools) · reach witness first (pay/reassure/threaten; threat adds fear) · Scout false tip · citizens block cars · break sightlines in a crowd.
output: findings → debrief Investigation state → next mission. Hero campaign: brave locals give false leads.

## §06 FIGHTS
fights measure preparation (arms, brave contacts, streets). Not a separate combat game: fast and real-time, Syndicate-simple (hit chances, not aiming); a fight is short, lethal and decided by what you prepared.
frequency [S]: Heat = campaign T + Menace standing. Threshold crossed → fight event (raid, ambush, rival hit) in next mission. No dice. Heat also sets how fast watchers notice (§05 attention).
The security level S (§05) is a separate axis: Heat brings fights to the cell; S makes a district watch harder.
size [S]: act I 3–6 hostiles · II 8–15 · III 20–30. Hard cap 30 simulated combatants; rest = background crowd (run/cheer only).
your side: cell + Accomplice/Fighter contacts + crowd joiners (Hero), armed up to Arms stock.
citizen actions: scatter (breaks sightlines, covers retreat) · block (lorry, stalled tram, crowd vs police vehicles) · join (bare-handed or armed) · vehicle strike (ram barricade or rigged vehicle; driver always jumps clear).
content line: civilians are never the payload; no coerced suicide attacks. Menace can be uglier (collateral, witnesses) but no civilian dies as the weapon. No stray shots: a miss never hits a bystander.
health [S]: civilian 2 · guard/officer 3 · rival 3 · patrol unit 4 · Scout/Fixer/Tech 4 · Enforcer 6. Pips on select and over all in a fight. Weapons (Arms): pistol 1 dmg, heavier 2–3. Armour via quartermaster, healing via turned doctor (people, not upgrades).
armed [S]: any op can carry a sidearm (pistol), one per Arms point; the Enforcer's carbine is the cell's and uses none. The Enforcer stays the heavy (carbine, health 6, the only one who carries Vale). The Handover issues all four; the campaign starts with the Fixer alone and Arms 0.
takedown [S]: anyone on the street can be targeted; there is no named list, and the cost comes only from who sees it (a lone killing in front of a camera is very likely to end in failure). The Enforcer, within 1.5 m, knocks a person out (6 s) or kills them (3 s), silently: no gunfire rule applies, no money. A target who sees him coming (or witnessed one of the cell's acts this mission, or is wary of him, §05) is aware: unarmed, he struggles (+3 s), screams (heard within 20 m) and holds the Enforcer's face; armed, the takedown fails and it is a fight. Knockout: the victim wakes and talks unless hidden, restrained or turned. Kill: never talks, but a found body is far worse (§05, §08). Not targets: the patrol robot and cameras (Reroute and Loop are their verbs). Vale: a kill fails the mission; a knockout is refused (use Take).
| weapon [S] | dmg | every | point blank | effective | long |
| pistol | 1 | 1.5 s | ≤ 5 m: 1000 ‰ | ≤ 15 m: 750 ‰ | ≤ 16 m: 400 ‰ |
| carbine | 2 | 2.0 s | ≤ 5 m: 1000 ‰ | ≤ 20 m: 750 ‰ | ≤ 30 m: 400 ‰ |
modifiers [S] (multiply, ‰): shooter walking 500 · target still beside cover 500 · untrained (Scout, Fixer, Tech, rivals) 750; Enforcer, officers and the guard are trained. No reload or ammunition: Arms is the limit. Draw 1.0 s, holster 0.5 s, first shot on a new target 1.0 s; a hostile surprised outside a fight reacts in 2.0 s.
hits [S]: deterministic, a hit rate, not a roll: each shooter keeps an integer accumulator per target (starts 500, + accuracy per shot, a hit at 1000). Shown as "hits 3 in 4 · down in n s"; the cursor shows the band by shape. No dice.
orders [S]: fire (until the target is down, or the final fight is over; holds aim 3 s if the line breaks; walking keeps firing at the walking rate, running cancels it; a fire order out of range walks into the weapon's effective ring on a clear line first, then fires) · a fire on a hostile with the gun holstered draws as part of the order, the draw time in its price · cease · stance: hold (fires only when ordered), return (default on draw: fires at whoever fires on this op), at will (fires at any combatant in reach, nearest first) · panic: every armed op draws and goes to at will, only once a fight is on. Combatant = a final-fight hostile, or anyone who has fired on or aims at the cell; never a civilian, contact, Vale, or a guard or patrol that hasn't engaged, so only an explicit fire starts a loud run. Group focus fire = one fire order to every selected op. When the final fight is over (every hostile down, broken or gone), every fire order on a final-fight hostile ends and a new one is refused: a broken crew is no target, so no shot after the final fight opens a fight of its own.
holster [S]: F draws or holsters the selection and stays as you set it. A gun drawn by a click or by panic holsters itself 4 s after the fight is over, unless a hostile is still in sight.
cost [S]: per person, not per shot: +2 T per person hit (once), +2 T more if they go down (a dropped guard = 4 T). A takedown costs the same per person without the gunfire: knockout +4 T, kill +6 T, charged when a report of it is confirmed or the victim gives a statement; a kill never found is a missing person (+2 T, §08); any gunfire → A ≥ 2 (§05); violence seen by a civilian → 3. Shots in the final fight are untraced (no T, no A): the fight is the consequence, not a new act; shots before it count. Gunfire still calls the response unit, draws rivals in 16 s, breaks the patrol within 30 m, moves Menace if witnessed. Shooting a contact ends the relationship. Shooting the target = fail.
hostiles [S] (Pulse): advance to the nearest free cover point (made at build time) toward their objective (the getaway car in the final fight, else where they last saw the cell), firing nothing while they move; engage when a foe is in effective reach. Target priority: whoever shoots at them → whoever holds Vale → nearest armed op → nearest op; never Vale, never a civilian. Rivals break when more than half are down; police hold. Crowds within 30 m of gunfire scatter.
answering fire [S]: an armed hostile shot at (hit or miss) by a shooter it can't answer moves, and its whole side moves with it, 0.5 s after the shot (one incident, one dispatch: the second officer of a pair never waits for his own first shot). A body already trading fire with anyone keeps its own fight.
- police (the response unit, final-fight officers, police units; never robots) close in by the grid route to 90 % of their effective ring of the shooter, at 2.2 m/s, re-planning every 1 s. Before the final fight each body walks at most 20 m for the run; with that spent it takes the nearest cover that shields it from the shooter and holds. The alarm lapses 10 s after the last shot on its side.
- rivals take the cover point that shields them from the shooter, else the nearest free spot within 8 m with no line to him; they don't advance on a sniper. The pre-fight crew steps out to cover and walks back once no shot has landed for 3 s, arriving late by its detour, never early.
- in the final fight, a police body shot from out of its reach takes the cover that shields it and fires back while the shooter is inside its weapon's max ring (from where it stands, at its band's odds); if the shooter is beyond its max, it holds that cover at most 3 s, then closes to the shielding cover nearest its stop point (else that point in the open), re-planning every 3 s. It never chases a runner. The blocker keeps its lane and answers only from it.
- on the street: the side turns to the shooter, police run to a stop point drawn as a move ring, rivals duck behind cover (§10).
getaway [S]: the cell gets into the car and drives away; the Enforcer can't run, so the others support him.
- Board: an order on the getaway car, by any op within 1.5 m of it, not running, tethered, mid-action or carrying a body: 1 s, or 4 s carrying (the carried go in first; only the Enforcer loads Vale). In the car an op is off the street and doesn't fire, and can still be hit as a target beside cover (×500): hiding in a held car loses. A move on a boarded op steps out at the car's side (1 s); Vale stays in. Standing at the car is not boarding. Allies (the guard, joiners) never board.
- Go: an order on the car by anyone once someone is in it. The car leaves at once with whoever is inside and the mission ends that tick. Go is refused while the car is held, and the refusal names the holders. Go without Vale is the cell withdrawing by car (Withdrawn, left-behind rules apply); the menu's Withdraw stays a free abort that captures nobody.
- the hold: a combatant not down, broken or gone holds the car while it stands within 8 m of the car (any level, line or not) or on the exit lane: the first 30 m of the getaway route's carriageway ahead of the car at the road's width, derived from the plan's getaway route (no route, no mission: unknown = reject). An ambusher lying in wait holds only if its spot does.
- the blocker: one hostile per fight goes for the road: in the safe branch the ambusher with the shortest grid walk to its post, moving when the ambush springs; in the loud branch the first officer on the street to reach his post (ties by id). Its post is the nearest free cover inside the lane 10–13 m from the car (beyond the hold and point blank, inside the cell's effective reach), else the open lane centreline 10 m along it. It walks there without stopping or firing, holds it as police hold a stand, and is never replaced: down it or break its crew (a rival blocker breaks with its crew) and the car is free. A warning (Inez's 10 s) doesn't hold its walk.
- left behind, at the tick the car leaves: a downed op not in the car is captured · a standing op is captured if a combatant is still up and not broken, or the seal clock runs · otherwise he walks out, free, and is back next mission. Captures happen only at Go; a failed mission captures nobody (§08 captives).
- seen leaving: a police officer, a robot or an engaged patrol up and with the car in its cone or within 13 m on a clear line (a final-fight officer: a clear line within its weapon's max, 16 m) makes the departure one witnessed incident: +1 T, and the investigation holds the car as a hunted car next mission (§08). Rivals don't report. The final fight's shots stay untraced; this charges the departure.
- rivals reach Vale: a rival within 1.5 m of the car while Vale is in it, with no op standing within 6 m of the car, fails the mission. The Enforcer down fails it only while Vale is not yet in the car; after that he is any op: carry him in, or he is left behind.
- so every route chooses at the car: board and go (down the blocker, board, Go: fewer seconds under fire, the fight moves Standing 0) or fight it out (break the crew, then go: +1 against feared rivals).
downed: op at 0 = downed; carry (3 s; the carrier walks and doesn't fire) to the car and Board, or he is left behind (captured).
civilians targetable: anyone can be targeted (takedown). Violence on a civilian seen by anyone → −1 Standing per incident, on top of the fight's own move (§04); a contact who witnesses it loses bravery and gains fear and resentment → some Informer, more Talkers. Fastest way to undo progress.
decisive shot [L]: 1–2× per fight: slow time, camera behind Enforcer, one aimed shot, back to tactical.
fight lab [lab]: a set of fight rules tried in the playground and on the fight range (§11), not the game: three fight modes on the same fight (real time · freeze: the plan view stops at a fight's beats and each agent queues one action, released together · target mode: time stops, pick shots with their pips and a charge cost); health ×10 shown as segmented bars, damage per hit by weapon, the chip as pips · damage · hits to down, never seconds; a hit chance of four pips (range band, −1 untrained, −1 cover inside its arc, −1 walking, +1 high ground); weapons chosen in the briefing within Arms (sidearm, machine pistol, riot gun, long rifle; the carbine the cell's); a recharging charge bar for heavy weapons and a short field; drawn means at will, with hold fire; cover marks and a snap to them; aim memory per shooter and target, and a first shot on a new target that ranges; a police warning before they fire (one per watcher, +1 T if heeded); in the final fight the police go for whoever holds Vale, then the car; +2 T per drive-back; cover inside the car ring. A rule leaves the lab only when Sky promotes it.

## §08 CAMPAIGN
acts: I one person (Fixer alone; recruit cell; first income; fights 3–6) · II networks (introductions; take a workplace e.g. docks, depot; brave contacts fight; 8–15) · III districts (streets rise or obey by standing; institutions + rival factions field squads; 20–30).
economy [S]: turned people become sources paying each mission: clerk skims cash, quartermaster diverts arms, café owner tips needs before Scout listens. Procurement missions target a source. Snowball.
wounds [S]: health carries over. Turned doctor heals fast and quietly. No doctor → optional pre-mission objective to watch/turn/pay one, if calendar allows. Main mission date never moves. Choice: go in hurt, short-handed, or spend the days.
objectives [S]: kidnapping · bank job via planted teller · killing by manipulated proxy · takeover via ruined reputation · rescue (a captured agent). Direct force always works, with bigger T.
captives [S]: an agent left behind and captured (§06) costs +4 T and is held at the district's police post, a place on the plan. A rescue objective (the Handover's shape, the captive as the target) is open there for 3 missions; after that the captive is moved out of the district, out of reach, not lost: a later act can come back to it. Wounds carry over. Questioning, once in each between-missions phase he is held, in order: fear +1, loyalty −1, then the Talker test (§04), each clamped to its scale. An agent abandoned while still standing also takes resentment +1 and loyalty −1 at capture. Talking is once and for good; a rescue doesn't take back what was said. With the founding cell's scales, an agent captured downed holds out 2 missions, one abandoned standing 1. A captive who talks gives every agent's face, the cell's car, the mission's place and time and every turned contact; each becomes a lead, and the investigators question those contacts next mission, link by link, each under their own Talker test.
hunted car [S]: a getaway seen by security puts the cell's car in the investigation as a hunted car next mission; the cell must answer it (a new car through a contact). The engine records the fact; the campaign side comes with the campaign layer.
heat [S]: the next mission's Heat is the campaign's Trail + Menace after the debrief; it sets the attention band (§05), so how a mission is won decides how fast the next mission's faces turn (from the Handover's reference results: Help noticed, B noticed, G-kill noticed, Pressure wanted, G wanted, loud infamous).
