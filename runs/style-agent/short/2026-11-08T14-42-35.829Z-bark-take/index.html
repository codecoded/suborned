What drags the family down is voice across the three temperaments. Phrases are shared between them: "Whatever you want" is in both the mercenary and the fearful Office lines.     Fix: Answer only what the Enforcer has done and can see: e.g. "Mind the step. You'll want to look your best."
  • VARIANTS done/take/enforcer “Walk with me. Easy now.”: "Easy" repeats the opening of "Easy, Popovic. Through the door." in the sister item. The two items play back to back in one scene, so he says the same word twice.
    Fix: Use a different take, e.g. "That's your car. This one's ours."
  • VOICE taken/take/councillor/loyal “I demand you release me.”: It's generic and stiff, and any frightened or angry captive could say it. It has none of the pompous official bluster. Today's "You will answer to the Council for this." was the stronger loyal line, and the draft dropped it for this.
    Fix: Bring back "You will answer to the Council for this." or write another bluster in his office's terms, e.g. "Do you know whose car this is?"
  • VOICE taken/take/councillor/mercenary “Please. I'm worth more to you unharmed.”: Opening on "Please." makes it sound fearful, and three fearful lines open the same way. A mercenary bargains as an equal; he doesn't plead.
    Fix: Drop the plea and make it a deal, e.g. "Whoever's paying you, I pay better."
  • VOICE taken/enter/councillor/mercenary “Whatever you want, it can be arranged.”: It shares "Whatever you want" with the fearful line "Whatever you want, please don't hurt me." Two temperaments shouldn't share a turn of phrase.
    Fix: Keep "Whatever you want" for one temperament only; for the mercenary, e.g. "Everything in this office has a price. Name it."
  • VOICE taken/enter/councillor/fearful “Whatever you want, please don't hurt me.”: It shares "Whatever you want" with the mercenary line. It also opens on yet another "please", so the fearful lines all sound alike.
    Fix: Show the fear differently, e.g. "I have a family. I've done nothing to you."
  • VARIANTS taken/enter/councillor/fearful “Please, take your hands off me.”: It is nearly the loyal line "Take your hands off a Councillor.", and it opens on "Please," like both fearful car lines, so the fearful rows feel like reshuffles of each other.
    Fix: Use a different fearful thought, e.g. "Where are you taking me? Tell me that much."
  • NATURAL taken/take/councillor/fearful “Please, let go. I won't resist.”: "I won't resist" is written, near-procedural phrasing; a frightened man doesn't talk like that. It also opens on "Please" like the sister variant.
    Fix: Make it sound spoken, e.g. "All right, all right. I'm coming. Don't hurt me."
