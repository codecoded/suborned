  • TRUE questioned/driver/gives “That's who wanted the Councillor driven round the back.”: This makes up what the Fixer asked for. The approach could have been a Pay, a Help or a Pressure, and none of them is said to be a request to drive round the back. The line isn't true every time the item plays.
    Fix: Name only the face and that they talked to him: "That one. Leaned on my window and talked money." Or keep it vaguer: "That's them. Wanted a word about the Councillor's car."
  • VOICE questioned/driver/gives “Them, officer. I hope you lock them up.”: Any witness could say this. Nothing in it is gruff, practical or about cars and wages.
    Fix: Give it his trade: "That's the one. Nearly cost me my job, that did."
  • VARIANTS summon-refused/waitress/summon-saw-violence “He saw what happened. He won't come now.”: "He won't come" repeats the phrasing of Sky's own came-once line ("He won't come again."), which already plays in the game. A shared phrase across rows reads as one line said twice.
    Fix: Say it in a different way: "He's seen enough for one day. He's staying put."
  • WORTH summon-refused/waitress/summon-busy “He's not free, dear.”: This only repeats what the HUD already shows. Its sister line "I can't get him for you just now." is just as flat, so neither line shows who Inez is.
    Fix: Give her café patter that holds for every busy state: "He's got his hands full, love. Ring me back." Or: "Not a hope just now, dear. He's up to his ears."
  • VOICE summon-refused/guard/summon-busy “It can't be helped.”: This is a stock phrase any speaker could say. There is nothing of the guard's post or his officialese in it.
    Fix: Use his clipped duty voice: "Not while I'm on, Inez. Rules are rules."
  • VOICE summon-refused/guard/summon-came-once “Don't push your luck.”: This is a generic brush-off with no door, no shift and no officialese, so anyone in the game could say it.
    Fix: Tie it to his post: "Once a shift is all the sergeant will wear, Inez." Or: "I've had my break, Inez. That's me done."
  • VARIANTS summon-refused/waitress/summon-off-street “I've looked for him. He's not out front.”: This is the same thought as "He isn't about. Not on the street.", just reworded.
    Fix: Give it a different detail or feeling: "His door's empty, love. Haven't seen him all this while."
  • TRUE summon-refused/guard/summon-off-street “They want me in here. Sorry.”: This invents a reason. We only know he isn't out front. No one is said to have ordered him to be anywhere.
    Fix: Keep to what he knows: "I'm round the back, Inez. Can't hear you properly." Or just: "I'm not on the door just now, Inez."
  • TRUE summon-refused/waitress/summon-no-visit “Who would I ring for you, love? There's nobody.”: In the Handover she isn't on this call and only follows the guard's line, so a line that answers the Fixer's call to her is false. "I'd help if I could, dear. Truly." is also vague filler.
    Fix: Write it as her reaction to the guard's line, which is true where she isn't on the call: "Me? I can't leave the tables, love." or "Fetch me? I've a café full, dear."
  • VARIANTS questioned/driver/nothing “I sit in the car and wait. That's the job.”: This is the same thought as "I watch the road.": he was busy with his job and saw nothing. It isn't a different take.
    Fix: Use a different detail: "Had the bonnet up half the morning. Didn't see a thing."
