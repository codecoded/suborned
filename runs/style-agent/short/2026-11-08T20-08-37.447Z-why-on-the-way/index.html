why/on-the-way: A report on its way: the reporter's plate, and the Alert meter

First draft · SCORE 5/10

REASON: The plates are mostly good. They fit their limits, keep the required fills and end in the countdown form. The lines are the weak spot. Five of them carry today's two-sentence form ("X is doing it. It lands in {s} s."), and the line form allows exactly one sentence. Keeping today's words only applies where they already pass every rule, and these don't. The rewritten check-in line drops the one useful fact: the desk learns of it. The screen line can be read as "the camera dies in 99 s". The look plate never says what the person is going to look at. "Too late to stop it" suggests the player could have acted sooner, but these reports could never be stopped. The meter rows, the stopped plate and line, and the tell and walk/run/radio/robot plates can ship as written. Every line opens with a fill that is lower case at its widest ("a kiosk attendant", "the guard"), so the host has to capitalise it.

  • SHORT onWay.ways.radio “{who} is radioing it in. It lands in {s} s.”: A line is one sentence. This one is two. Keeping today's words doesn't apply because they break the form.
    Fix: {who} is radioing it in, and it lands in {s} s.
  • SHORT onWay.ways.walk “{who} walks to {to} to report it. It lands in {s} s.”: Two sentences where the form allows one. At its widest it is also 76 characters, close to the 80 limit.
    Fix: {who} walks to {to} to report it, landing in {s} s.
  • SHORT onWay.ways.run “{who} runs to {to} to report it. It lands in {s} s.”: Two sentences where the form allows one.
    Fix: {who} runs to {to} to report it, landing in {s} s.
  • SHORT onWay.ways.camera “{who} has it on tape. The desk sees it in {s} s.”: Two sentences where the form allows one.
    Fix: {who} has it on tape, and the desk sees it in {s} s.
  • SHORT onWay.ways.robot “{who} is ringing the desk. It lands in {s} s.”: Two sentences where the form allows one.
    Fix: {who} is ringing the desk, and it lands in {s} s.
  • TRUE onWay.ways.checkin “{about} is down, with a check-in due in {s} s.”: The cause is that the desk learns when the check-in is missed. This rewrite only says a check-in is due, so the player isn't told what happens or who finds out. It also leaves today's line behind without fixing it in a way that keeps the cause.
    Fix: The desk misses {about}'s check-in in {s} s.
  • PLAIN onWay.ways.screen “The desk sees {about} is dead in {s} s.”: With the place in, this reads as if the camera goes dead in 99 s. In fact the camera is already cut and the desk sees the blank screen at its next scan.
    Fix: The desk will see the dead screen from {about} in {s} s.
  • USEFUL onWay.ways.look “Going to look”: It doesn't say what they are going to look at. The cause is a body, and that is the thing the player needs to know to act.
    Fix: Going to look at the body
  • USEFUL onWay.ways.look “{who} is going to look first, then report it.”: 'Look first' at what? The line leaves out the body, which is the whole cause.
    Fix: {who} is going to look at the body, then report it.
  • TRUE onWay.nofix “Too late to stop it”: Tapes, dead screens, robot rings and check-ins could never be stopped. 'Too late' suggests the player missed a chance to stop them earlier.
    Fix: No way to stop it
  • VOICE onWay.ways.camera “On tape · desk in {s} s”: The countdown plates follow a pattern of a phrase then ' · {s} s'. This one, and 'Dead screen · desk in {s} s', puts the seconds inside the phrase, so the countdown reads differently on these two.
    Fix: On the desk's tape · {s} s
