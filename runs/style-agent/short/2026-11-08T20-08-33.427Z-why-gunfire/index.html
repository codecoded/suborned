REASON: This is a solid family that is close to shipping. Every short fits its lozenge budget at the widest fills. Every line keeps its required fills and the HUD pin (`{agent} fired at {about}`). No engine words, AI words or filter words appear. The writer was right to rewrite two of today's lines. The old alert.warned line ("Holster, or it's called in.") and the old police.gang line ("Police come, but don't seal the district yet.") stated game rules and ran to two sentences, and the new lines drop that. Four problems keep the score down. 1. Two police lines are false when the fill is a camera. A camera cannot call the police; a person watching it does. 2. The same act is worded two ways. The shorts say "fired on" but the matching lines say "shot at". The warned title and short say "drop it" but the new line says "put the gun away". 3. alert.drawn and alert.drawn-people share the title "Weapon seen", so two causes the player must tell apart (a watcher's Alert 2 and passers-by's Alert 1) read the same on the Journal row. "Weapon" also breaks with "gun", the word used everywhere else. 4. Some shorts and lines are weaker than they could be: "the fight with the rivals" is not something that happened, "drew" is unclear, and the base gunfire short "shots fired" doesn't say the cell fired when "{agent} fired" fits.

  • TRUE police.witness “{who} saw the shooting and called the police.”: {who} can be a camera by its place (widest: the entrance camera). "the entrance camera saw the shooting and called the police" is false, because a camera does not call anyone. In this world cameras are closed-circuit and watched by people.
    Fix: "{who} saw the shooting, and the police were called." At the widest fill that is 64 characters, so it reads true for a person or a camera.
  • TRUE police.drawn “{who} saw {agent} with a gun out and called the police.”: {who} can be a camera (the entrance camera, a camera by its place). A camera cannot call the police, so the line is false for that fill.
    Fix: "{who} saw {agent} with a gun out, and the police were called." At the widest fills that is exactly 80 characters and 12 words.
  • VOICE police.shot-at “{about} was shot at, and people called the police.”: The short above it says "someone was fired on", and alert.violence.variants.shot says "was fired on". One glossary means one phrase for one act.
    Fix: "{about} was fired on, and people called the police."
  • VOICE police.patrol “The patrol was shot at {where} and called it in.”: Its own short reads "the patrol was fired on", so the hover line uses a different phrase for the same act.
    Fix: "The patrol was fired on {where} and called it in."
  • VOICE alert.warned “{who} told {agent} to put the gun away.”: The title says "Told to drop it" and the short says "{who} said to drop it", but the line switches to "put the gun away". The player sees the same warning worded two ways.
    Fix: Use one phrase across all three fields. Either use "{who} told {agent} to drop it." for the line, or reword the title and short to "put it away" if they fit the lozenge.
  • USEFUL alert.drawn “Weapon seen”: alert.drawn-people has the same title. These are causes the player must tell apart (a guard, patrol or camera at Alert 2 against passers-by at Alert 1), but on the Journal row they look identical. "Weapon" also breaks with "gun", the word every other line uses.
    Fix: Use "Gun seen" here and give drawn-people its own title, such as "People saw a gun".
  • VOICE alert.drawn-people “Weapon seen”: This title is the same as alert.drawn's and uses "weapon" where the rest of the family says "gun".
    Fix: "People saw a gun" (4 words, 16 characters).
  • PLAIN police.rivals “the fight with the rivals”: This is a noun phrase, not something that happened. Read at a glance as "the fight with the rivals by the gate · 4:44", it doesn't say what went on. The line also calls them "the rival crew", so the same group gets two names.
    Fix: Use "gunfight with the rivals" (24 characters) for the short, and "the rivals" or "the rival crew" in both fields.
  • PLAIN alert.fought “{about} saw the Enforcer coming, drew and called it in.”: "drew" on its own is unclear and reads like shorthand.
    Fix: "{about} saw the Enforcer coming, drew a gun and called it in." At the widest fill (an officer) that is 63 characters.
  • USEFUL alert.gunfire “shots fired”: The base cause is that the cell fired, and the short could say which agent. "the Enforcer fired" is 18 characters, within the about-24 budget. Naming the agent tells the player whose shot raised the Alert, and it reads clearly apart from the other variant ("the guard opened fire").
    Fix: Use "{agent} fired" for the short. You could also align the other variant on "fired" ("{who} fired") so both use the same verb.
