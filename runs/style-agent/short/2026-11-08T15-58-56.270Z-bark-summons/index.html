The questioned rows lean on 'remember' and 'know that face', the same family as the 'I remember them, officer' line Sky turned down. Several summon lines tell the player what to do next ('Try me again.', 'Ask later.') or read the refusal reason back like a label ('He's too far to come quickly.', 'I saw the violence'). One of the new lines, the guard's summon-no-visit, makes up a motive. The facts say he has nobody to send, not that he refuses to involve Inez. Voices blur: 'I notice nothing' is in both Inez's and the driver's mouths, 'I'm afraid' is a tic in nearly every Inez line, and she and the guard share 'leave the door' and 'alarm up'. Many pairs are synonyms rather than different takes. The few strong lines are 'I park where I'm told.', 'I watch the road.', 'After what I saw? No, Inez.', 'I've been off this door enough today, Inez.' and 'That's the one, officer. I'm not taking the blame for anybody.' They show the bar the rest should meet. Lengths and names are fine.

  • VOICE questioned/waitress/lies “Strangers? I pour coffee. I notice nothing.”: The driver says 'I notice nothing.' word for word, so the phrase is shared between speakers. 'I saw nobody, officer. A quiet day.' could come from anyone and has none of Inez's quick patter.

  • NATURAL questioned/waitress/nothing “I don't remember anything, officer.”: It uses the game's word 'remember' and reads like a stock answer. 'I've nothing useful to tell you' grades her own testimony the way the game would. Neither line has Inez in it.

    Fix: Use a lie that is true in every run: 'Same run as every day. The Councillor's car, nothing else.'
  • VOICE questioned/driver/nothing “I only drive. I notice nothing.”: It shares 'I notice nothing' with Inez. It's also a flat stock denial, close to the kept 'I watch the road' line.
    Fix: Use his trade: 'I was under the bonnet half the shift, officer.'
  • NATURAL summon-refused/waitress/summon-busy “He's tied up just now. Try me again.”: 'Try me again' tells the player what to do next, the way 'Let me listen first' does in the calibration.
    Fix: Have her describe what she can see: 'He's got somebody at the door with him, love.'
  • VOICE summon-refused/waitress/summon-busy “He can't leave the door, I'm afraid.”: It echoes the guard's 'I can't leave the door right now.' 'I'm afraid' appears in five Inez lines and has turned into a tic.
    Fix: Use another take that is still true: 'He's busy signing somebody in. Not a chance.'
  • NATURAL summon-refused/guard/summon-came-once “Not again this shift, Inez.”: It recites the once-a-shift rule. It is also close to Sky's own 'He's been once this shift', which no other row may repeat.
    Fix: Use a reason of his own: 'They already asked where I'd got to, Inez.'
  • WORTH summon-refused/waitress/summon-alert “There's an alarm, I'm afraid. He won't leave his post.”: It reads the HUD back: alarm up, so he stays put. It also repeats variant two's thought and shares 'alarm up' with the guard.
    Fix: Show the alarm through her eyes: 'Bells going over there. He's not budging, love.'
  • VOICE summon-refused/guard/summon-alert “The alarm's up. I'm staying at my post.”: It labels the event and could be anyone's line. 'Not with an alarm up, Inez.' repeats Inez's phrase.
    Fix: Use clipped officialese: 'Bells are ringing, Inez. Nobody leaves the door.'
  • NATURAL summon-refused/guard/summon-saw-violence “I saw the violence, Inez. I'm not coming.”: Nobody says 'the violence'. It is the game's category word. The waitress's 'He saw that violence' has the same fault and is a synonym of her first line.
    Fix: Keep 'After what I saw? No, Inez.' and add a concrete one: 'Somebody got hurt out there. I'm staying put.' For Inez: 'He saw that fight, love. He's shaking.'
  • WORTH summon-refused/waitress/summon-too-far “He's too far to come quickly.”: It just labels the refusal reason. The guard's 'Too far from my post. I can't.' is telegraphese that labels it the same way.
    Fix: Keep 'Bless him, he'd be ages getting here.' Write a second take, e.g. 'He's off down the far end. He'd never make it back in time.' For the guard: 'I'm clear across the square, Inez.'
  • NATURAL summon-refused/guard/summon-off-street “I'm not out front. Ask later.”: 'Ask later' tells the player what to do. 'Not out there at the moment.' is clipped into a fragment. A guard Inez can't reach saying 'I'm not out front' also strains belief.
    Fix: Give a real answer in his voice: 'They've got me round the back, Inez.' / 'Inside on the desk. Can't step out.'
  • TRUE summon-refused/guard/summon-no-visit “Leave Inez out of this. That's final.”: This invents a motive. The fact is that his tie, Inez, has no post to leave, so the call moves nobody. He isn't refusing to involve her. The second variant, 'I never said Inez would', also makes up a refusal.
    Fix: Say the plain truth in his words, to the Fixer: 'Inez? She's behind her counter already. Who'd I fetch?' / 'There's only Inez, and she never leaves that café.'
  • VARIANTS summon-refused/waitress/summon-no-visit “I'm afraid there's nobody I can call over.”: It's a synonym of 'There's nobody I can send for.' and uses the 'I'm afraid' tic again.
    Fix: Use a different thought: 'Who'd I ring, love? Everyone I know is on shift.'
  • MECHANICS summon-refused/waitress/summon-no-visit “There's nobody I can send for.”: 'Send for' and 'call over' echo the game's own Call rule wording ('a contact send for their tie').
    Fix: Put it in everyday terms: 'There's not a soul I could fetch for you.'
