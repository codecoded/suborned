REASON: The passer-by rows mostly work. The flee rewrites ("That could have been me.", "I want to go home.") are human and show the fear without explaining anything. "I didn't see a thing." and "I'm getting the police." already meet the bar. Both guard rows let the family down. report-begins/guard/radio keeps four lines that all say "desk, incident to report", which is the reshuffled-synonym failure the rubric names, and it uses the form's word "incident". witness-reacts/guard/radio makes up the guard's years on the door, mutters "by the book" to himself, and keeps "Calling the desk about this.", which only labels the act. That line is also the same moment as the report-begins row. Within avoid and quiet, the variants sit close together: "Don't look. Keep walking." / "Eyes down. Just walk past." and "I'm keeping out of this." / "Not my trouble." are two pairs of near-synonyms. VARIANTS is the lowest score. No cut is warranted: each witness reaction is the only word the player gets about who saw the moment and how they took it.

  • VARIANTS report-begins/guard/radio “Desk, come in. Incident to report.”: All four lines say one thing: desk, incident to report. They are reshuffles of a single fragment, and the first and fourth lines even open the same way.
    Fix: Give each line a different detail he can see from the post, in clipped radio talk. For example: "Desk, front door. We've trouble out here." / "Desk, it's the door. Somebody's down out front." / "Front door to desk. Send someone out, quick." / "Desk? Door here. You'd better come and look."
  • NATURAL report-begins/guard/radio “This is the post. Reporting an incident.”: "Reporting an incident" sounds like a form or a report, not a guard on the radio. "Incident" is also the game's own word for a found fault.
    Fix: Have him name what he sees in plain words: "Desk, door here. There's a man down out front."
  • MECHANICS report-begins/guard/radio “Post to desk. I've an incident to report.”: "Incident" and "report" are the game's rule words for this event, and this line says both.
    Fix: "Post to desk. Something's wrong out front."
  • TRUE witness-reacts/guard/radio “Never, in all my years on the door.”: It makes up a service history the item doesn't give. It's also sentimental rather than clipped, so it doesn't sound like the guard.
    Fix: Use a clipped reaction to what he has just seen: "Hey! You there, stop!" won't do, because he isn't fighting. Try "Right. That's not on my post."
  • VOICE witness-reacts/guard/radio “Easy, now. Do it properly, by the book.”: He coaches himself out loud, and nobody says that while reaching for a radio. "By the book" is a cliché, not officialese.
    Fix: "Not on my door. Not on my watch." or "Stay where you are. Nobody moves."
  • WORTH witness-reacts/guard/radio “Calling the desk about this.”: It labels the act the HUD already shows. It also repeats the report-begins/guard/radio moment, which plays next.
    Fix: Use his reaction instead of a description of the call: "What the devil? Right."
  • VARIANTS witness-reacts/crowd/avoid “Eyes down. Just walk past.”: It is a synonym of "Don't look. Keep walking." and reads as clipped self-instruction.
    Fix: Give it a different thought: "None of my business. None at all." would clash with quiet, so try "I'm late as it is."
  • VARIANTS witness-reacts/crowd/quiet “Not my trouble. Not today.”: It is a synonym of "I'm keeping out of this." in the same row, and it overlaps the avoid row too. "Not today" also hints at other days.
    Fix: Give it another take, such as "Somebody else will see to it."
  • VOICE witness-reacts/crowd/report “Here? In this city? I won't have it.”: It sounds a little stagey and civic-minded for a startled passer-by. It's close but not short and human enough.
    Fix: "Not here. Not on my street."
