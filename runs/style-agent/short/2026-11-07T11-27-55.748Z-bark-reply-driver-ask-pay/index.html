First draft · SCORE 6/10

REASON: The outcomes are mostly clear, all variants meet the length limits, names are correct, and no forbidden words appear. But repeated third-person references to being a driver sound written rather than spoken. Several refusals omit the increased cost of returning, one report remains merely habitual, and some retained lines lack the driver's voice. No cuts are warranted.

  • USEFUL reply/ask/driver/refused/idealist “No. I earn honest wages. I'll remember you asked.”: Remembering the request does not communicate its useful consequence: a later offer costs more.
    Fix: Make the consequence explicit: "No. I earn honest wages. Coming back will cost you more."
  • USEFUL reply/ask/driver/refused/idealist “No. Ask another driver. I won't forget this.”: The refusal is clear, but the increased cost of returning is missing.
    Fix: "No. Ask another driver. I'll want more if you come back."
  • VOICE reply/ask/driver/turned/idealist “A driver can help too.”: This sounds like commentary about the driver's role; "too" also suggests an unnecessary comparison.
    Fix: "Yes. I'll do my part. Call when you need me."
  • VOICE reply/ask/driver/turned/idealist “You've got this driver's help when you call.”: Calling himself "this driver" makes an otherwise natural acceptance sound manufactured.
    Fix: "All right. I'll help. Give me a call."
  • VOICE reply/ask/driver/suspicious/idealist “I'm telling the Office what you've asked this driver to do.”: The third-person self-reference is unnatural and adds words without adding information.
    Fix: "I'm telling the Office what you've asked me to do."
  • VOICE reply/ask/driver/suspicious/idealist “A driver's wages don't cover that.”: The occupational generalization sounds composed for the voice sheet rather than spoken by this person.
    Fix: "I'm not paid for that. I'm reporting your offer."
  • VOICE reply/ask/driver/refused/mercenary “You've just made this driver's time more expensive.”: The consequence fits the temperament, but "this driver's time" is an awkward third-person self-reference.
    Fix: "No. My time'll cost you more next time."
  • USEFUL reply/ask/driver/refused/fearful “No. Risk my driving job? I'll remember you asking.”: It communicates refusal and fear, but leaves the increased cost unstated.
    Fix: "No. Risk my job? I'll need more if you ask again."
  • USEFUL reply/ask/driver/refused/fearful “No. I won't forget this next time you need a driver.”: Remembering is vague; the player needs to understand that returning costs more.
    Fix: "No. Next time I'll need more. I've my job to consider."
  • VOICE reply/ask/driver/turned/fearful “Just keep this between you and the driver.”: Calling himself "the driver" makes the request for secrecy sound artificial.
    Fix: "Yes, I'll help. Just keep this between us."
  • VOICE reply/ask/driver/turned/fearful “Call this driver when you need me.”: Switching from "this driver" to "me" is particularly awkward spoken self-reference.
    Fix: "All right. Call when you need me. Quietly."
  • VOICE reply/ask/driver/suspicious/loyal “The Councillor will hear what you've asked his driver to do.”: The correct title helps, but referring to himself as "his driver" sounds staged.
    Fix: "The Councillor will hear what you've asked me to do."
  • USEFUL reply/pay/driver/refused/idealist “I can't be bought. Not even by you.”: It refuses the bribe but omits remembering the offer and the increased cost of returning.
    Fix: "Keep your money. Come back and I'll want more from you."
  • VOICE reply/pay/driver/refused/idealist “I can't be bought. Not even by you.”: This is a generic defiant line with no gruff, practical turn particular to the driver.
    Fix: Ground the refusal in honest wages while making the later consequence clear.
  • USEFUL reply/pay/driver/refused/idealist “Keep your money. This driver won't forget you offered.”: Remembering the offer leaves its increased future cost unclear.
    Fix: "Keep your money. Next time I'll want more for my trouble."
  • VOICE reply/pay/driver/refused/idealist “This driver won't forget you offered.”: The third-person self-reference sounds like a character label inserted into dialogue.
    Fix: Use "I" and express the consequence directly.
  • VOICE reply/pay/driver/suspicious/idealist “I'm reporting your money to the Office.”: People report a bribe or an offer; "reporting your money" is strained phrasing.
    Fix: "I earn my wages. I'm telling the Office about your bribe."
  • VOICE reply/pay/driver/turned/mercenary “Cash? Say no more.”: This stock mercenary response could be spoken unchanged by any paid recruit.
    Fix: "That covers my time. You've got a driver."
  • USEFUL reply/pay/driver/suspicious/loyal “I report bribes. Even polite ones.”: It states a general habit rather than clearly saying this offer is being reported.
    Fix: "I'm telling the Councillor about your bribe."
  • VOICE reply/pay/driver/suspicious/loyal “I report bribes. Even polite ones.”: The polished punchline is generic and arch rather than gruff and practical.
    Fix: Use a direct statement that the Councillor will hear about this offer.
  • VOICE reply/pay/driver/suspicious/loyal “The Councillor's hearing about this money you offered his driver.”: The third-person reference to himself makes a simple report sound theatrical.
    Fix: "The Councillor's hearing about the money you offered me."
