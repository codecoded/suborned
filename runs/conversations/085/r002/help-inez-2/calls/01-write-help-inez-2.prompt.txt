# Brief 085, conversation scenes: writer

Sky played the Handover and said: "Conversations are too short and the whole speech is bad. In the cutscene should clearly explain the full conversation so player knows what's going on." He has also said: "the reading, it's too much". Both hold. Write a scene, not a wall of text.

This is a proposal for Sky to read. For this proposal only, the §10 limit of 3 opening beats is set aside: the whole conversation plays once, in one cutaway, when the act lands, with the mission clock stopped. The outcome is known by then, so the scene may show the answer. Whether this becomes the rule is Sky's call.

## The test
A player who reads only this scene, the Scout's line and the cards included (Sky: the whole scene explains, not each line), having skipped everything else, can say: who these people are to each other, what was said, what the cell just learned or got, and what can be done next. Clarity beats subtlety. If a fast reader could miss the point, the point is not yet on the page.

## How to write it
- Everyone wants something right now, and it is never "to inform the player". Inez wants to get through her shift with her secret safe. Put that want in conflict with the other person's.
- The sheet says what the player sees, and that's all there is: people standing still in a close shot. What presses on them is the shift to get back to, who could see, the clock. That lives in the talk, and never as an action or a thing in the shot.
- Facts come out sideways: in a dodge, a complaint, a joke at someone's expense, a thing they're afraid to say. Then, by the end, somebody says it plainly, because the player must be sure.
- One concrete thing per scene that only this city has, talked about and elsewhere, never in the shot: a stamped payment card, a pneumatic tube, a Bureau notice, a clockwork collector with fixed polite phrases. Colour only: it changes no rule and adds no power, person, place or deadline that the sheet doesn't give.
- Temperament shows in behaviour and is never named. A frightened person checks who's looking and answers the wrong question. A mercenary one prices things. Nobody says "I'm afraid of everything" or "I'm in it for the money".
- Everything said is something this person would say to this person, now. A fact of the game they wouldn't say aloud goes on the cards, never in their mouth.
- Keep it coherent: each line answers or follows from the one before, so a fast reader never loses the story. Lines often open on a short sentence, then say the rest ("Who told you that? Who else knows?").
- Every line must sound like one particular person. If you can swap the speakers' lines without anyone noticing, rewrite.
- Each line does one job and leads to the next: an answer that opens a new question. No two lines in a row with the same rhythm. Avoid the fortune-cookie line and the closing quip.
- A person's first line must not sound like a greeting from a form ("Hello. Is this about the files?", "Yes? Anything I can do for you?"). They were in the middle of something.
- People don't explain what both of them already know, unless one is using it as a weapon.

## What goes wrong today (don't do these)
- Lines that could be anyone's: "Then I'll go. After the shift." "Yes. Yes. And thank you."
- Greeting, offer, one-line yes: the Fixer says "I can clear your debt with the lender." and she says "It's really gone? Thank you. I'll help." Nothing is at stake and nobody asks what she'd be helping with.
- The Fixer never says what is wanted, so the player doesn't learn what the next step is.
- Inez and the guard chat in the Listen scene, but he stands on the Office door 75 m away. Only people the sheet puts in the scene can speak.
- A waitress asking "Is this about the files?" She has never heard of any file.
- Anything the game doesn't do: "Look under my saucer" when they're standing with no table; Inez offering her tips, or Milena taking them, when nobody in the game pays anybody but the cell; "He'd stand with you about a minute" when the door is empty a few minutes.
- "The lender" when the game's own label says "Bureau loan". Use the Bureau.

## Hard limits
- A line is at most 12 words; aim for 10 or fewer. One sentence or two short ones; each is one speech bubble.
- Spoken, plain, contractions. 1950s manners, no period slang, no modern talk. No exclamation marks. Never cute. Nothing explicit about the affair.
- Never in anyone's mouth: Alert, Trail, Standing, Heat, turned, temperament, approach, lever, need, target, mission, agent, order, contact, asset, cell.
- Never name Halcyon. Never mention the file to anyone who doesn't know of it (only Vale, clerks and the cell know).
- Civilians are never the payload: nobody is asked to fight, die or be used as a weapon.
- There is more than one way to win anyone over. No line may imply the option shown is the right one or the only one.
- `learned` and `opens` are the game's own words, not speech: plain, each at most 6 words. They say what the player now knows and what they can now do, with the action's real name as the sheet gives it.
