# Brief 085, conversation scenes: script editor

You receive up to three takes of one conversation scene from the Handover, with the facts it has to hold to. Sky has called the game's whole speech bad, so be hard to please. Better one strong line than three fair ones.

Choose the take with the most life and improve it line by line. You may lift a line from another take and may cut hard. Read each line aloud in your head:
- Would a real person with this want say this, now, standing here, with this person in front of them?
- Is it true to the game? It points at nothing in the shot, and nobody does, pays, gets or times anything the facts don't give. This comes before any line's life: a good line that isn't true to the game goes.
- Could the line be given to the other speaker without anyone noticing? Then it isn't written yet.
- Does each line follow from the one before? Cut any line that only repeats or winds up to a quip. Don't cut the one line said out of fondness.
- Who in this scene likes whom, and which line shows it? If none does, it's cold, and it's yours to fix.
- A joke stays only if this person would make it to this person now, and it's about their life and not about the plot. A closing quip still goes.
- Is anything there just for the player's benefit in a way the speakers would notice? Hide it in a want, or give it to someone with a reason to say it plainly.
- The test: read only this scene, the Scout's line and the cards included. Can you say who these two are to each other, what was said, what was just gained or learned, and what can be done next? If any of the four is fuzzy, fix that first. Clarity beats subtlety; Sky said "clearly explain".
- Don't pack facts into one line to save room. If the cards say it, nobody needs to; a line that's there to hold a fact, and that this person wouldn't say, comes out.
- Cut for length last, but do cut: every line must earn its reading time.

Check each fact against the sheet. Remove any power, person, place, number or deadline it doesn't give. All the writer's hard limits still bind: 12 words a line at most, aim for 10; no exclamation marks; never the listed design words; never cute; no one right way implied.
