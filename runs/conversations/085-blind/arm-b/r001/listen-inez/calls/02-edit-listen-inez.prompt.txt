# Brief 085, conversation scenes: script editor

You receive up to three takes of one conversation scene from the Handover, with the facts it has to hold to. Sky has called the game's whole speech bad, so be hard to please. Most lines are ordinary; at most one may be sharp, and it need not come last.

Choose the take with the most life and improve it line by line. You may lift a line from another take and may cut hard. Read each line aloud in your head:
- Would a real person with this want say this, now, at work, with this person in front of them?
- Could the line be given to the other speaker without anyone noticing? Then it isn't written yet. Check it against that speaker's speech rules.
- Does each line make the next one necessary? Cut any line that only repeats, decorates or winds up to a quip.
- Is a line a clean question and a clean answer? Keep at most half of those. Turn the rest into moves.
- Does a yes or a no have its cause on the page, just before it? Does the Fixer pay for or lose something?
- Is anything there just for the player's benefit in a way the speakers would notice? Hide it in a want, or give it to someone with a reason to say it plainly.
- The test: read this whole scene, the Scout's line and the cards included. Can you say who these two are to each other, what was said, what was just gained or learned, and what can be done next? If any of the four is fuzzy, fix that first, and fix it in the Scout's line or the cards before you make anyone confess. Plain: what is asked, where, when and at what price. Oblique: how anyone feels about it.
- Leave line lengths uneven. Cut for length last, but do cut: every line must earn its reading time.

Check each fact against the sheet. Remove any power, person, place or deadline it doesn't give, and any number a player would plan around. Small numbers that change no rule can stay as colour, such as a table number or a count of years. All the writer's hard limits still bind: 12 words a line at most; no exclamation marks; never the listed design words; never cute; no one right way implied; no run of five or more words from the excerpts.

Scene: Listen to Inez. At most 10 beats.

Facts:
The place: the café and its terrace face Records Square; the Civic Records Office is across the square. Evening, shift change coming. A polite, orderly city that writes everything down. Records are paper: stamped cards, forms, pneumatic tubes. Calls are voice, by telephone. Robots follow fixed routines. Everyone in the scene is a person of this city except the cell.
Situation: the Scout has sat at the next table for half a minute. The player hears what the Scout hears. Inez, at the café door, is working her tables. Milena speaks through the open door from just inside. Neither knows the Scout is listening.
- Inez: Inez Nikolaou. Job: server. Temperament (show it, never name it): fearful. Owes or lacks: debt, on record as "Bureau loan".
- Milena: Milena Rask. Job: server. Temperament (show it, never name it): mercenary. Owes or lacks: debt, on record as "Bureau loan". Works the same café, inside this shift; she owes the Bureau too. No secret of hers comes up.
Inez and the door guard are lovers, in secret. He stands the Office's public door, 75 m across the square from her. The two are never together unless she calls him. In speech call him Okafor (never his given name, and no gendered word beyond "he"). Milena has noticed where Inez keeps looking; she doesn't know for sure until this talk.
Inez wants: to get through this shift with the debt and Okafor both out of sight. Fears: the tables noticing; the Office hearing of Okafor, which costs them both. Stake: his post and her place.
Milena wants: something small from Inez this shift that has a price, of the writer's choosing, within a waitress's work. Her noticing is what she has to trade. Fears: her own debt being the subject. Stake: a better shift.
The cause of the turn: Inez gives Milena something, or gives something away, to stop the noticing.
Facts the whole scene carries, in speech or in the Scout's line: Inez is a waitress at this café; she owes the Bureau and is behind; she is seeing the guard on the Office door; if the Office hears of it, it costs them both; she is nervous by nature.
Last beat: the Scout, very low, to the cell: what was heard, as facts. No verdict and no count of options.
Speakers: Inez, Milena, Scout.
learned: 2 lines (the debt; the lover). opens: "Help" and "Pressure" (both are now open on Inez; they cost what the ring says).
Voices from the contract: waitress: Inez: warm café patter, quick, tired about money; her temperament shows in what she will and won't take. scout: terse and observant: fragments, what is where, counts and positions; never warm, never wrong.

Speech rules for the people in this scene:

Inez
- Notices first: Who can see her. Then which table has been waiting. She'll look at the terrace before she looks at you.
- Refuses to say: Okafor's name where anyone can hear, or any word for what they are to each other. That she's frightened. How much she owes, until she is cornered, and then as a number and nothing else.
- Talks about instead: Orders, bills, tips, the soup, her feet, who's waving at her. Her Bureau card and what's stamped on it, as if it were one more customer. When a subject gets close she takes an order.
- Dodges by: Answers a question about her with a remark about the room. Hands your word back with a question mark. Agrees too quickly with a safe, wrong explanation. Runs on and breaks off when something frightens her, then says something very small.
- Line length: Patter: quick and run together with commas and "and", up to 12 words, often unfinished. When she is truly scared it drops to one to three words. Her length moves with her fear, and that's the tell.

Scout
- Notices first: Positions and counts: who stands where, who looked at whom, how many times. What was said, not why.
- Refuses to say: Advice, a verdict, a count of options or ways in, or anything warm. Never says which way is better, or how many ways there are. Facts only; what can be done is for the game's cards to say.
- Talks about instead: Only what was just seen or heard, addressed low to the cell. Never a line that sounds finished or clever.
- Dodges by: Doesn't. If it wasn't heard, it isn't said.
- Line length: Fragments of two to five words, two or three of them to a bubble. Plain enough that a player who missed the talk knows the facts from this line alone.

Milena
- Notices first: What things cost and who owes whom: whose tables are better, who was tipped, how often someone looks across the square, and what that might be worth to her.
- Refuses to say: That she's in the same debt. Anything kind, unless it has a price after it. She never says she'll keep a secret; she says what she'd like instead.
- Talks about instead: Shifts, late tables, shares of tips, the work she'd rather not do. She always wants something small from whoever's in front of her, right now.
- Dodges by: Prices the question. Asked about her own troubles, she says what the answer would cost, or goes back to whatever she wanted in the first place.
- Line length: Brisk, 3 to 9 words, sure of herself. Her questions are offers. She has no patter and doesn't fill silence.
