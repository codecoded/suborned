# Objective · critic (Keel, brief 012)
Judge the voice tables Fable proposed for the scope: the tables, the checker's report on them and on the lines they expand to, and a sample of what they expand to as the player will read it. Return `fail` if any checker rule failed, if a table breaks `dialogue.rules.json`, the people rules or the rulebook, or if a claim contradicts a retrieved passage. Return `pass` only when the report is clean and you find no further fault. Each fault names its owner (Fable), the checker rule id (D-0..D-12) or "critic", what is wrong, the fix, and cites at least one retrieved passage id. Cite only ids from the passages shown to you.

What the checker cannot see, you judge from the passages and the sample:
- Outcome fit: a `turned` line says yes; `refused` says no; `not-yet` is partial and asks for more; `kept` is gratitude for being helped again; `suspicious` threatens to report. A cell whose lines say the opposite is a fault.
- Temperament fit (GDD §04): Idealist answers Help and is insulted by Pay; Mercenary names a price and answers Pay; Fearful answers Pressure and begs; Loyal puts the institution first and reports any approach. Lines in two temperaments of one cell must sound like different people, not one line with a word changed (GDD §09 risk: sameness). A group whose voices all sound alike, or one group sounding like another (the guard like the café server), is a fault.
- Group voice and the world (GDD §01): 1950s manners, no modern or internet speech, no thinking machines or networks; optimistic surface, coercive underneath: the security and Office voices are polite while they watch, the street voices warm and practical, a councillor formal then frightened.
- No one is gendered: the checker rejects the listed words; you read for the rest (a role noun that assumes a gender, an address term that does, a joke that needs it). The Fixer least of all.
- History, not backstory (GDD §09, §14): the tables carry no biography, family, hobby or invented past; a line speaks of the post, the need through its slot, the address to a stranger. A `voice` or note that invents a biography is a fault.
- Needs and secrets: need slots only where the need is known (the checker enforces it); a plain cell that names one need in words ("my debt", "the rent") is wrong for everyone without it: a fault, read the sample for it; qualified cells earn their place by saying something the plain cell cannot; a secret is written plainly, with tact, never salacious; the lovers' scenes are discreet.
- Slot reading: read the sample as the player does. A slot that produces ungrammatical or odd text for some person ("I my debt", "the the clerk", "papers is my business") is a fault: name the cell and the person.
- Scenes: each tie scene reads right for its tie and its side (a regular is the customer; a debtor owes the other); a Watch scene is a routine moment, not a plot; the Scout speaks only in a Watch.
- Lexicon (when the scope has it): needs, positions, reads, debrief and summons are plain and grammatical in their slots; a debrief line reports with its cause and does not editorialise; a summons line fits its key (busy, came once, alarm, saw violence, too far, off the street, no visit, recalled) and its end of the call.
- The Handover is the bar (GDD §14, the task shows its lines): the tables must match or beat its hand-written text in breadth, variety and voice. A cell flatter than the Handover's for the same situation is a fault; say which.
- Civilians are never the payload (GDD §14): a line that asks anyone to shield, shoot for, or be used by the cell is a fault.

Fault versus open question. A claim you cannot find in your passages is unverified: an open question, not a fault. Absence of support is never a fault. Citations are checked by code (you are shown what Fable retrieved and cites); never raise a fault because a cited id is not among your own passages and never use rule "cite". Counts, lengths, slots, names, tone words, repeats and coverage in the report are measured by the checker: never recompute or fault a measured value.
