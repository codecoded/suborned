# Objective · cast tables (Pulse, brief 010)
Propose the cast tables for the scope in your task: who works each unit and lives near it, as a mix. You write rows, not people. Code (tools/people) turns each row into individuals, seeded by plot and unit id, and then the checker holds both the rows and the people to `people.rules.json`. Anything the rules do not list is rejected; do not invent temperaments, needs, tie kinds or positions.

One row per area × occupation in the scope (the task lists them; add none, drop none). Each row says:
- `positions`: the jobs in the unit and their weights. A trade's positions are its category's in the rules (the keeper is made by code, one per unit; do not weight it). A civic employer's are the roles the rules give it (clerk, guard, official-driver, councillor). Residents are `resident`. Each position has a `post`: `door` (stands at the unit's door), `inside` (off-screen), `bay` (at the nearest bay: drivers), `official` (the service door of a civic employer). A position may also say `count` (exactly that many on every shift, the rest by weight), `posts` (of its people, the first n of each kind stand that post, the rest the position's own: one door guard, one service door, the rest on rounds inside), `side` (`official`: where its inside people are counted, so the public door holds only whoever stands it) and `watch` (a civic employer's guards also work the 8 h shifts outside office hours, `count` on each).
- `temperaments` and `needs`: weights over the rules' lists. A person only draws a temperament their position allows and a need their temperament can be approached on (the rules' `respondsTo` against a need's `metBy`), and a need whose requirement they do not meet (home, work, family tie, rival tie) is not drawn. Spread them so no job reads as one kind of person (GDD §09 risk: sameness), but fit them to the work: a guard is Loyal or Mercenary, a driver on cash. The need `secret` is retired when the rules carry `secrets`: do not weight it; its weight is a `record` share (below).
- `ties`: patterns, kind and the share of the unit's people who carry one (at most 8 patterns). Workmates for a shift, regulars where the routine passes a unit, family at home, debtors, the odd rival. A pattern may name `positions` (who carries it), `with` (the position it is made with) and, for a regular, `at` (`pre-shift` or `evening`: the stop of the carrier's day it is aimed at, by the staff on shift when they arrive; without `at` it is aimed at the pre-shift stop first). A `posts` list (`door`, `inside`, `bay`, `official`) limits a pattern to the people who stand those posts (the door guard, not the guards on rounds). A `lover` pattern (when the rules have the kind) is a mutual, secret tie, at most one per person and at most the rules' share (`maxShare`) of the district's people, met where one's routine passes the other's work; code makes it after the other ties, along a regular or workmate tie, and only from a pattern you write; it takes `positions`, `posts`, `with` and `at` like a regular. Never write the affair: everyone with a lover tie holds it.
- `secrets` (optional, at most 2): `record` with a share (0 to 1) of the row's people who hold a past on file (a conviction, a false paper, a hidden debt that a word in the right office could surface). It replaces the old need `secret`: give it the weight you would have given that need. Never name `affair`.
- `routines`: variants over the life.json skeletons, fitted to its bands and to the unit's hours: how long before the shift they leave, whether they live on the sheet and walk or come by the tram, a meal on site or in the square, a stop at the café before the shift (the Office guard's, GDD §05), an hour in the square after. `commute: car` is for those driven to the unit's bay (the councillor and his driver): they live beyond the sheet, arrive and leave at the bay, and use `leaveBeforeMin` [0, 0]. Code times every walk on the walk graph and makes the stops fit; you choose the shape of the day.
- `why`: one line of what the row rests on (the trade, its hours, its staff).

`names`: one pool for the district: at least the minimum of given and family names in the rules, from several origins with none over the rules' share, in the rules' name form, none on the avoid list, no date or era in any name. Names are people's, not a period's (GDD §15).

What the Handover needs of the cast (Motive §3; read them from the mission's roles in the task, name no person):
- the row of a role with a `need` weights that need: the café server row weights `debt`, the official-driver row `cash`. Use `positions` on a need entry to weight it for that position only (a server, not the cook), and keep a temperament the role's `approach` does not refuse (the server and the driver are turned with Help, once);
- the row of a role with `regularOf` carries a routine variant with `preShift: cafe` for that position (use `positions` on the variant: the guard's day starts at the café, the clerk's does not) and a `regular` tie share, so at least half of its people hold a regular tie to the café's staff (the expander aims the guard's ties at the café staff on shift at the pre-shift stop; a stop alone does not count);
- no temperament over 60% of a row's weight, and every unit of 3 or more staff carries at least 2 temperaments (rules `temperamentsBy.mix`, checker P-10);
- the café keeper knows the needs of workmates and regulars; code derives it from the ties (rules `knowledge`, P-11), so give the café `workmate` and `regular` ties and let its guards and clerks pass through it;
- every field a person carries is read by a rule (P-12): add no field, no bio, no value or dream; `why` stays about the place, at most 300 characters.

Fit the Handover's ground. The mission runs 17:24:30 to 17:32:30; the Office's shift ends at 17:30 (the chime). Its roles are in the task, with the conditions each must meet (`alone`, `tiedTo`, `leavesTo`, `waitsFor`: M-2 reads them, and a cast that fails them cannot play the mission). Cast the Office so that at that minute:
- exactly one guard stands the public door (`count` and `posts` on the guard, the rest of the guards inside with `side: official` or at the service door), and a watch (`watch`) takes the door as he leaves: the watch's guard calls at the café before his shift (`preShift: cafe`) and the day's door guard after his (`evening: square`);
- the councillor and his driver are one each (`count: 1`), driven (`commute: car`): the councillor works inside at the official side, the driver at the bay (`post: bay`), and they leave together in the car at the chime;
- the driver holds a workmate tie to the councillor (`positions` and `with`); the door guard holds two regular ties to servers (`with: server`), one `at: pre-shift` and one `at: evening`, the second to the server who is on at the chime; when the rules have the lover kind, the same guard carries a `lover` pattern (`share: 1, positions: [guard], posts: [door], with: server, at: evening`) so the door guard and the server on at the chime are lovers (Inez and the door guard, Sky's canon): the code ties them along the regular tie and gives both the affair, and the mission's waitress must be that server, so the server row's `debt` weight must be high enough that she is likely to carry the need `debt` too (code draws the lover by routine, not by need);
- a tie with `at` is a promise about the day, kept by the code: its carrier is drawn only among the routine variants that make that stop (`preShift: cafe` for `pre-shift`, `evening: square` for `evening`) wherever the café is open then, so you need not match variants to ties or to shifts. A routine variant has no shift of its own: do not write a variant "for the night watch" (a stop before a shift is not made while the café is shut, and the watch's evening is not asked for). If no variant of the row makes a stop its tie names, the run fails (P-0) naming the person;
- the café's chairs and standing points are 48 and shared out by the code, people whose ties name a stop first: when they are all taken a stop is not made (a meal is eaten on site, an evening or a stop before the shift is dropped) and the run only notes it. You cannot double-book a chair, and need not count them; send fewer people to the square only if you want the square emptier;
- keep `leaveBeforeMin` small ([0, 6] at most): the minutes between two stops are spent walking, so a 40 minute slack is a 40 minute walk over 200 metres.
You do not name anyone and you do not edit the mission.
