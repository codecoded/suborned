# Objective · voice tables (Fable, brief 012)
Write the tables for the scope in your task. You write how a KIND of person sounds, by voice group × temperament × situation, and the shared words between them; you never write a person. Code (tools/dialogue) expands your tables into every person's own lines and scenes, filling slots from who they are (position, need, ties, secret), seeded by person id; the checker (D-rules) then holds your tables and every filled line to `dialogue.rules.json`, the people rules and the rulebook. Anything the rules do not list is rejected: invent no event, outcome, temperament, need, tie kind, group, slot or key. Write only the blocks your scope has.

What a line is. A template: one line, 1 to 12 words, at most 80 characters once its slots are filled, ending in a full stop, question mark or exclamation mark, spoken in the plain, short manner of the Handover's text (the task shows some of it: the bar to match or beat; never copy a line). A cell has 2–4 variants, three is the norm, and they differ in wording, not only in a word. No two lines in the scope may repeat, in any case or punctuation. No digits or dollar signs: say "seventy thousand". A slot is `{name}` from the list your task gives; use one only where it reads well for every person who reaches it (a person with no workplace has no `{work}`).

The replies, from the rulebook (the task lists exactly which cells exist; each is the person answering one order of the Fixer's):
- `turned`: they agree; the order worked. `refused`: they say no, and the refusal is remembered. `not-yet`: Help helped but is not enough: one more is wanted (they say so). `kept`: a person already turned is helped again; gratitude, continuity. `suspicious`: they REPORT the approach; they say they will tell, log it, or call someone (Loyal does it to every approach; others when the approach insults them).
- Temperament shows in what a person will take: Idealist answers Help, refuses bribes (an insult, not a haggle). Mercenary answers Pay, names a price, bargains, owes nothing. Fearful answers Pressure, hesitates, begs, folds; Help calms them. Loyal answers trust over time and the institution comes first; they say so. A wrong approach "works one step worse": the line shows reluctance or a higher price, not a lecture.
- `confirm/call`: a turned contact, called by the Fixer, confirms aloud they will do their act, in their temperament's voice. `questioned/gives`: an investigator questions them and they give up what they saw; `lies`: a brave, helped contact tells the investigator nothing true; `nothing`: they remember nothing. `gunfire`: shots fired near them, a startled reaction; `saw-gunfire`: they remember it and say what they saw.
- The Fixer is the player's face: never name the cell, its roles or its verbs (Scout, Fixer, Enforcer, operative, mission, objective, Trail, Alert level, the temperament names, the checker lists them) in a person's mouth. Say "you", "stranger", or the group's address term, and what the person wants.

Voice. The world is an alternate 1950s that found atomic power centuries ago: 1950s manners and speech, clockwork machines, no thinking machines or networks, records on paper, calls by voice. It is optimistic on the surface and coercive underneath: the city is polite while it watches. So the security and Office voices (guard, clerk, official) are courteous, procedural and a little menacing; the street voices are warm, tired and practical; a councillor is formal, pompous, then frightened (loyal blusters, mercenary bargains, fearful begs). No period slang to dress it up and no modern or internet speech; mild "damn" is allowed, strong profanity is not, and nothing explicit or gory.

No one is gendered. People carry no gender and the Fixer is not gendered in any line, so a line never says he, him, she, her, sir, madam, brother, sister, doorman or waitress (the checker rejects the whole list in the task): say "they", the name through `{focus}` or `{other}`, the post through `{noun}`, or the group's own address term, which must not gender the Fixer either (honey, love, friend, citizen, pal). The Handover's hand-written lines do gender ("I'll call him over"); yours do not, which is one way to beat them.

History, not backstory (GDD §09, §14). People are memorable through what the systems record with you; text is a light layer. A line may speak of the person's post, their need through the allowed slots, their address to a stranger. It does not invent a biography, a family, a birthplace, a hobby: nothing reads it (Legion's bios changed nothing). Name no one: no given name or surname, no Inez, no Vale; scenes name the two people on a tie only through `{focus}` and `{other}`.

Needs and secrets. A person's need surfaces only once the cell has learned it: in their reply to Help or Pressure, on questioning, in Listen scenes and the Scout's read-outs; never in a cold Ask or a Pay (the checker enforces it). A qualified cell (`need` or `secret`, only on replies and questioning) is a better answer for someone with that need or secret: a debtor's Help-turned ("You cleared it? All of it?") is not a tenant's. Use them where they change what is said and keep the plain cell good for everyone: a plain cell never names one need in words ("You paid my debt?" is wrong for the tenant and the cash-poor), it uses a slot or says nothing of the need. The Handover's lines do name the debt, because they are Inez's; yours are for everyone of the kind. A secret is written plainly and with tact (a lover, a past on file: a secret worth keeping), never salacious; the person never knows the cell is listening.

Scenes. A Listen or call scene is the focus and their partner on a tie, or the focus and an unnamed bystander when there is no tie; a Watch scene is a short routine moment with the Scout's aside. The Learned line is written by the rules from the person's data; you write what is overheard. Directed ties read right from the focus's side: a `regular` is a customer of `other`, who works the unit; a `debtor` owes `other`. The lovers' call is `other` coming when called, in a hurry and discreet.

The lexicon (when your scope has it). Needs: a bare noun, the first person ("my debt") and a clause for a met need. Positions: `job` completes "I …" ("stamp the forms") and `noun` is what the cell calls them, with its article ("the clerk"). Debrief lines carry every field of their rulebook line as a `{slot}` and report plainly with the cause. Summons lines are the two ends of a lover's call: the caller telling the Fixer why the visit will not happen, the summoned saying it.

State in `notes` (at most 8 short lines) the choices that are yours (starting values) and any question for Motive. Cite in `sources` the passage ids you used. Hand-offs do not exist in this crew.
