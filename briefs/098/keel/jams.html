Jams: the law now

  • Boxed in. A passing car that has stood 3 s (traffic.stand.standS) on a way out boxes it in, as any solid car does: Go is refused car-boxed naming it, a Drive drive-boxed, the official car waits at the pickup, and at the close a boxed car is closed-in, cause boxed. A car merely pausing (under 3 s) boxes nothing.
  • Held behind traffic. A mission car whose next place on its leg meets a passing car that has not been shot holds there, its leg's clock waiting, for as long as that car is in its way. No cap. A car already touching it (a turn's rear swing) may always move apart; only moving into a car is held.
  • Shot cars are got past, physically. A wreck in a mission car's next place is pushed aside (traffic.shove): away from the mission car's line, toward the side the wreck's centre is on (its right, the kerb, at a tie), at 1.5 m/s, never into a building, street furniture or another car, 6 m at most in all. The mission car holds while it pushes and drives on once the wreck is clear. The wreck stands where it was pushed from then on: its footprint, cover and box move with it. A Go (the getaway car's or the lent car's) whose way out a wreck stands on is taken, and the wreck is pushed off the way as the car leaves. The log says traffic.shoved ("The rival car pushes the wrecked passing sedan aside.").
  • Nobody waits for each other for good. A passing car that stands in a mission car's way while waiting for that very car makes room: it backs off along its route (into the gap behind it, keeping 0.5 m) by the least that clears the mission car's next second of road, or its next few places; failing that it pulls aside away from it, across the centreline when the other lane is clear, as an overtake goes. Its sideways move tests bodies only.
  • A held car's road holds nobody. A mission car held 2 s in a row is not coming soon: the road it would drive is no car in anyone's way and crosses no junction (its body still is, and does), so a queue whose head waited for that road can move and the jam clears.
  • The rival car's turn-in is seen coming. Before its timetabled turn-in, traffic sees it coming along the way it faces for its first 3 s of road, so no passing car is mid-junction, nose to nose with it, when it turns in.

The last three came out of the sweep, not the brief: with the cap gone, two cars could each wait for the other. The commonest cases were the official car's drive round, which still runs its precomputed route against the flow on Apron Road, and the rival car turning in mid-junction at Orbit Walk's mouth. Holds of a minute or more of that kind went from 7 to 0 in 1,800 runs.

Where my reading might clash, built as read:

  • "Shot cars can get past" is applied to passing cars. The mission's own cars keep brief 096's rule: the rival car shot to disabled across the cell's way out still boxes it in (blown, it no longer does). Extending Sky's rule to them would change the rival block (Motive's rules page). Sky's call.
  • "Abandoned by a driver who fled gunfire": in the engine a driver leaves the car only when the car itself is hit (that car counts as shot). A driver who hears gunfire brakes, then ducks (Fearful, Loyal) or drives off (Idealist, Mercenary). A ducked driver's car is ordinary traffic: it blocks until they sit up, 30 s after the last shot. In loud plays that now holds the rival car for 20–90 s. If Sky meant that some drivers run from gunfire on foot and leave the car (which could then be got past), that is a new reaction for Motive's natures (step 2).

People on the street

  • What counts. A person's street time is the ticks their routine has them out of doors inside the window, from 0:00 to the close: the people file's stops and legs with this game's seeded shift of their day, an added passer-by's own visit, and the cast by the people file's stops for them (Inez on the terrace all mission, the driver at the car to 7:10, Vale out from 5:10 to 7:10: 2.1 people). The count is that, summed over the clock (Street.Ticks, GameDialStreet.cs).
  • Today 16 (15.9), not 22. The 22 in step 0's report counted the guards on their posts too (6.2 on the street) and missed the cast. The guards have their own row, so the people row counts the rest. Sky's call if the row should hold everyone on foot instead.
  • More. Passers-by are added in id order, each with their own visit, until the street holds the count (the nearest a whole passer-by allows). One whose visit misses the clock altogether is not added (nobody would ever see them).
  • Fewer. People leave in the dial's fixed order, skipping tenets (the cast, a lover's tie) and anyone whose routine keeps them indoors all mission (the cooks, most clerks at their desks: they change nothing on the street), until the street holds the count. The fewest is the cast's own street time: 2.
  • Today's number (16) plays today's world exactly. Both directions take a prefix of a fixed order, so one more never takes anyone away and one fewer never brings anyone back.
  • kinds.people.max is now 500 on the street (about 2,000 passers-by), so a hopeless setting still starts in time.
