The law's own moves at a walk

What it does. The law's own moves for a body go no faster than a walk (the city's, 1.4 m/s), or the body's own step this tick when that is faster. Those moves are: a sidestep, making room (for a tenet too), giving way, a way round or back, a spot of a stop's form, a ring round its own place, stepping off ground no body stands on, and sliding round whoever is in its way. Two things keep the law's pace: its own way (its own place, catching it up, a change of plans' place), and stepping clear of one already inside two radii. The second is what keeps "no pair inside two radii". Nothing is new in the rulebook but the words (bodies.rule, and body-shove-cm-s's reason): the cap is the city's existing walk, so nothing new to tune.

Why x-person-0050 dashed. He comes out of the café door, whose point is on solid ground. The law's "step off ground no body stands on" gave him shove (40 cm a tick, capped only at his run, 26), and he slid at his run for 0.7 s. A way back then slid him straight back: the no-dancing rule only remembers the last tick's slide, and the move before was a plain step.
