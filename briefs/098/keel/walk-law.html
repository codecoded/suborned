The fountain. People and agents walk between the atom fountain and its four gardens, both ways. Those gaps are 1.9 to 2.3 m wide. 0.48.0 walled 12 of the 16, and the 2.3 m ones had no route at all. Now all 16 are crossed on a straight line. Across the whole zone, every gap a body fits through is walkable (161 of 161, below).

The café lane. On 0.48.0 the Listen parked the Scout and the Fixer 85 cm apart across the café's only lane, and people stood behind them for up to 117 s. Now the Scout sits, and a Listen with no chair stands by a wall and never across a passage. The café front's longest hold at x4 goes from 117 s to 16 s, at people=64 from 84 s to 5 s, and in the default game from 32 s to 2 s.

The listener sits. Where a café chair comes free in time, the Scout walks to it, steps into the seat as a customer does, and listens seated. In quiet-a that's 41 Listens of 50 (none on 0.48.0), prepared-loud 37 of 100, thin-quiet 26 of 50. Elsewhere there's no chair in reach, and she stands by a wall. Walking to the chair makes the Listen start later: in quiet-a the order-to-act wait is 9.8 s at the median (3.9 s on 0.48.0), at most 14 s.

Idle agents step aside. An operative with no order, standing off the place the player put him, steps aside at a walk for someone stuck behind him, then steps back. He never does it while acting, watching, listening, or standing where the player put him. This is Aster's reading of Sky's words, for Sky to confirm.

What gets harder. The rival crew now find cover where the old margin kept them out. At the tip of the café terrace's west wall, a rival can loop: he advances, hides, steps out of the shooter's line, and advances again. The quiet-g reference wins 33 % (78.5 % in c060). That's for Pulse and Motive, and it isn't tuned here (Bands, below).

The walk law

A walker keeps the rulebook's bodies.radius (30 cm) plus bodies.margin (5 cm) from every solid, a building's face included. Both values are read from the rulebook. The margin is one raster cell: the walk is tested on 5 cm samples, so between two samples a body's centre can come up to half a cell nearer a face. Five centimetres keeps its disc off every face with room to spare. Before, the walk kept the plan's design r (65 cm) on 50 cm cells, a face 30 cm, with no corner cutting.

How it's represented: the site carries a 5 cm walk raster (grid.fine, written by tools/sim/walkfine.ts). The engine keeps the 50 cm cells as route topology (the drive, takedowns and lanes are untouched). A cell's nodes are its pieces of free raster, 46,115 nodes and 357,152 edges for the zone. Neighbours join wherever the true line between them clears. Routes are string-pulled on the raster (GridFine.cs).

The solids are the pieces as built. The 097 pins are gone and the volumes come from kit.collide.json: 14 builder-declared, 31 measured from their meshes. The plan's design widths and W-rules are untouched (tools/plan has no change this round).

What Latch must draw

  • Seated listeners. ListenSpotNow's at is the seat. SeatedAt(name) says she's in a chair, and she starts the act seated. Draw her sitting, as a customer, and standing up when her next order walks her out.
  • Stepping into a seat. Anyone (the Scout, a customer) takes the last step into a chair across the table's ground, at a walk. It isn't a slide or a jump.
  • Idle step aside. An operative with no order moves aside at a walk and back (BodyIdle). No snap.
  • Door beats moved. The dial's guards stand 2 m out from the Office's public door and 50 cm off the official door, on its open side. The beats are no longer in the pilaster slot.
  • The walk is finer: people pass close by planters, tables and kerb furniture, 35 cm from the face to their centre. A figure wider than the 30 cm radius will touch faces.
