Patrol robot sound

Chime · brief 104 · 2026-11-10 · the ball-balancing patrol robot (DK_PatrolBot104) · a proposal with drafts, nothing wired

$0 spent · all synthesis7 drafts + one listening aidNot in Unity: hooks come after Sky listensI can't hear these: the numbers only rule things out. Sky's ears decide.

Recommendation

Make the robot's whole voice by synthesis, from parts it shares: one grille speaker, one tin shell, one gyro hum. That way every sound reads as the same little machine. It's deterministic and free, the licence is our own code, and a tweak is a number change.

  1. The draw is the cue that matters most. At 38° the hatch hardly shows (Vela's demo notes), so the player has to hear a robot draw. The rulebook gives a hostile 2 s from first sight to first shot (react-s), so the draw is the warning: "about a second until it fires". The draft ends on two rising "ready" tones, because those still carry over gunfire (12.3 dB clear) and on a laptop speaker.
  2. For this robot, replace four of the pack's sounds: the walking footfalls (patrol_loop, patrol_chase) become the roll and hum, because a ball has no feet. The relay clunk (patrol_break) becomes the siren. The bolt gun (fire_robot) becomes a pistol with the shell ringing. down_robot gets a gyro spin-down on top of its crash.
  3. The siren sounds once, when the robot leaves its route. After that, only the beacon's light stays on. Five robots wailing non-stop would bury the clock cues and the police siren.
  4. Before the draw can be wired, the sim needs one read-only hook: the tick a robot takes a target it has cause to fire on. Keel and Latch can do it; sound reads the sim and changes nothing.
  5. No paid pass now. If Sky wants more real-world texture in the hatch or the down, there's a tiny Seed Audio pass at the end (about $0.15, capped at $0.50).

Draw · robot_draw

The latch lets go with a click. A small geared motor whirrs up as the panel swings (0 to 0.45 s), and the panel thunks on its stop. The sidearm scrapes out on its rail, the ratchet speeding up (0.5 to 0.9 s), then locks home with a hard double clack. Last come two rising tones, C6 then F6: "ready".

-20.0 LUFS-M · peak -3.9 dBTP · 13.3 LU over the bed with the robot rolling near · 11.5 dB over gunfire

Siren · robot_siren

A 1950s motor siren in miniature: a rotor chopping air through a horn. Two quick whoops from 380 up to 1,240 Hz, then a wind-down, with a short street echo. Cute because it's small and quick; still unmistakably a siren because it's the classic shape.

-22.0 LUFS-M · sweeps 1.33 octaves (police: 0.57) · shape 13.1 dB from the police siren

Compare: the pack's police siren (far, panned, from where they come in)and the clunk it would replace for robots

Roll · robot_roll (loop)

The ball on stone: a low rumble and a grit band that ride the ball's turn, and a soft thump once a turn where the band's seam meets the ground. Recorded at 1 turn a second (1.26 m/s); the host scales pitch and volume with speed.

-36.0 LUFS-I · seamless 4 s loop (seam step 0.05 of a typical step)

The pack's walking loop it would replace

Idle hum · robot_hum (loop)

The atomic gyroscope that keeps it upright: a soft 118 Hz hum that sways twice a loop as it corrects its balance, a faint high whine beating slowly, and a clockwork escapement ticking six times a second. It plays always, under the roll.

-42.0 LUFS-I · 7 LU under the bed · nearly nothing on a small speaker (-50): felt close up, not heard far off

Curious · robot_chirp_curious

A question from the grille, "hm?": a low note, then a higher one bending up at its end. It plays when the face goes curious.

-28.0 LUFS-M · 22.8 dB over the bed · 22.9 dB in shape from the notice ting

Alert · robot_chirp_alert

"Hey!": two quick high notes and a held lower one, with a buzzer's rasp in it. Firmer than curious, but not a scream. It plays when the face goes alert.

-27.0 LUFS-M · 20.5 dB over the bed · buried under gunfire (-0.1 dB), which is fine: it comes before a fight

The 2D stage cues it sits under

Shot · robot_shot

A small, hard, dry crack with a pneumatic push and a drop in pitch. Then what no person's gun has: the robot's tin shell rings for about a fifth of a second, and the sidearm's 18 mm recoil clacks back on its rail.

-18.9 LUFS-M (cell pistol -18.5) · at least 6.4 dB in shape from every other gun; the pack's closest pair is 4.8

The guns it sits among: the old robot bolt gun, the cell pistol, the police revolver

Many robots on screen

  • How many: the Handover runs at S2: the patrol and up to 2 response robots. At S4 ("swarm") it's the patrol and 4.
  • Loops: the hum and roll play for the 2 nearest robots to the camera's ground focus, on 4 sources of their own outside the 16-voice pool, as the burning car has. Past those two, robots make no loop sound, and at 45 m the 3D-near rolloff has faded them out anyway. If 3 or more ran at min distance, the sum would rise by about 4.8 dB, a muddle rather than information.
  • Danger before guns: the draw (priority 20) is never stolen. The siren is one voice for the whole response, so a dispatch of four robots sounds once, from the nearest. Chirps get 2 voices in all, so a crowd of faces changing at once doesn't turn into chatter.
  • Guns: the robot's shots join the fight group's 6 live gunshots, each delayed 0–40 ms and pitched ±3 % by hash, as the pack already does for volleys.
  • Replays: nothing here reads a random stream or writes to the sim. Variation comes from Fnv1a32(id|tick), as in the pack's wiring (briefs/087/wiring.md §1).

What the hooks need (for later, not now)

  • Draw: the tick a robot takes a target it has cause to fire on. That's the start of its react-s window, 2 s before the first shot outside a fight. The sim draws hostiles' guns in its own way, so this wants a read-only field or a log line from Keel, which Latch plays. This is the one hook the design depends on.
  • Siren: patrol.alarm and patrol.breaks, and a dispatch that sends robots only. These are the codes patrol_break uses today.
  • Chirps: the face changes that RobotFace already makes from the attention stages: curious on noticed, alert on wary and reporting.
  • Roll and hum: the robot's pose speed, as the rival car's engine reads it. Stop and settle plays when the speed drops under 0.1 m/s.
  • Hit, down, stand-down: patrol.hit, patrol.down, response.hit, response.down with a robot-* id, and patrol.stands-down. The flutter reads the robot's health.

Measures

From the WAVs, by make/robot_measure.py, with brief 087's own sums (briefs/087/chime/audiolib.py: BS.1770 loudness, 4× true peak, third-octave masking margins). Bed + roll is street_bed_even (-38.0 LUFS-I) with this robot's roll and hum at min distance. Gunfire is the cell pistol and the robot shot alternating every 0.75 s on the fight bus. That's twice as dense as brief 087's test, so it's the harder case. Margins are dB in the cue's three strongest bands. These numbers rule out masking and clipping. They say nothing about whether a sound is good.

  • The ceiling holds: no draft peaks above -1.0 dBTP, and none clips. The shot sits at the ceiling. I soft-clipped it, so it reaches the pistols' loudness without going over.
  • The draw's "ready" tones alone come to -22.9 LUFS-M and stay 12.3 dB clear of gunfire in their bands (1.0–2.5 kHz). The clacks don't: they carry the character, the tones carry the warning.
  • The siren is only 2.8 dB clear of dense gunfire (the police siren measured 0.0 in brief 087's test), so it needs the fight-bus dip while it rings, the rule that already exists.
  • The loops' seams are clean as WAV (step ratios 0.05 and 0.25 against the checker's limit of 2). After Vorbis, Latch should listen to them at the loop point, as with the bed.
  • Running the script twice writes the same bytes; the sha256 of each file is in measures.json.

A paid pass, if wanted (not run)

Synthesis covers every cue, and I'd start there. If Sky hears the mechanics as too clean or "electronic", a small Seed Audio 1.0 pass through tools/paid/paid.py could add real-world texture for layering: the latch and rail, the shell ringing, the tip-over crash and the ball rolling off. That's 4 sounds × 3 takes = 12 calls. At the ledger's prices for one-second effects (about $0.0066–0.012 a call), it comes to about $0.15. I'd set the budget at $0.50. It would only be layered under the synth, never replace the draw's tones, and it carries brief 087's inference that Seed Audio counts as a commercial Partner Model on Runware.
