2 · Scene kinds

Six kinds, each told by a quiet header tint, an icon and, for the two that matter, a shape: the warning has a striped header, the fight the darkest header and a lamp. None of them uses red, amber or violet for the card itself.

3 · How it works

  • A card is one event: header (kind icon, verb icon, title, where, clock, NEW), 3 to 6 lines with speaker chips (cell = Forma's portrait; Inez a cup; guard the amber shield; driver a wheel; Vale a star; rival the violet diamond; watcher a camera; passer-by a plain figure), a highlighted result footer, and a tick with its key.
  • Live scenes (frame 01): a Listen running shows its card with the bar and time left; lines arrive as the act progresses, a typing indicator shows who speaks next, and the footer says the result lands when she finishes. At completion the footer fills in with the result, the bar goes, the unread dot and the tick appear.
  • Click a card pans the camera to where it happened and rings the place (frame 02 shows the ring on Inez while the card is hovered).
  • Acknowledge: click the tick, or press G for the newest. G is free in briefs/011/play/HOW-TO-PLAY.md (taken: 1-4, Q/W/E/R as listed there, F1-F4, Tab, Space, =, -, N, H, arrows, F1, Return). L opens the history (also free). Neither pauses anything.
  • Stack: unacknowledged cards stay; newest on top and expanded, the other two visible are folded to their header and footer; the rest are "+N more" (frame 04). The bar shows "N new". Acknowledged cards go to the history: unread first with "Read all", filters, earlier scenes, a scrollbar (frame 05).
  • The fight (frame 06): the cards fold to one line each so the street stays clear; the newest still has its tick. Going loud, "Tech is down" and the alarm are the three.

4 · What happens to the bottom-left dialogue log

Recommendation: replace it with a one-line ticker under the roster, and move everything said to NPCs and by NPCs into the feed. Frames 01 to 06 all show the ticker (the strip under the column): it carries only the cell's own chatter, the order acknowledgement and the "arrive" line ("Close enough to hear. I'll keep my head down."), for a few seconds, then fades. It does not stack and cannot be acknowledged. Reasons:

  • Two logs saying the same thing in two corners is the clutter Sky has already marked down. The feed has the full scene and the history, so the log's history job is done.
  • The ticker keeps the one thing the feed cannot: the instant "I'm on it" when an order is given, before any scene exists.
  • It frees the bottom-left, so the left column can grow, or nothing sits there.
  • Cost: the round 6 expandable dialogue (frame 06 of round 6) is gone. If Sky wants the cell's whole conversation too, the history can carry the ticker lines as a "Cell" filter.

5 · Notes and open questions

  1. Real-time reading load. A three-line scene takes about eight seconds to read, and a Listen produces a line every few seconds. In a busy minute (three agents acting) cards will arrive faster than they can be read; the stack, the unread count and G are for that. The feed should never take the camera or the pause.
  2. Screen. The feed is 4.9 to 6.0% of the frame as a rectangle while live (10.5% with history open, 2.8% in a fight). It sits under no agent or Inez on these beats. With the left column, the bottom-left log gone and the feed on, the frame is about 14 to 15% HUD; round 6 was 10 to 13%.
  3. Spoilers. The overheard exchange shows people talking about each other, so the engine must only reveal what the Scout could hear (range and need), and the learned footer must match what the Intel list now says. Keel owns that; the card is only the surface.
  4. Cards need an event model: id, kind, verb, where (a place for the pan), clock, lines (speaker role + text), result, live (progress), acknowledged. Fable's text.json events map to these directly (arrive, read, reply, confirm, guard-at-cafe, run-seen, going-loud, agent-down, chime, rivals-arrive).
  5. Not shown: pan animation; the card slide-in motion (it enters from the right edge over about 0.25 s and the older cards shift up); investigator cards; the audio cue.
