# Overheard scenes and state barks in stealth, tactics and text-only games (brief 012, research strand 2)

Strand 02 for brief 012 (the dialogue generator). Assembled 2026-10-16 by Fable from four finished sub-reports, after the strand's coordinator stopped before writing this file. The sub-reports are kept in `out/resume/research-02/` (`a-hitman-thief-splintercell.md`, `b-arkane-deusex.md`, `c-tactics-watchdogs-ac.md`, `d-social-spy-textonly.md`; `out/` is git-ignored, so this file is the record). Nothing here is added from outside them. Where a report said its source was thin, blocked, or a search excerpt only, that is carried over as **[snippet only]** (the page was not readable, only the search summary) or **[unverified]** (no source found). The "what transfers" lines and the synthesis at the end are mine and are marked so; every other claim is a source's.

The question: how do stealth, tactics and text-only games plant a fact in a short overheard exchange, how do they voice a watcher's state, how do they keep those lines readable and unrepeated, what did players criticise, and what carries to a generator whose tables are written at build time and picked by seeded code, with a feed of short text lines beside a top-down view and no AI at runtime.

Strand 01 covers how systems select and remember lines (Valve, Naughty Dog, Halo, Firewatch, Hades and the rest). Strand 03 covers craft and lore. This one covers the two jobs they leave open: the overheard scene as a delivery vehicle for a fact, and the state bark as feedback. Overlaps (Ruskin, Orkin, Mikkelson, the Kotaku bark article) are cited once, here or there.

## What Suborned already has

Measured against `tools/dialogue/`, `contracts/missions/handover/text.json` and `unity/.../HostFeed.cs`:

- **Overheard scenes are results of a timed act.** A Scout's Watch or Listen opens a feed card; the scene's beats arrive as the act's progress runs; the card's footer is the "Learned:" line, written by rules from the person's data, never by the model. Call scenes (the lovers' summons) play a beat every 1.5 s. A scene is 3 to 6 beats of 1 to 12 words.
- **Facts are planted two ways today.** The Handover's hand-written Listen scenes state the need outright ("The garage bill won't wait", "I'm terrified of losing the post"). The generator's Listen scenes are written by tie kind × temperament and cannot know the person's need, so they can only carry it through the closed slots `{need}` `{own}` `{met}`.
- **Watcher state is hand-written and mission-bound.** `ai-state` (alerted, advance, engage, hold), `run-seen`, `drawn-seen`, `alarm` and `crowd-state` (startled, fleeing, sheltered, returning, back) exist in the Handover for its roles. The generator voices a person's replies, confirmations, questioning, `saw-gunfire` and `gunfire`; it voices no watcher state and no step down.
- **Pick** is `pickIndex`: a hash per use with only an immediate-repeat block (see strand 01, rule 4).

## 1. Hitman (2016, Hitman 2, Hitman 3)

Source: report a. Opportunities became Mission Stories; the wiki says they "typically reveal themselves either after eavesdropping in on an NPC conversation, chancing upon a certain event, or after examining an object" ([wiki](https://hitman.fandom.com/wiki/Mission_Stories)). Guidance has three levels (Full, Minimal, Off); on Master difficulty all guides are disabled (same page).

- **Scene design.**
  - **Sapienza, "A Case Most Peculiar" ([wiki](https://hitman.fandom.com/wiki/A_Case_Most_Peculiar)).** Two speakers: the barbershop clerk and a customer. One exchange plants three facts: the clerk's cousin, a private detective, is in town on a case for Francesca De Santis; the customer saw him asleep on a nearby bench. The handler then frames it in one sentence: *"Francesca De Santis has hired a PI. Wherever they plan to meet, they are bound to do so in private."* The chain that follows: find him, wake him (a radio), overhear his phone call to her, take his disguise, meet her on the pier where she steps away from her bodyguards. He sees through every disguise.
  - **Paris, "15 Seconds of Fame".** Two NPCs talk beside a table with Helmut Kruger's book. The guests' remarks about his resemblance to 47 seed the idea; the handler confirms it (*"He has your cheekbones, 47."*). A second overhear later is Kruger's phone call arranging a private meeting after the catwalk.
  - **Paris, "A Private Meeting".** Two bodyguards talk near an exit; then Decker's call with Novikov reveals the place (the Pavilion) and that a security guard will escort Decker, which tells the player the disguise.
  - **Dartmoor.** The investigator talks to the gate guards as he arrives; the guide says to "simply listen to the private investigator".
  - **Report a's pattern across the four:** two speakers, one usually an outsider or service worker; the exchange names a person, a reason to be alone with the target, and a place; the handler restates it as one sentence about the target; the payoff is usually a disguise that gets a private meeting.
  - **IOI's stated intent.** Executive producer Forest Swartout Large: opportunities surface through "well-placed and well-presented dialogue" and "visual hints like well-placed spotlights that draw the player's eye, or draw their ear to something" ([The Gamer](https://www.thegamer.com/hitman-3-interview-the-dna-of-an-io-game-and-what-it-means-for-the-future-of-hitman-and-bond/)).
  - Scene length in lines and the hook wording are not given by any source.
- **States.** Guards have three suspicion levels: Curious (yellow "?", car alarms, breaking glass), Suspicious (orange "?", seeing or hearing a crime; decays if there is no evidence such as a body), Alerted (red radio icon) ([wiki](https://hitman.fandom.com/wiki/Guards)). The player's own ladder on the HUD: Trespassing, Suspicious, Compromised, Hunted, Combat ([Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/gb-en/hitman-3-tips-guide)). Trespassing guards ask 47 to follow them out. Enforcers see through disguises. **[unverified]** the actual spoken guard state lines; no reliable source found.
- **Readability.** Players report target-introduction lines replaying every run in 2016; Hitman 2 gated them to 24 or 48 hours since the last contract on that map ([forum](https://www.hitmanforum.com/t/frequency-of-targert-introduction-by-diana-or-other-character/6327)). A 2011 Absolution blog notes scripts shared across a group of NPCs ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-hitman-and-individual-ai-personalities)). **[unverified]** IOI's speaker priority and repetition prevention.
- **Criticism** (all [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitman_(2016_video_game))): opportunities "too overbearing", "too much guidance and hand-holding"; players "could not stumble upon meaningful opportunities in an organic and spontaneous manner" (Game Informer); voice actors reused across locations; disguise-detection rules "difficult to discern". Hitman 2 drew the same ("too much guidance, making exploration and discovery of creative assassinations less organic"). Ball State Daily: a guide for new players, "a handicap" for veterans.
- **Transfers (mine).** The overheard exchange as a delivery vehicle is: outsiders talking shop, three facts, restated once by the system. Our restatement is the "Learned:" line. The guidance setting is a precedent for letting the player turn the restatement down.
- **Thin:** writers Michael Vogt and Nick Price are named, but no interview on how incidental chatter is written was found; no GDC talk on dialogue by Christian Elverdam.

## 2. Thief (Looking Glass, 1998)

Source: report a. Primary: Tom Leonard's [postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-i-thief-the-dark-project-i-) and his 2003 article ["Building an AI Sensory System"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/building-an-ai-sensory-system-examining-the-design-of-i-thief-the-dark-project-i-) (an article; whether it was also a GDC 2003 talk is unverified). Shipped lines: [Thief Gold guard voice files](https://thief.fandom.com/wiki/TG_Sound_Folder:_GUARD1).

- **State voicing.** Speech was "the primary medium through which the AIs communicated both their location and their internal state to the player", designed for more than "the typical two states". The Dark Engine's "concept" system: an AI asks for a general concept plus tags ("moderate alert", "+sense:sound"); a pattern matcher gathers the matching samples and a weighted random pick chooses one; designers wrote the tables without code. Awareness is a range of discrete states; it rises after a delay then jumps to the goal state, and falls "across some amount of time, passing through all the intermediate states". Awareness spreads: "Through verbalization and observation sense links can be propagated among peer AIs", with controls to constrain cascades. Leonard's rules: "The result of their work must be perceivable and understandable by the player"; "the player seen by an opponent they do not see often feels cheated."
- **Real lines** (the file-name reading, `a1/a2/a3` as alert 1 to 3 and so on, is report a's inference, not documented):
  - first suspicion: "Hmm.... Someone there?", "What was that noise?", "Hmm.... Thought I saw something."; with a companion: "Did you hear that?", "Did you see something over there?"
  - second: "Who's that there? Identify yourself!", "I heard that! Stop right there!"; search: "I know you're around here somewhere."
  - third: "Hey! Hold it right there, taffer!"
  - **back to calm:** "Oh well.... Must've been nothing.", "Hmm.... Must a' been rats.", "Just the wind, I guess."
  - lost contact: "Damn, where'd he go?"
  - found something: "This didn't look like this this morning." (small change), "Murder!" (body), "Scepter's missing! Sir will have my head!"
  - passed on to peers: "I saw an intruder earlier. Stay sharp.", "Someone's killed Kevel. Watch out!"
  - escalating warning: "This is private property. Be on your way at once." then "I won't say it again, miscreant…"
  - **ambient banter that plants facts:** "Just so you're clear: if there's any trouble, we double the guard on Lady Valerius's rooms." (tells the player where guards go when an alarm sounds); "I hear Grandmauden is gonna put in some Hammer-built security."
- **Readability (mine, counted from the quoted lines):** nearly all are under ten words, one thought, and a state step has its own line, including the step down.
- **Criticism.** Leonard ties the dislike of the monster levels to the limited expressiveness of non-human creatures that could not voice their state the way humans could.
- **Transfers (mine).** Every step of the ladder needs a line, including the step down and the passing on. The banter that plants a fact is two guards telling each other something both know, which is the "as you know" trap that strand 03 warns about, but it works here because the fact is operational (where they go) and not backstory.

## 3. Splinter Cell (Chaos Theory, Conviction, Blacklist)

Source: report a. Primary: Martin Walsh, "Modeling Perception and Awareness in Tom Clancy's Splinter Cell Blacklist", Game AI Pro 2 ch. 28 ([PDF](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter28_Modeling_Perception_and_Awareness_in_Tom_Clancy's_Splinter_Cell_Blacklist.pdf), [GDC 2014](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020195/Modeling-AI-Perception-and-Awareness)). This supplies what strand 01 §4 recorded as missing ("no primary source on Ubisoft Toronto's bark system"): the chapter is on perception but states the bark anti-repetition design.

- **Three-tier anti-repetition (report a: "the most useful finding").** "The more specific a dialog line is, the more memorable it is, and the more immersion breaking it is when you hear that line repeated." Tier 1 very specific ("Did I leave that door open?"), tier 2 generic to a category ("Did I leave that open?"), tier 3 fully generic ("Huh?"). They "cycle through the tier 1 barks first so the player would get the specific barks the first few times… then we would drop to tier 2 and finally tier 3"; the generic tiers have larger pools, so the player "never hear[s] the same memorable line twice". Sound example: "I think I heard footsteps" then "I think I heard someone over there" then "I think someone's over there": the player learns that running makes noise, then stops being told.
- **Scene design: conversations that survive interruption.** Each NPC conversation is a group behaviour; writers wrote "custom breakout and rejoin lines and also blocked out each conversation"; after an interruption the conversation resumes at the start of the current block. Worked example, two guards on a football match: "Hey, did you hear that?" / "Yeah, must've been the TV, now what were you saying…"; second noise: "Hey that wasn't the TV; you better go check it out!"; then "Guess it was nothing. So what were you saying?" or, if the investigator dies, "Hey, are you ok over there? Talk to me!" Context lines when the player is on an unreachable roof: "He's hiding up there! Toss a frag and flush him out!" / "I'm on it! Give me cover fire!"; later "Do you think he's still up there?" / "I doubt it. Let's spread out and search the area!" Walsh: these "aren't callouts but a discussion".
- **States/readability.** Predictability "does not mean repetitiveness": reaction timing stays consistent while barks vary; the player "must be able to 'read'… what the AI is doing and why". A "Hey, who's there!?!" from a guard the player cannot see felt unfair in reviews; Walsh: "it's what the player thinks the NPC can see and hear." An accessibility review (strand 01) notes Blacklist conveyed guard state mostly by barks.
- **Conviction (Richard Dansky)** via [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/why-video-game-characters-say-such-ridiculous-things-5921878): "It's not only what is said, but also how often they're said"; barks are "the stuff that players are going to be hearing the most often"; "each bark should convey one piece of information" (report d, same article); bark sets swapped by map and campaign progress so enemies "would seem like they were aware of what was going on"; "the temptation is always there to get cartoony". The journalist counted "more than 130 distinct lines with 'Fisher!' in them". Clint Hocking: "Game dialogue is a form of feedback." Nels Anderson (Mark of the Ninja): a "Guess I'm just imagining things" line fired at a broken light made the guard "an insane moron"; "Make them clear and unobtrusive"; "Getting too 'clever' or flavourful can actually make the barks less useful."
- **Interrogations.** Chaos Theory interrogations yield "keypad lock codes, mission objective intel… the location of a Medical Kit" ([wiki](https://splintercell.fandom.com/wiki/Interrogation)); some are comic relief. GameSpot called Conviction's interrogations "more predictable than provocative".
- **Thin:** how Chaos Theory's guard banter was written is **[unverified]**.
- **Transfers (mine).** Specific then generic is the answer to "one qualified line heard from ten mouths". The football-match example is the model of a scene whose beats are re-evaluated against current state.

## 4. Metal Gear Solid (brief)

Source: report a, [wiki](https://metalgear.fandom.com/wiki/Enemy_status). MGS1 phases: Infiltration, Noise (investigate), Alert, Evasion (countdown), back to Infiltration. MGS2 adds Detect (the guard radios for backup and can be stopped before Alert) and Caution after Evasion. MGSV: guards "radio their findings to CP"; a base alert lasts about three in-game days; "Commencing Search" checks the last known position. The "?" means "curious or confused and will immediately resume his patrols". Interrogation answers are short, place-specific hints ("TNT, under the bed", "Beyond the Swamp, there are mines"); in MGSV you need an interpreter first ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/287700/discussions/0/458604254454652153)). **[unverified]** "Must've been my imagination" as an MGS line.

**Transfer (mine):** a named countdown state (Evasion) and a named step-down state (Caution) are what let calm-down lines be honest.

## 5. General bark sources

Source: report a (Ruskin, Orkin, TLOU, writing guides) and report d (Skyrim, Mikkelson, Kotaku).

- **Elan Ruskin, GDC 2012** ([vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1015528/AI-driven-Dynamic-Dialog-through)): hundreds of world facts matched against thousands of lines; characters "remember history" and "cascade from special to general cases"; writers add "special cases, running gags" without programmers; lines break off when a character is attacked ([Emily Short](https://emshort.blog/2012/03/16/gdc-2012-talk-on-dynamic-dialogue/)). Detail in strand 01. **[unverified]** his anti-repetition settings (slides gone).
- **Jeff Orkin, "Three States and a Plan" (GDC 2006, [PDF](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)) and Game AI Pro 2 ch. 2 ([PDF](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter02_Combat_Dialogue_in_FEAR_The_Illusion_of_Communication.pdf)).**
  - "Having A.I. speak to each other allows us to cue the player in to the fact that the coordination is intentional."
  - "If the AI didn't say it, it didn't happen."
  - The last survivor says "I need reinforcements," but "We did not really implement any mechanism… to bring in reinforcements."
  - "Wherever possible, we try to make the vocalizations a dialogue between two or more characters": a squadmate asks the injured soldier his status rather than having him cry out. Dialogue also explains inaction: "I've got nowhere to go!"
  - Canned dialogues of "2 or 3 lines", triggered "when enough squad members were in proximity and some criteria was met": "Anyone see him?" / "He's behind the tree!"; "Man down!"
  - What went wrong: decisions about what to say are made after the squad behaviour decides; the dialogue was "manually hooked… into the code", which led to asking a dismembered ally "What's your status?"
- **The Last of Us (Travis McIntosh, GDC 2014, [vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020338/The-Last-of-Us-Human); [chapter](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter34_Human_Enemy_AI_in_The_Last_of_Us.pdf)).** The talk was McIntosh's, not Max Dyckhoff's (Dyckhoff gave the Ellie buddy-AI talk). Dialogue was layered in after decision-making "allowed the AI to communicate the decisions they make to the player". **[unverified]** specific enemy callout lines.
- **Writing guides.** Barks "require a huge amount of variety"; write many and keep the best few ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/8-key-principles-of-writing-effective-game-dialogue)). Natalie Mikkelson ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/adding-life-to-worlds-with-dialogue-barks)): barks "should be as subtle as possible, bearing in mind that they're often repeatable"; kinds are thinking aloud and overheard banter "written as small excerpts of a longer conversation"; topics are life, politics and legends, reactions to the player (observing, praising, shaming), and gameplay aids (warnings, tips, goals).
- **Skyrim, "arrow in the knee"** ([Bethesda](https://elderscrolls.bethesda.net/en-EU/news/how-an-arrow-in-the-knee-made-its-way-to-skyrim)): Emil Pagliarulo was "just jamming through a ton of guard lines" late at night; "the ones that catch on are those that fans can apply to different situations".
- **Transfers (mine).** Orkin's rule is the engine-honesty rule: a bark may claim only a state the engine has. His pairing of question and answer is the cheapest way to show a state change and confirm it was noticed.

## 6. Dishonored 1 and 2, Prey (Arkane)

Source: report b.

- **Why chatter exists.** Austin Grossman to [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/in-dishonored-sometimes-the-story-is-what-you-dont-see-5950999): "NPC chatter is where you can keep world-building and play with language." It is timed to the stealth wait: "you got nothing to do but listen to them talk"; "It fills a space if you're waiting for a searchlight to finish its sweep"; "It doesn't stop the game." Tone: "fun, indulgent language that helps the world." Anything that stops play must be short: "a very short line or have an actor talk very fast. Or have somebody talking over a PA system." Production order: level design first, then a spreadsheet of "the conversations that we need". Volume: about 90,000 words of AI-driven one-liners and spoken dialogue plus 40,000 written words (Smith and Colantonio, via search summary of PC Gamer **[snippet only]**).
- **Overheard scenes as information.**
  - **Lady Boyle's Last Party** ([guide](https://www.gamerguides.com/dishonored/guide/main-game/walkthrough/lady-boyles-last-party)): three Boyles in identical costumes in white, black and red. Miss White tells you which colour is which in exchange for a drink; Lord Brisby gives the target's name. The actionable fact is split across two speakers and bought with a small favour. That the target is randomised each run is a wiki and preview claim; no developer statement found.
  - **Keyholes** (D1) and conversations heard through walls and doors (D2, Death of the Outsider) **[snippet only]**.
  - **Clockwork Mansion (D2):** two aristocrats waiting for and talking about a Clockwork demo; the room becomes an elevator, so the chatter pre-announces the level's mechanic ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/level-design-deep-dive-i-dishonored-2-s-i-clockwork-mansion)).
  - **Codes** come mostly from notes, not speech; D1's Bunting code is from torture or from Slackjaw; D2 codes change between playthroughs. No verified case of a guard conversation giving a combination.
  - **Lore that lands later.** A guard: "You're afraid of him? He's Serkonan. It's all merchants and whores down there." An investigator: "Well, [Corvo] is Serkonan." It plays while the player deals with rats and is easy to miss; its meaning arrives with a late book ([essay](https://oracleturret.wordpress.com/2013/03/26/102/)).
- **The Heart.** Lines "based on its perception of specific or general-case characters in the world": a mix of bespoke lines for named NPCs and pooled lines for generic ones; it "helped differentiate the social classes" ([RPS via GameBanshee](http://www.gamebanshee.com/xxf)). Harvey Smith: players "were replaying the whole game with the Heart out, deciding who to kill based on what the Heart said... So we made that mechanically relevant" (D2: each NPC Sympathetic, Guilty or Murderous; killing a Sympathetic one adds Chaos) **[snippet only]**. Smith: "far more popular than we ever expected"; players should "pull narrative to them when they want it" ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/continuing-the-tale-in-conversation-with-dishonored-2-director-harvey-smith/)). Line count and writer: not found.
- **Alert states.** No Arkane primary source. A critique ([Celia Wagar](https://critpoints.net/2015/03/30/stealth-game-spotting-deconstruction/)) describes a three-stage meter above each NPC, a loud noise jumping to stage 2, grace periods, and guards left "permanently on stage 1" after an alert; it cites visual cues, no barks. **[unverified]** any verbatim alert bark.
- **Criticism.** Overlapping lines "making it difficult to pick up on what they're saying... The subtitles work as a minor fix" ([Cubed3](https://cubed3.com/games/reviews/playstation-4/dishonored-2-2)); guards standing on a table while chatting break immersion ([Gameluster](https://gameluster.com/dishonored-2-review/)); chatty enemies wear thin once lines repeat and stealth chatter should be flavour more than next-step hints ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/do-you-like-bad-guys-who-talk-a-lot), **[snippet only]**).
- **Prey (2017).** Ricardo Bare: "everything in the world should have a reason for why it's there"; "We're totally OK with the player not seeing every beautiful, meticulous thing that we created" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2017-06-05-prey-arkane-alternate-history-space-race.html)). Every crew member is named ("All the dead people have stories", [Daily Dot](https://dailydot.com/prey-arkane-studios-interview)); guides count 267 people aboard and Security Station terminals track each one. Codes are planted several ways: an audio recording (0523), a sticky note of periodic-table elements whose atomic numbers are the code (9954: encoded, not stated), a note on a body, and a person who says it when you talk to her ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/99864/all-of-preys-safe-locations-and-keycodes)); some codes are dynamic. Criticism: email and audio-log reading is "the worst way to deliver a story" ([CheatCC](https://cheatcc.com/articles/prey-review-for-pc-pc/)); the main quest "light in story", side-crew stories praised.
- **Transfers (mine).** Chatter fills the wait the player already has (our Listen/Watch is 20 to 42 s of exactly that). Splitting a fact across two speakers and trading it for a favour is the shape of our approach scenes. Every overheard fact in these games has a second route; for us that is the Scout's read-out, the Intel panel and Help or Pressure.

## 7. Deus Ex (2000) and Human Revolution (2011)

Source: report b.

- **State-bark taxonomy: the closest thing to a primary one.** The ConEdit documentation ([fan-hosted, author unnamed](https://gamespy-archives.quaddicted.com/sites/www.planetdeusex.com/dx1/help/editing/ion_conedit/index.html)) lists 18 bark types owned per NPC (e.g. `AnnaNavarre_BarkIdle`): AllianceFriendly, AllianceHostile, AreaSecure, CriticalDamage, Futz (player tampers with an object), GoingForAlarm, Gore (corpse found), Idle, OnFire, OutOfAmmo, PostAttackSearching, PreAttackSearching, PreAttackSighting, SearchGiveUp, Surprise, TargetAcquired, TargetLost, TearGas. A Random event "randomly jumps to one of these labels every time it is executed"; Cycle and Random-after-Cycle also exist.
- **Overheard scene mechanics.** Overheard conversations are "non-blocking" ("Non-interactive Conversation", "Remain in First-Person Mode"); the trigger is "NPC Enters PC Radius" (16 units = 1 foot); sight triggering "was never implemented"; state is Boolean flags; repeating barks must have "Display Conversation Only Once" unchecked. Barks show in a popup, conversations in the letterbox ([Unreal Wiki](https://unrealarchive.org/wikis/unreal-wiki/Legacy:Deus_Ex/Conversations.html)).
- **Real overheard lines** (writers listed on [Wikiquote](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Deus_Ex) as Grossman, Pacotti, Todd): "All I know is we could use a few mechs for ops like this." / "I'd watch what I say about JoJo. He's got a temper." (a warning about a person) / "UNATCO got Echelon IV back up. Trust me, they know where he is." / "Executive Order 10990. It lets us take over all modes of transportation."
- **Spector's 2000 postmortem** ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-ion-storm-s-i-deus-ex-i-)): "People had to behave like people"; "Problems will have multiple solutions"; self-critique: "giving people a hint of what human AI could be in games, but delivering the goods inconsistently". It does not discuss barks or overheard dialogue. Harvey Smith (2001 essay): "The IK 'body language' communicates AI state and change to the player" ([witchboy](https://www.witchboy.net/?p=22)).
- **Human Revolution.** CASIE lets Adam "view level of persuasion and personality profiles" and influence people "using pheromones keyed to personality type" ([GameBanshee](http://www.gamebanshee.com/deusexhumanrevolution/augmentations.php)); three options per choice, a wrong pick closes that route ("conversation boss fights", DeMarle). Lapikas at GDC 2012: the team "worried that players wouldn't be able to read the feedback from the characters", but "found in playtesting that players liked the uncertainty, because it felt real" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/deus-ex-human-revolution-dev-we-shouldve-put-more-eff-5891360)). Overhearing as an alternate route: a 2010 demo let the player "overhear conversations about a lost device that held the key code for a door" and get the same information another way ([GameBanshee](https://www.gamebanshee.com/3yh)); guides list eavesdropping as one of three ways to learn passwords.
- **Criticism.** "50% guesswork and 50% reload spam until you find the right combo" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/deus-ex-non-revolution-)). The combat bosses were outsourced and panned; the social fights were praised. No source argues CASIE trivialised conversations.
- **Transfers (mine).** The 18 types are the best ready list of what a watcher might need to say: idle, pre-attack sighting and searching, target acquired and lost, search give-up, alarm, discovery (Gore, Futz), damage, alliance. The radius trigger plus non-blocking display is our Listen reach. The uncertain-read finding supports our "?" until Watch or Listen reveals the temperament.

## 8. Shadow Tactics, Desperados III, Commandos

Source: report c. Primary: Moritz Wagner, ["Game Design Deep Dive: Dynamic detection in Shadow Tactics"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-dynamic-detection-in-i-shadow-tactics-i-) (13 Feb 2017).

- **State display (a high camera, like ours).** Four kinds of space in the cone (free, stay crouched, off limits, bushes); sharp edges rather than gradients; detection as a yellow fill that travels from the guard's eyes toward the player, and "As soon as the yellow part reaches the player character the enemy actually detects them" (clearer than a cone that only changes colour; it shows the time left); one cone shown at a time ("the single half-hidden information"); identical cone size, angles and speed for every guard and difficulty; several cues fire together on detection (a sound, the cone locking on, slow motion). A pause on detection was cut because it "kills flow and feels bad". Boiling Steam complained that you cannot see every guard's field of view at once. Commandos (Wikipedia): green cone, close zone and long zone, one enemy's cone at a time; origin of cones in games is **[unverified]**; nothing found on guard dialogue there.
- **Barks.** The deep dive mentions one rule: enemies entering bushes "always announce with a voice line", a bark with a job. "?" and "!" icons in Shadow Tactics are **[unverified]**; Desperados III cone colours (green, yellow when searching, red when spotted) are **[snippet only]**.
- **Overheard scene (Desperados III).** A Steam post ([17 Jun 2020](https://steamcommunity.com/app/610370/discussions/0/2527030866854832593)): "some civilians have question marks above their heads… you can stand near them and listen to their conversation… They will give you a hint on how to approach each target." Example: two women on a balcony talk about where to grab a bottle of poison. The pattern: a marker before it plays, stand in range, the scene plays, the hint is written to a log; you must wait for the speakers' other dialogue to finish before the hint completes, then "check your hints" **[snippet only: TrueAchievements]**.
- **Criticism.** The one-cone limit, above.
- **Transfers (mine).** Mark a scene before it plays; write its result to a log so it never needs re-listening. Show time left, not only state.

## 10. Phantom Doctrine (CreativeForge)

Source: report c. "No word will ever appear more than twice within each investigation", so every keyword links exactly one pair. Solving a chain gives agents or intel and closes the case. ## 12. Assassin's Creed

## 13. SpyParty (Chris Hecker)

Source: report d.

- **The overheard hook.** "The spy must casually join a conversation in which the double agent is participating, then utter the code phrase 'banana bread.' That's it; mission complete… except the sniper heard that." ([Fev Games](https://fevgames.net/fev-games-faves-spyparty/)). The hook is loud and public; the fact (who spoke) is hidden, and the sniper infers it from context: "many snipers will immediately look around for people who aren't in conversation and lowlight them." Counter-play ("banana split"): say it and leave the conversation at once.
- **Tells.** Observable, subtle occurrences ("a brief look to the left when stealing a book"; the hand that "will dart out and suddenly retract"); "the spy's watch-check in the last five seconds was a dead giveaway" ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/hands-on-spy-party-making-paranoia-fun/)). Hecker: "By saying, 'He's left handed,' you eliminate everyone that's not left-handed"; "I wanted to make sure that it wasn't completely solvable" ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2011/06/08/chris-hecker-spy-party-interview.aspx)). On Assassin's Creed: Brotherhood: "You're not blending behaviourally, you're just the same 3D model" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-reasons-behind-i-spyparty-i-)).
- **Transfers (mine).** The hook can be public while finding its source takes inference. Each observable fact narrows a set; a fact is worth more when it excludes people.
- **Thin:** the fan-wiki decoy claim and the phrase's origin are second-hand **[unverified]**; no Hecker talk specifically on tells was confirmed.

## 14. Disco Elysium, Citizen Sleeper, Gone Home, Return of the Obra Dinn

Source: report d.

- **Disco Elysium (ZA/UM).** Skills as overheard information: Esprit de Corps delivers "flash-sideways scenes as they play out in your precinct" ("Station 41. ## What the sources support, and what they do not

**Rules the sources support (report d's list, with report a, b and c findings merged; each names its source):**

1. One fact per line (Dansky).
2. Fill the wait the player already has; never stop play for a long line; lines that block must be very short (Grossman).
3. The hook can be public; finding its source takes inference (SpyParty). The actionable fact can be split across two speakers (Lady Boyle) or encoded (Prey).
4. Outsiders talking shop plant facts; the system restates it once (Hitman).
5. Repeat the key fact through several voices (Disco Elysium).
6. Mark a scene before it plays and write its result to a log (Desperados III).
7. Voice every step of a watcher's ladder, including the step down and the passing on (Thief, MGS, Deus Ex).
8. Two voices show a state change and confirm it was noticed (Orkin, Walsh, Thief's companion lines).
9. A bark may claim only a state the engine has (Orkin, "I need reinforcements").
10. Specific then generic: spend memorable lines first, then fall to larger pools (Blacklist).
11. Conversations that can be cut need breakout and rejoin handling (Blacklist); re-evaluate against current state (strand 01, Valve).
12. Clear over clever; one piece of information per bark (Anderson, Dansky).
13. A state shown in a meter or icon is better than a state shown only by a bark (Dishonored's meter; Blacklist's accessibility review).
14. Show the variables before the player commits (Citizen Sleeper).
15. Variant lines keyed to a typed stat, with a plain default (Wildermyth); most specific rule wins, general rules fall back (Valve).
16. A profile or fact line that changes nothing is useless (Watch Dogs, République); independent random fields make scandal (Legion).
17. Missing a scene should not fail the mission (Black Flag Resynced).
18. Guidance can be turned down (Hitman's three levels; Phantom Doctrine's Analytics).
19. Plan for repetition: lines heard often become memes; the ones that spread are the ones players can reuse (Pagliarulo).

**What no source gives:** a hard number for line length or for how often lines fire (report d); the length of an overheard scene in lines (the 2 to 6 in report b's synthesis is its summary, not a sourced figure); any data on how players read a feed of text beside a game view, other than Disco Elysium's column. The numbers in `fable-dialogue-rules.md` for those are starting values to tune by playtest.

- The Last of Us human-enemy talk was Travis McIntosh's.
