# Space-age fashion: notes for brief 012 round 4 (cell wardrobes, "space age made modern")

Gathered 2026-10-15 by Forma. Sources are search extracts, a few museum and press pages; several pages returned 403 (Europeana story body, SCMP) so those claims come from search snippets and are marked secondhand. Items marked (inference) are mine. People prompts keep no date and no designer names (style contract); this file keeps both.

## What the 1960s designers did (vocabulary to draw from)

**Courrèges, Spring 1964 "Space Age" / "Moon Girl"**
- White and silver were the signature colours; Britannica: garments "made almost entirely in white" with "smooth trapeze lines" and short hems (https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Andr%C3%A9-Courr%C3%A8ges/320492 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Courr%C3%A8ges).
- Low-heeled calf-high white boots in vinyl or plastic; the Met's pair is white kidskin and patent leather "inspired by astronautic uniforms", for men and women (https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/185390 ; https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2016/01/08/462392349/designer-andr-courr-ges-master-of-miniskirts-and-go-go-boots-dies).
- Cut-out panels showing waist, midriff and back; cropped jackets, gloves, goggles, helmets; unusual materials for couture: metal, plastic, PVC (Wikipedia, NPR above).
- One snippet dates the collection to 1968; Met, Britannica, Wikipedia and NPR say 1964. Use 1964.

**Pierre Cardin, Cosmocorps (1963 or 1964, sources differ)**
- "Razor-sharp, geometric silhouettes, jewelry-sized adornments, large zippers and cutouts" (CNN: https://edition.cnn.com/style/article/pierre-cardin-future-fashion); bold colour and graphic shapes; tunics, helmets, goggles, thigh-high boots; acrylic, vinyl, Lurex, shimmering metals (Europeana: https://www.europeana.eu/stories/space-age-fashion, snippet; https://blog.fabrics-store.com/?p=17730).
- Same cuts, colours and fabrics for men and women (Europeana snippet): useful for a mixed cast.
- A soaring collar and illuminated bodice are noted by 1968 (CNN). I found no source for a ring collar or circle cut-out as such; those are my own extensions of his circle and cut-out geometry (inference).

**Paco Rabanne, "12 Unwearable Dresses in Contemporary Materials", 1966**
- Plastic (Rhodoid or acrylic) discs joined with metal rings or links, chain-mail structure that shapes the silhouette (Met: https://metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/762106 ; FIT: https://fashionmuseum.fitnyc.edu/people/686/paco-rabanne ; V&A: https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O1469014/belt-bag-handbag-paco-rabanne ; Barcelona piece on Google Arts: https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/DAEg7d_uZ74Wlw).
- V&A groups Rabanne with Cardin and Courrèges as the Paris Space Age movement.
- (inference) Discs on rings are the cleanest "hardware you can build" detail: ring pulls, disc yokes, chain-mail panels.

## Screen
- **2001: A Space Odyssey (1968):** costume by Hardy Amies, Savile Row tailor to the Queen; a Mr Porter piece quotes the archive curator that the results "weren't very futuristic at all", because Amies did not want the film to date (https://www.mrporter.com/journal/fashion/space-men-style-25458844 ; https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Hardy_Amies). (inference) Restraint dates least: clean tailoring, one strong colour, white. I found nothing on the flight-attendant uniforms or the red chairs.
- **Space: 1999 (1975):** Emma Porteous was brought in to soften "very uncompromising" uniforms with jackets and more colour (TV Zone interview via https://catacombs.space1999.net/press/tvz/wrefptvziep.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Porteous); Year 2 added turtle necks and stitching lines in the section colour (https://www.thepropgallery.com/moonbase-alpha-uniform). (inference) One shared base plus a colour code per person is exactly how a team reads at distance.
- **Thunderbirds (1965):** a simple sky-blue bodysuit, baggy for puppet movement; each of five brothers wears a different-colour sash over the left shoulder with the shared emblem on the chest (https://gerryanderson.com/blogs/blog/thunderbirds-thursday-the-international-rescue-uniforms). (inference) Strongest precedent for our brief: shared uniform and emblem, one personal colour. Round 3b's four separate wardrobes were the opposite pole; round 4 sits between them.

## Modern revivals
- Courrèges under Nicolas Di Felice (2020 on): "remixing rather than replication", vinyl trench and miniskirt, workwear and clubwear archetypes (trucker and bomber jackets) with the old codes (https://fashionunited.uk/news/fashion/nicolas-di-felice-presents-first-collection-for-courreges/2021030354143 ; https://vmagazine.com/article/courreges-fw22-the-return-of-space-age/). One tag page headline says the house stops plastic and says goodbye to vinyl; I could not open it. Wikipedia says Di Felice left in 2026 (unverified).
- Gucci Fall 2014 mod shifts with patent boots; Dior Spring 2015 couture astronaut jumpsuits (via https://www.europeana.eu/eu/blog/space-age-fashion, snippet). A 2025 exhibition traced the line to Miyake, Kawakubo, Watanabe, McCartney, Chalayan, Kei Ninomiya (https://fashionexhibitionmaking.arts.ac.uk/fashion-in-orbit-space-age-past-and-future/).
- Nothing found on bonded seams or moulded panels as such. Those are my modern-construction additions (inference).

## What this means for the cell (round 4)
1. Round 3b went to khaki, moss, chestnut, ripstop and canvas: tactical workwear, the opposite of the references. The space-age palette is white, silver, one bold block, plus vinyl, patent, lacquer.
2. Draw each person from a different corner: Scout from the Courrèges cropped jacket and cut-out, Fixer from Cardin's column tunic and circle, Tech from quilted technician's coverall and Rabanne's disc-and-ring hardware, Enforcer from sculpted yoke and gauntlets (all inference from the vocabulary above).
3. Thread like Thunderbirds and Space: 1999: white and silver base, one emblem (the lamp bead), one personal colour each.
4. Keep the 2001 lesson: restraint. A few strong shapes and colours; no costume clutter.
5. Boots, gauntlets and belts with round buckles are Courrèges and Cardin staples and are animation-friendly (rigid, below the hem rules).
