Throwaway spike, off by default (unity/Assets/Suborned/Scratch/Print073). Nothing shipped changed: grade, style contract, GDD, scenes and play camera are as they were. Shimmer verdict

The screen-anchored grain and halftone don't hold in motion. They don't crawl among themselves: they're perfectly still on the screen. The problem is that the city slides and turns under a still dot screen, which reads as a dirty window or screen door in front of the game. It's the most visible thing in every print and comic clip whenever the camera moves, and it disappears the moment the camera stops. The quick fix I tried: ground-anchored patterns, which take the screen offset of a world point, cost nothing and need no depth. They lock the paper and dots to the world on a pan (the A/B below has screen on the left and ground on the right). They only remove translation: on a Q/E turn the pattern doesn't rotate with the city, and in third person (perspective) there's no single offset, so tp stays screen-fixed. A real round would need the grain at 2 px or more, or a mip-mapped baked tile so it doesn't alias when it moves. It would also need either world- or triplanar-mapped paper in the materials, or accepting the print finish as a still and screenshot look only. My read: the print's grade and matte finish travel well; its paper and dot screen don't, at play.

1 · Vintage print (lead), preset default

Redline (Air, est.): as prototyped +0.4–0.7 ms (subtle) to +0.6–1.1 ms (default, strong; the ink brings back the depth texture). The cheap production way (grade in the LUT, matte chrome and glass in the materials, one paper and halftone pass, no ink) is 0 to +0.35 ms.

2 · Comic-book ink, preset comic

Redline (Air, est.): depth-only outlines +0.6–1.1 ms. Clean lines from normals or hulls are +1.5–3.5 ms and bring the depth texture back. Without city outlines it's +0.2–0.4 ms, which is no longer comic.

3 · Pastel haze, preset haze

Redline (Air, est.): −0.3 to +0.05 ms done properly (the existing fog, the LUT, quarter-res bloom). As prototyped here it runs through the print passes (cap plus grade pass, about the subtle preset's +0.4–0.7), which a real round wouldn't need.

4 · Matte gouache, preset gouache

Redline (Air, est.): a painterly filter +1.5–4 ms (over budget); done in the materials and the LUT 0 to +0.35 ms. This one is a smoothed 4-sector Kuwahara, radius 4 px at 1080 (100 taps), plus a matte grade and a paper wash.

Evening and night (round 3, Sky: "also evening / night time")

Same A/B split (left today, right the look), 1080p H.264, offscreen batchmode. The phase is set through the game's own day/night system: evening is LightPhase.SetEvening held at full Dusk (the lens.json evening sun, ambient, sky and grade), night is LightPhase.Set(true) with the night floor and the lamp cap at the camera focus. Edit mode doesn't run the per-frame parts, so the recorder steps them by hand every frame, plus one step and a warm grab after every switch: the evening grade (AtmosphereLens), RoadLight (kerb strip, lit windows, people fill) and every crossing head (its phase colours; the heads now run their cycle on the clip clock, so walk, clearing and stop all show). The round 2 night clip ran without RoadLight and with the heads still, so it is superseded by these.

+stencil, new in this round: cones, the crossing heads and their dot rows, and the lamp fixtures' emissive materials (DK_Lamp, the café and pavilion opals, the table lamps) are tagged with stencil bit 64 after the transparents, and every print pass (cap, paint, print) copies those pixels through untouched. Night and evening findings

  • Highlight cap. The print and gouache take almost every pixel above 235 away at evening and night. The DK_Lamp fixture band itself is protected by the stencil and keeps its level. The glow you lose under each plaza lamp at play zoom is mostly the lamp spot's hot spot on the banner and pole just below the head and its bloom, not a fixture. The stencil can't save it, because that's lit geometry, not an emissive. The cap belongs in the materials (chrome and glass), not in screen space.
  • Grain and dots in the dark. The print adds ×1.5–1.9 fine noise in the darks and comic adds ×2, which you can see as a crawling dot screen on the night paving. Haze and gouache lower it.
  • Signals. The signal layer (turquoise line and ghost, violet, amber) is untouched in every clip. With the stencil, the crossing heads and in-road dots keep their own state colours under every look and every phase, including gouache, which erased them by day. The cone keeps its red.
  • Night base. The print and gouache lift the teal-black base by about 13 levels to a foggy teal. Haze turns it grey-blue, which breaks the night. Comic crushes it to black, which loses the teal.

Exclusions (signals, crossing heads, cones, lamps)

  • Signal layer (our turquoise line and ghost, the rival violet and guard amber lines): drawn after every look by SignalsFeature, untouched in every clip, day and night.
  • Cones are world objects in the main pass, so every look grades them. The print fades the red fan toward salmon and puts the dot screen over it. Comic saturates it and keeps it. Haze washes it to pink. Gouache keeps the colour but softens the edge.
  • Crossing heads and crossing dots are world objects too. The print keeps them legible but fainter. Haze washes the head's icon faint. Comic inks them and keeps them. Gouache wipes the in-road dots and turns the head into a blob (the Kuwahara removes anything a few pixels wide). The hold ("the crossing heads keep their own colours under every light") fails under haze and gouache and is weakened under the print.
  • Night lamps: under the print the pools survive, but the screen-space specular cap clips the glowing fixture heads and their bloom, and the fade lifts the dark base. That breaks "a dark teal-black base, many small lamp pools each beside a glowing fixture".
  • The fix for all of the above: a stencil bit on cones, crossing heads, crossing dots and lamp fixtures that the post passes skip, plus the cap moved into the materials (not screen space). Redline costs the stencil at about 0.
