Brief 031 · how a watcher shows its attention

Ready for Latch, option A (Sky, 2026-10-21) Glyph, 2026-10-21. Cue board for briefs/031-everyone-watches.md (§3 cues, §7 Glyph list). Everything below is a world signal drawn unlit and emissive, outside the post grade; no sentence appears in play. The stage is the engine's AttentionStage (0 calm, 1 noticed, 2 wary, 3 reporting): the watcher shows its highest stage towards any agent. Frames marked "true size" are 1080p pixels, characters 55 px tall. Every stage must still read with colour off, and with motion off (the static shape differs).

The rule (Sky: it never reveals an unlearned cone). A cue is a state of the watcher, never a drawing in the street. (1) No cue draws a cone, a wedge, a line or a flare on the ground. (2) Calm looks identical whether anyone is in sight or not, so the cone's edge never flickers. (3) Stages are earned by standing still for seconds, and the cue falls one stage only after the clock has stayed under the mark for 2 s [S, Latch: display delay only, the sim is untouched], so stepping across an edge paints no edge. (4) The only geometry a cue carries is a turn towards the agent. It tells you "this watcher has you", exactly where you stand, which is the warning Sky wants; it does not tell you where the cone ends. (5) Only watchers on screen show anything; nothing is added at the screen edge.

Drawn on the ground when Watch is picked (cell turquoise, 3 px with a dark inner edge so it reads on cream stone and on the quay). One 38° ellipse, 1 m = 5 px in A and B, 4 px in C. The agent's portrait disc sits on its east rim. No digits in play; Alt adds the metres.

7 · The briefing's Heat word

One chip on the briefing card, under the mission title, beside the cell's start state ($, Trail). Icon first, then Fable's word (unknown / noticed / wanted / infamous, a proposal pending plain copy). The icon is a watcher's eye with 0 to 3 turns: the more notorious, the more the eye is looking. It is the same eye family as the world marker, so the player already knows it.

9 · The close-up callout (PiP): Sky, 2026-10-21

Instead of bigger robot heads: when a watcher steps up a stage towards an agent, a small round close-up of its face, lens or head appears for a moment. Same round medallion as the live portraits (brief 033), so the player reads "his face, my face". Two options, same content and rules; they differ only in where it sits.

What is the same in both

size72 px round at 1080p (the portraits are 64): 0.25 % of the screen each; 0 % at rest. A 56 px medallion is the floor.
contentThe watcher close up, nothing else: camera lens and collar, the robot's face plate, the guard's head and visor. Never on calm, never on a fall (cooling shows nothing, so stepping out of a cone paints no edge). Noticed shows once per watcher per mission (then the world cue alone); wary and reporting show once each, which is how often the engine produces them. So at most 3 per watcher per agent per mission. Civilians and rivals have none.
stayAppears in 0.15 s. Noticed holds 2 s, wary 3 s, reporting holds until the report lands (6 s cap) plus 1 s. A higher stage replaces it and restarts the timer. It does not stop for pause, and it does not show under a modal (briefing, end card).
stackingOne inset per agent at a time, the highest stage wins, the newest wins a tie; any others queue behind it (a small "+1" count, no number above 3). So at most four on screen (1 % of the screen), usually one.
clickClick an inset: the camera centres on that watcher (a double-tap, as for portraits). It never issues an order.

Glyph's recommendation was B. Fixed slots tied to the portraits cost nothing in layout and tell the player which agent, even when the watcher is off screen, which is the case the world cues cannot cover. A is better only if Sky wants the picture to stay in the street; if so, add B's edge route for off-screen watchers. Variant B2 (a fixed stack in the free top-right corner, with a mini portrait on each badge) is the same as A without the stem: not recommended, because it breaks the agent link.

Sky chose A (2026-10-21), to revisit after play. B stays on the board as the alternative; the shared rules above hold for both. A is specified below.

A, final: placement, off-screen, stacking

ruleA, as built
anchorThe medallion centre sits 70 px above the watcher's head (screen up), with a stem on its lower rim pointing at the head. The stem is a notch of 14 px; when the medallion is moved aside, the notch slides round the rim and stays aimed at the head, never longer than 48 px.
what it must not cover(1) the watcher itself (its figure box); (2) any agent (22 px margin); (3) the strip between the watched agent and the watcher (a 36 px keep-out capsule, so the line of the confrontation stays visible); (4) any other callout; (5) the HUD and a 12 px screen margin.
which sideCandidates are tried in a fixed order, so the result is steady and replayable: straight up; then up and to the side away from the agent (the side the agent is not on, so the callout moves out of the action, the stem leans back towards the watcher); then up and to the agent's side; then level away; level toward; then the same again a row higher (140 px). Away first, because the player's eye is on the agent and the watcher between them.
order of claimHighest stage first, then newest. The first claims its best spot and the rest fit round it (the fan-out in the frame above).
no spot fitsIf none of the 8 spots clears every rule within 140 px, that callout takes the edge dock (below) rather than cover anything.
movingPositions are recomputed each frame (the camera pans and turns with Q/E) but eased over 0.1 s and only re-chosen when the current spot is blocked, so a callout does not jump between spots. It fades with the watcher when the watcher is hidden (the architecture fade does not hide it).
watcher off screenIf the watcher leaves the view while the callout is up, or was off screen when it stepped up, the callout docks to the screen edge nearest the watcher: 56 px, inset 12 px, clear of the roster column, the plot scope, the notes key and the status pill, with a chevron on its edge side pointing towards the watcher (the same chevron as the objective edge arrows). Timer and stage are unchanged; if the watcher comes back into view it floats back to its spot. A click centres the camera on the watcher. So the case the world cue cannot show still reaches the player.
stackingOne callout per watcher and one per agent: the same watcher towards two agents shows the higher stage only; two watchers towards the same agent show the higher stage and queue the other behind it (a "+1" chip on the medallion's top right; when the first expires, the queued one shows for its remaining time, at most 2 s, if its stage is still 1 or more). At most 3 float in the street; a fourth takes the edge dock at 56 px, from the roster-free edge. Never more than 4 in total (1 % of the screen).
