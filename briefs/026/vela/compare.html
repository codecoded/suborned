Recommendation 1: restore #C42A1F and #EEA82D. Under identical light the current oxblood loses the "enamel" read: in shade it goes to a dull brown-maroon that sits like the pavement's shadow (Cola, post box, hydrant), and the ochre goes olive on the truck, kiosk edge and snack-machine labels. The old values stay clearly red and mustard in shade and in sun, and next to cream and seafoam they give the paintings' accent pop without making whole buildings coloured (they sit on small props, trim and roofs). The only reason for the retune (a colour distance gate against the signals) was dropped on 16 Oct. Cost: a one-line change each in mats.py, then bun run kit; it touches every red and mustard enamel at once (listed above). Signals stay readable because they draw unlit with their own shape cue. Not shown: robots look the same either way (their red is only the cart box).

Recommendation 2. (a) Keep the round-2 Planter_Urn_Play rebuild: the round bowl and the ball on its stem fix the two things visible in Unity (octagon bowl, floating diamond) and the Blender before/after shows it. (b) Adopt the soft coral and flamingo bloom cards for FlowerBed, Planter_Trough, WindowBox and HangingBasket: it moves them from red-orange (which goes deep maroon in shade) toward the paintings' pastel coral, and it needs no new art. In shade the candidate still lifts the beds, but the stronger fix for the dark read in Unity is shade, so (c) Splice should put beds and urns in light where the plan allows and not against a shaded frontage; and the footprint clash (tub palm in an urn, sandwich board in front of a machine) is Splice's rules, not the mesh. If you want the beds more vivid than the paintings, the next step is a brighter card sheet, not a material change. Nothing here was applied; say go and I make (b) in furnish_garden.py/plants.py and rebuild with bun run kit.
