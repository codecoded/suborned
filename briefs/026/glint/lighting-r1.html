1. Answers

  1. B's exposure and grade hold on the new ground. Round 1 at play zoom: mean luma 138.5 (paintings 145, old city 115), p5 44 (old city 44), spread 60 (paintings 60, old city 48), 18 percent below luma 60 (old city 25). It is already darker than B on the flat ground (150), because the textures are darker. No grade change.
  2. The textured road is darker than the old flat asphalt, and that is within the old city's range. Sunlit asphalt (132, 114, 114) and shaded (40, 38, 46), against the flat asphalt's (195, 176, 173) / (116, 110, 116); the old city's real-settings road is (65, 62, 43) lit. The shade is the only part I would lift: it is a 3.3 : 1 sun to shade ratio against 1.8 : 1 on the paving. Lifting it by ambient also brightens the paving and the frame (R1-a, R1-b: mean 145 to 153), against Sky's "too bright", so I did not. The clean fix is the texture's albedo, which is Vela's (kit materials keep their own albedo; look.json value is ignored for them): R1-d shows asphalt x1.15 (frame mean 141.5, shade 40 -> 50, lit 132 -> 157) and R1-b x1.3. Optional; my call is x1.15 only if Sky finds the road heavy.
  3. Contact shade now reads at play zoom without lifting the frame. The round-1 defaults were drawn but invisible: 0.8 m wide (about 22 px) with a power-2.2 falloff leaves about 6 px of visible shade. New contact in light.json: foot 1.6 m, strength 0.6, power 1.2; kerb 0.6 m, 0.6, 1.2; blobs strength 0.7, power 1.1. Darkest pixel at a contact line 20 to 50 levels below round 1 on the play frames (old city: up to about 36), over 1 to 4 percent of a frame; frame mean 138.5 -> 138.0.
