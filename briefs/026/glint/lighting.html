Why the world reads too bright and flat, and a proposed fix

Brief 026 phase 1 · Glint · 2026-10-19 · the 21 baseline frames (1920x1080, pinned cameras, Latch's baseline) shot four ways. All frames use the same capture path (HDR buffer, the player's post volume). Click an image for full size.

1. Diagnosis in six lines

  1. The anchors are right; the curve is not. Sunlit paving is about (238, 214, 188) against the paintings' (240, 220, 201); shaded paving (174, 162, 149) sits inside the paintings' shaded stone (161-197, 148-185, 137-173). The sun, the ambient ratio and the cream were calibrated in brief 008 and they hold. What is missing is everything between and below the anchors.
  2. Neutral tonemapping has no toe. Mid-values and shade stay lifted and the frame sits in a narrow band: at play zoom the 5th percentile is luma 95 (paintings 43, old city 63), the spread (std dev) 44 (paintings 60), and the mean 176 (paintings 145, old city 137). That is the "too bright" Sky sees: not clipped, but nothing is dark, so nothing has form.
  3. No ambient occlusion at play zoom. The renderer already has SSAO on (intensity 0.4, radius 0.3 m), but it does nothing on the orthographic play camera: the same frames with SSAO at 1.0 / 0.7 m, both sources and both orders differ by under 0.1 of 255 on average (the perspective street frames respond fully: base of cypresses, planters, kerbs). Planters, kerbs, building bases and props float on the pavement in every play frame. Probable cause: the 160 m stand-off and 1660 m far plane leave the depth buffer too coarse for a 0.3 m radius, and the SSAO pass reconstructs position for a perspective camera. Not fixable in light.json (see section 7).
  4. Colour is washed. Mean colourfulness of 0.2 to 0.3 against the paintings' 0.3 to 0.4 and the old city's 0.4: saturation +14 is applied after a flat tonemap, and the asphalt is a pale lavender grey (#a5a2a8 at value 1) that reads as a second pavement, not a road.
  5. Everything is matte. Pavement, kerb, asphalt, stone and the procedural enamel and chrome stand-ins all use smoothness 0.1 to 0.5 (chrome stand-ins pylon, column: metallic 0.6, smoothness 0.5; the adopted kit DK_Chrome is already 0.95 / metallic 1.0 and DK_Enamel 0.68, so the kit is fine, the generated pieces are not). With reflections off the pavement and no sheen on stone, stone, enamel and chrome do not separate. The paintings' pale 10 percent clipped highlights (glints on chrome, glass, wet-looking stone) are 0.5 percent here.
  6. The haze does no work inside the district. Fog is linear 1100 to 7500 m, so the district is clear air by design (round 2) and distance only dissolves in the horizon band. That is right for readability at play zoom, but it means the skyline frames get no layered distance; I left it alone (see section 6).

Hue is not the problem and is not touched: the §15 pastel base is kept; every variant shifts the median hue of the coloured pixels by under 2.5 degrees (A 0.7, B 1.5, C 2.4).

6. The old city and the paintings, for comparison

The old DecoKit city (ACES, HDRI sky, exposure -0.8) is denser and warmer, with real value structure; it is also the cautionary case: at yaw 30 its avenue is 62 percent below luma 60, with navy asphalt. Variant C goes to that depth in the shade and shows what happens when the old exposure is copied: the shade goes heavy and the pastel base is lost. B takes the old city's curve (ACES) and none of its crush.

What the paintings have that light alone cannot give (and B does not pretend to): dense planting in every bed, polished stone with soft reflections of the sky, carved facade depth, and chrome as the brightest thing in the frame. Planting, signage and facade detail are Vela's plan; the pavement sheen and chrome here are a first step on the reflections. Haze: the paintings layer distance with a blue-white haze over the skyline; ours has none inside 1.1 km. A shorter, bluer fog is a skyline-view question, not a play-zoom one, and I left it for phase 2 so the street frames keep their colour.

7. Perf and what is open

  • Cost of B: nil. ACES instead of Neutral is the same single lookup in the post pass; sun, ambient, grade and material values add no draw, pass, texture or shadow work; the SSAO feature is already enabled (raising its radius changes cost only on frames where it fires: the perspective follow view, not play zoom). Editor timing pass over the 21 frames (mean of per-frame medians): A 24.5 ms, B 24.3 ms, C 24.4 ms (each round's frames.json), so ACES, the ambient values and the materials cost nothing measurable between variants; the baseline's own pass (38 ms, taken at another moment with a cold editor) is not comparable and a like-for-like re-time of today's values is Latch's with the Air. Measured only in the Editor on the M5 Max, as the baseline was; the Air is Redline and Latch's to measure.
  • SSAO at play zoom is the one real gap. Options, cheapest first: (1) Latch checks whether SSAO fires on the ortho play camera with a nearer far plane or Depth Normals at a larger radius (my attempts changed nothing); (2) contact AO baked into the generator: vertex or decal AO along kerbs, planter and building bases (Splice/Vela, nothing hand-placed; no runtime cost); (3) a small custom ortho AO pass (Latch; SSAO cost was 24 to 35 percent of the heaviest stop in brief 008 B6, so it would need its own budget). I recommend (2).
  • The architecture fade stays about half the heaviest frame (brief 008 B6): not touched.
  • Signals draw unlit outside the grade as before; nothing here shifts them. Cones, guard and rival markers are out of scope.
  • Open for phase 2: the facade and enamel albedo (Vela's textures; the grade will carry them), haze for the skyline, whether the kit's DK_Chrome probes need a rebake after the sun change (Latch: Light/Probes.cs bakes with the sun as light.json sets it).

8. Recommendation

Take B (medium). It brings play-zoom mean luma from 176 to 154 and the 5th percentile from 95 to 58 (street frames 168 to 146 and 74 to 40), the spread from 44 to 56 against the paintings' 60, colourfulness up, the anchors held (sunlit paving stays above 225, shade paving 140 to 160) and hues within 1.5 degrees of today. The frames now have form: shade reads as shade, windows as depth, stone texture shows, chrome catches the sky. A is safe but changes little (mean 165); C is the old city's depth and loses the pastel at play zoom.

Applied to my files (not yet in the scene; the builder applies them on the next world build, which is Latch's): unity/Assets/Suborned/Editor/World/light.json (sun 1.8, ambient values 0.69 / 0.71 / 0.41, ACES, exposure +0.15, contrast 6, saturation 18, bloom 0.22) and look.json (asphalt value 0.9, kerb 0.92, pavement, foot, open 0.25, square 0.3, pylon, column, plate, hoop, trim, crossing, enamel, limestone, civic smoothness raised as in the table). Palette hexes untouched.
