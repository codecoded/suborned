Organic play: how the Handover varies game to game

Motive, brief 080, for Sky, 2026-10-30. A design only: no game file changed. Numbers marked "measured" come from a throwaway probe on the current sim, not a built feature.
The design in five lines. Each new game gets a seed, saved with the run. The seed sets the start: where everyone is and how far through their routine. After that, routines, crowds and rivals play out with no dice. Only one thing rolls during play: each shot, to hit, on an RPG stat sheet (your call: "hit rolls"). A saved run still replays exactly, because the seed and the orders are both saved. A plan built from watching this game stays true for this game.

Limits, so every start is fair.

  • Nobody starts where a watcher already sees the cell's opening: no noticing, no report, in the first 15 s.
  • Same mission facts every seed: the routes A, B and G exist in all of them.
  • Nothing changes after the start, so whatever a Watch shows holds for the whole game.
  • The ranges are proven before shipping, across hundreds of seeds (section 7). A range that makes a route unwinnable gets narrowed; the game never re-rolls while you play.

2. Does a different start feel alive? Honestly: not by itself

Measured on seven Handover routes, 40 starts each, with the same fixed orders:

  • Crowd moved ±20 s: the outcome was identical in all 280 runs. No agent, cast member or rival ended up anywhere else; typically 1 crowd figure was out of place at 3:00, and 24 at 7:00 on the longest route. Today's routines don't react to each other, so a small difference doesn't spread. The log typically stays the same until the fight.
  • Rivals moved ±15 s: 2–5 people out of place at 3:00, back to 0 by 4:00 or 5:00 because the car parks and waits. But the fixed-tick route B then won only 18 of 40 and route G 11 of 40. The script can't see the car coming. A player can.
  • Shots rolled: this is where real difference comes from (section 3's table).

What this means. You can't memorise a start you haven't seen, and the street looks different each game. But the seed alone makes a new-looking street, not a new story. The story changes when the start touches something the plan depends on (the rivals, the patrol) and in the fight. Chain reactions between ordinary people would need people who react to each other: that's your 1000-character work, later.

4. How one roll can't lose the mission

  • No big hits. Fixed damage, no criticals. Hostile pistols do 1, so the Enforcer takes 8 hits and the others 5. No mission ends on one shot, unless you shoot Vale yourself, and that order always asks first.
  • Bounded streaks. After 3 hits in a row from one hostile on one target, the next misses. After 3 misses in a row from one of the cell on one target, the next hits, if the chance is 20 % or more. It leans your way, on purpose (decision 2).
  • Warnings first. The Enforcer at 3 HP gets a roster flash and a line; a refused Go names the blocker. Check: nobody full-to-down under 5 s, the Enforcer under 7 s (today's measured 5.5 and 7.0).
  • Bad luck has an answer. Cover, breaking the line, moving out of range and Board all change the odds at once. Hostile rolls can hurt, but a player who answers should still get out.

6. Windows

The "4–7 s" isn't quite what a player faces. It's measured by a fixed script, moving one step while the rest keep their ticks. On route B the script's Go comes 0.1 s after the blocker drops; let Go wait for a free car and it goes from 27/60 to 60/60. On route A the 7 s looks like a fixed Enter firing before the guard has left (my reading of the script); once the call is made, there are 52 s of slack.

  • The rule: a window opens and closes on something you can see (guard gone, car free, loop running), never on a tick. Every window a reference route needs is at least 30 s at a person's pace, after the seed's worst case: anything seeded ±15 s needs its window to survive that.
  • Route A: the guard's 2:54 away and the 2:00 loop stay. The human bot's failure was the loop running out at 5:37, so show the tape's time left, warn at 0:30, and let re-looping (already allowed) save it.
  • Route B: driver by 5:40 keeps its 104 s. The false tip has minutes either way. Go becomes state-based, so the 4 s goes away.
  • Route G fast (13 s, and 7/60 once rolled) stays a daring line, not a reference route. Waymark re-measures all of these at human pace.

8. Risks, first slice, your calls

  • Long fights, stalemates: the 267 s tail could be a real stall. The band catches it; tuning fixes it.
  • Loud gets easier: police aim drops from 75 to 60. T2 (loud mustn't dominate) has to be re-run.
  • Rolls feel unfair: a 95 % miss happens. Bounded streaks and visible misses help, but only a person's hands can tell.
  • The biggest job is the checks, not the engine. Until adaptive plans exist, MC-5 and the route checks can't judge a seeded build.
