# Kerb return radii: sources (Datum, 2026-10-24, brief 028)

Used for `standards.streetscape.kerbReturn` in contracts/world/harbour-district.plan.json. The plan and the GDD stay the source; these are the references the values were checked against.

- **Manual for Streets (DfT 2007) §6.3.12–6.3.13; MfS2 (CIHT 2010) §9.4.10–9.4.13.** Tight corner radii (under 2 m) at side or minor road junctions, and under 6 m at other junctions, as a pedestrian measure. Seen cited in council access tables, not read in the original: https://www.devon.gov.uk/accesstoinformation/?p=15308 · https://www.rbwm.gov.uk/council-and-democracy/strategies-and-policies/freedom-information/information-requests/foi001680-foi-highways
- **NACTO Urban Street Design Guide (2013), Corner Radii.** Standard curb radii are 10–15 ft (3.0–4.6 m); in urban settings smaller radii are preferred and actual radii over 15 ft are the exception; turning speeds 15 mph or less; the effective turning radius is larger than the kerb radius. https://nacto.org/publication/urban-street-design-guide/intersection-design-elements/corner-radii/ (the page returned 403 to the fetch; text from the search summary)
- **Wakefield District Design Code B4.4.1 Crossings, guidance 4.46g.** Dropped kerbs across the minor route "can lie within the corner radius for junctions with radii of 4 or 6 metres"; "for junctions with radii of 10 or 15 metres, they should be positioned further from the major routes" (or refuges in the bell mouth); "radii should be minimised to assist pedestrians." https://districtdesigncode.wakefield.gov.uk/part-b-codes-and-guidance/b4-streets-for-people/b44-junctions-and-crossings/b441-crossings
- **AASHTO Green Book 2018 §9.6.1.4**, as NACTO cites it: about 5 m for passenger cars, 12 m or more for trucks and buses. https://nacto.org/wp-content/uploads/Part-IV-Citation-13-17-18_-Corner-Design-for-All-Users.pdf
- **Testbed** `~/Projects/the-suborned/testbed/src/civic-road-layout.js` `civicRoadBoundaries`: loop corners R 9.8 on the outside and the island R 3.2 on the inside (concentric across the 6.6 m loop), junction fillets R 1.2 to 2.6, bay corners R 1.4, concave joins 1.25 ("enough radius for the curb and blue edging to remain parallel curves").

How it was used: street practice keeps radii small so drivers turn slowly and crossings stay short. The plan goes larger (6 / 8 m) for the look Sky asked for. People cross anywhere here (standards.walking), and every junction crossing stands on the straight past the arc, which is Wakefield 4.46g's answer for large radii.
