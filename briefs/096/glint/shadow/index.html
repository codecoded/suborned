Brief 096 · Glint · Redline's item 2: the shadow map fitted to the play frame

Sky said yes to briefs/096/redline/render-audit.html item 2. The built player at the same ticks and yaws, before and after, from one scratch build (the review switch brings back the old fit for the before frames).

What changed (real, in the player)

  • TacticalShadows (the play camera's per-frame shadow fit): in the orthographic view the street band's far edge now comes from the frame. The ground spans ±(ortho size × cot pitch) along the view: ±12.8 m at play zoom 10, ±23.7 m at the planning zoom 18.5. The band ends there plus the fade (3 m, was 8) plus a 1.5 m margin, capped at the old 40 m. The fade always starts past the frame's top edge, so nothing cuts off while panning, turning or zooming, and the planning zoom keeps its reach. At zoom 10 the street cascade's bounding sphere goes from about 54 m to about 44 m: about 1.24× finer texels, Redline's option A.
  • Soft shadows High → Medium: PC_RPAsset, and the sun, whose own UniversalAdditionalLightData setting (High) overrode the asset. TacticalShadows sets it to Medium only while the play camera renders and puts the sun's value back afterwards, so other views keep theirs.

What it looks like

Almost the same frame. About 1 % of pixels differ (shadow edges), the frame's mean is unchanged, and the shadow edges in the 2× crop are a touch crisper. The finer texels and Medium's tighter filter keep the penumbra close to today's width in metres, as Redline predicted: the win is cost (9 shadow taps per lit pixel instead of 16), not a visibly sharper look. The planning frames show shadows right up to the top edge.

For visibly sharper shadows (Redline's option B, about 2.3×): set the camera's stand-off each frame from the tallest roof in the frame column (about 70 m in the Handover zone, not 160), then one cascade over about 86 m on the full 4096 map. That's the camera rig's stand-off (Latch), not this script; it isn't in this round.
