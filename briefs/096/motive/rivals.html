3. The block on arrival

The rule, and Pulse's routine as data

Every point comes from the mission's own data (its cell car stands with their ambush spots, the target's car and stand, a lookout post with a line to that stand, the crew's way off the map). Nothing is placed by hand, so any mission's rivals run it.

  1. Arrive. On its timetable, and each time it comes back onto the map, the rival car drives to the block point of the stand your car is at, or is driving to.   3. The wheelman sits in until the final fight springs, until there's gunfire at his car or within 30 m, or until his car is disabled. Then he gets out (1 s) and fights for the usual reasons. (My last page had "when he sees one of the cell". At 10 m from your car that would have started a fight at 2:56.)
  4. Drawn by gunfire, the crew on foot go to the shots as today, but the car keeps its block. Otherwise one shot anywhere would free your car.
  5. The block ends (your call 4) when, in the final fight, the crew breaks with the wheelman up and in the car (he drives off the way he came, for good), or when the car blows up. A crew that breaks before the fight leaves for 175 s, then comes back and blocks again.

4. The veteran's shot on the way in

The rule

The rival car can be shot from the moment it turns in, and it takes damage as any car does (§06): 10 HP. At 5 it stops for good where it is, and its riders get out, the first after 1 s, then 2 s apart in id order with the wheelman last. At 0 it burns, and 6 s later it explodes: 3 damage to everyone within 5 m with a line, anyone still inside included. If it stops short of its block point, it blocks nothing. A car driving past counts as a running target (−10).

What it takes on the Handover. From the square's south corner the car is in a carbine's 20 m for about 8–10 s as it comes along Orbit Walk and turns up Rocket Lane. Three down breaks the crew; with its car gone it walks off the map and is gone for the mission. The final fight is then loud (police), with no rivals and no block.
