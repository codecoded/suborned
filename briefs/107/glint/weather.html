Weather and night at the play camera

Brief 107 · Glint (lighting) · 2026-11-11 · desk study, nothing run in Unity · for Aster, then Sky

In short · The night shot finding · Haze · Night · Rain · Overcast, wind, mist · Assets · Air cost · From the crew · Questions for Sky · Summary
In short Build all of it ourselves on what URP already ships; buy nothing yet. Fix night in the old town first (the batch's night shot is daylight, see below). Then rain, which is the biggest visual win: wet paving, puddles and lamp reflections from our own shaders and decals, falling rain from particles. Haze at the play camera works only as low ground mist and a height haze, not as distance fog. Everything stays a look; the signals, the ring and the outlines don't change. Estimated cost on the Air at play zoom: about 0.4–1.1 ms of GPU time for the rain stack at 1080p, well inside the 60 fps budget.

Finding: the batch's night shot is daylight

Why (from the code)
  1. tools/game/shots.json gives the shot "night": true, and ShotRunner.cs:118 calls HandoverHost.SetNight(true). The hour and the -tod flag play no part.
  2. SetNight (Play/HostNight.cs) looks for a LightPhase. If it finds none, it quietly sets NightLook = false and returns. Its comment already says why: "the old town has no night phase yet: it stays day".
  3. Only HarbourWorldBuilder builds a LightPhase (NightPhase.Build, under the scene's lighting root). The swap that makes OldTown.unity skips that root (SwapOurs.SkipRoots: cameras, lighting, …). So the scene the game plays has no night at all.
  4. Adding a LightPhase alone wouldn't fix it. OldTownPlayLook's light swap puts its own day sun and ambient back on every play-camera render. Today the only thing that moves those values is TimeOfDay097, and that runs only with -tod or -todFrom.

It reaches the player too. The briefing's day/night choice (HudToolkit.cs:546) calls the same SetNight, so picking night in the old town gives day without saying so.

The fix (Latch builds it, I give the values):

  1. Make one driver own the play light: night writes OldTownPlayLook's sun, ambient and probe fields, the way TimeOfDay097 already does (it has the moon, the dark ambient, the exposure, the lamp lights and the night APV scenario). SetNight(true) in the old town sets a fixed night hour on that driver.
  2. Give the old town a LightPhase for everything else that reads it: RoadLight (road strips, lit windows, people fill), SignalLight's night colours, the glows and the night grade. Build it at swap time from the night data Glint077 already copies out of HarbourDistrict, rather than keeping the lighting root.
  3. No hidden fallback. If a shot or the briefing asks for night and the scene can't do it, the shot fails with an error and the briefing doesn't offer night. As a quick check, the batch also fails a night shot whose mean brightness is within 10 % of its day pair.

Haze at the play camera

The catch. The play camera is orthographic, about 20 m tall at zoom 10. From the bottom of the frame to the top, the ground is only about 26 m further from the camera. Distance fog over 26 m is nearly the same everywhere, so it would grey the whole frame evenly. That's why the fog is off at play zoom today, along with URP's fog not working under an orthographic camera. Haze has to come from something other than distance:

  • Ground mist: soft patches a metre or two deep, drifting down-wind and lying thickest over open road and water, as in the concept. It's the same world-space noise and wind we already use for the cloud shadows.
  • Height haze: a little more haze at street level than on the deck (9.43 m) or the sky concourse (24 m). This also helps tell the levels apart.
  • A slight lift toward the top of the frame, scaled by zoom: barely there at zoom 10, more when zoomed out for planning.

How

built-in One full-screen pass of our own on the Render Graph. It reads the depth, works out each pixel's world position, and adds mist by height and noise. It runs before transparents, so the target rings, outlines and signals all draw over it. Values go in haze.json under a new play tier, so DistanceHaze keeps being the one place haze is set.

asset Kronnect Volumetric Fog & Mist 2: Render Graph ready, v31.4.2 (18 Sep 2026), lists orthographic support. It does far more than we need (ray-marched volumes, light shafts) and would overlap our haze. I'd only trial it if our pass fails the look.

Readability

  • Signals: safe. They draw after post-processing (SignalsFeature, AfterRenderingPostProcessing), so mist never reaches them.
  • People: a risk. Mist at foot height eats the 80 px figures' feet and contact shade. Cap it at about 15 % on anything taller than knee height, and thin it to nothing inside a few metres of a selected agent or the ring's focus.
  • Ring and outlines: safe if the pass runs before transparents (the rings are ground decals in the transparent queue).
Recommendation Our own ground-mist and height-haze pass, not distance fog. It's a morning or after-rain look, not on all the time.

Night

Where we are. Most of night is built, but in the HarbourDistrict scene (LightPhase, RoadLight, the night colours in signals.json, the night floor in lens.json) and in TimeOfDay097 (moon, lamps, the night APV blend). The old town that Sky plays gets none of it (see the finding above).

How

built-in All of it: the fix above, then match the concept:

  • Lit rooms: look097.json rooms nightShare and night are both 0 today, so the rooms behind the glass go dark at night. Light about half of them.
  • The seafoam inlay bands and the planter feet as light set in the ground: new emission on those kit materials (Vela). RoadLight's glow list switches them on at dusk, with no new lights.
  • Lamp pools: shadowless point lights at the lamp heads, which TimeOfDay097.Lamps() already places; Forward+ handles dozens of them. Table lamps under the parasols take a small pool each.
  • Bloom stays clamped at 4. No pool may be brighter than a signal.

asset None needed.

Readability

  • Signals: safe. They're unlit and drawn after post, with their own night colours in signals.json.
  • People: a risk, already handled once. Dark clothes go black. RoadLight's people fill and rim fixed this in HarbourDistrict (Glyph's 0.2.115 check), and it has to come across to the old town.
  • Warm pools beside the guard's amber: a small risk. The guard reads by shape and motion, and the pools are soft and round. Glyph checks one frame.
  • A violet lamp beside a rival's ring: already handled (lens.json turns lilac lamps to flamingo).
Recommendation Fix night in the old town first. It's mostly wiring what exists, plus three new glows from Vela, and it's what the rain look builds on.

Rain

Rain is four separate jobs. Most of the look comes from the wet ground, not the falling rain.

Readability

  • Signals: safe. They're drawn after post, so on top of rain and wetness.
  • Ring: a small risk. Rain streaks are transparent and can draw across the ground rings. Draw the rings after the rain, and keep the streaks thin and pale.
  • Wet glare on the ground near red, amber and violet: a small risk. Signals don't own their colours, but a red umbrella's reflection under a cone's edge muddies it. The streak decals take the warm lamp and cream colours only, never a saturated hue.
  • People: the biggest risk. From 38° above, an open umbrella hides most of an 80 px figure, so the crowd turns into discs. Keep umbrellas to a share of the crowd (about a third), in pale or translucent canopies. The cell never carries umbrellas. They're visitors and stand out because they don't.
  • Outlines and the occlusion cut: safe. They aren't lit.
Recommendation Wetness, puddles and the streak decals first. Those three make the concept on their own. Falling rain second. Hold back SSR.

Overcast, wind, mist

  • Overcast: an almost free day variant. More cloud-cookie cover (look097.json clouds cover), a weaker and softer sun, a cooler and higher ambient. Risk: no shadows means less form, so keep the contact shade. It pairs with rain.
  • Wind: already built (Look097 wind into every shader). A gusty setting for rain moves the awnings, parasols and umbrellas. Free.
  • After the rain: puddles stay while the paving dries in patches, and steam rises from the grates (already built). This makes a lovely start state, and is cheap once rain exists.
  • Not worth it: volumetric clouds, sky systems, lightning and snow. The sky is barely in frame at 38°, and snow is a whole new material pass for a city that may never see it.

What it costs on the Air (estimates)

Measured base (brief 097, 15-inch Air M4, 1920 × 1080 windowed): GPU time at play zoom 5.0–6.7 ms, 120–160 fps. The 60 fps budget is 16.7 ms. Native fullscreen 2880 × 1864 has about 2.6 times the pixels, so full-screen work costs roughly 2.6 times as much there (brief 106 is measuring STP for exactly that).

Questions for Sky

Undecided until Sky rules; the GDD changes only after that.

1. Does weather or night ever change play, or are they only a look?

Today the GDD says night is a look only, and no rule reads light. Decision-log question Q7 is still open.

  1. Look only. The same mission, the same rules, the same sweep. Cheap and safe, and it keeps the focus on the Handover.
  2. Look, plus the city behaves differently. In rain, people shelter under awnings and the café tables empty. Sight rules stay the same, but where people stand changes, so the Handover plays differently (Inez's café at an empty terrace). This needs Pulse and Keel, plus a sweep for each weather.
  3. Full rules. Shorter sight at night and in rain, faces hidden under umbrellas (harder to recognise the cell, harder to Watch), and rain covering noise. This suits social infiltration best, but it's a big round: every price, band and check, and the HUD has to show it, or it breaks "readable over rich".

Recommendation: A now, and plan for B later. Keep the weather in the seed's start state from day one, so a later rule can read it without breaking saved runs. Avoid C unless the HUD can show its effect plainly.

2. Who picks the weather and the time of day?
  1. Rolled by the seed, per game. Every game differs, as brief 080 wants, and it's shown on the briefing.
  2. Chosen at the briefing. The day/night choice is already there; add weather beside it.
  3. Fixed per mission. The Handover is always a sunny afternoon.
  4. Rolled by the seed, changeable at the briefing. The roll sets it and the player can change it.

Recommendation: D while it's a look only (there's no reason to lock a look), moving to A if it ever changes play (then it's part of the challenge, and choosing it would be a cheat).

3. Can the weather change during a mission?
  1. No. One state for the whole mission (8–12 minutes).
  2. Yes, at a time shown on the briefing. For example, a shower at 3:00 and dry by 6:00, shown like the other key world events.

Recommendation: A to start. B is a nice later touch, and it fits "show time only for key world events".

Summary

  1. Fix night in the old town first. The batch's night shot is daylight because OldTown.unity has no LightPhase, and SetNight quietly falls back to day. The briefing's night choice has the same bug. One driver should own the play light; the shot should fail loudly when night isn't possible.
  2. Rain next, built ourselves: wetness and puddles in our shaders, reflection streak decals, and VFX Graph rain. About 0.4–1.1 ms at 1080p (estimate). No SSR for now.
  3. Haze as ground mist and height haze in one pass of our own. Distance fog does nothing at an orthographic play camera.
  4. Overcast and gusty wind come almost free from what's built.
  5. Buy nothing yet. Hold Volumetric Fog & Mist 2 and Shiny SSR 2 as trials only if our versions fall short. Skip Enviro and COZY.
  6. Readability: signals are safe (drawn after post). Draw the rings after the rain. Keep umbrellas pale and on a share of the crowd, and never on the cell.
  7. For Sky: look only for now (A), rolled by the seed and changeable at the briefing (D), one weather per mission (A).
