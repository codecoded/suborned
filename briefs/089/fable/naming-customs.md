## D. Normal names, the way GTA does it

**What GTA and its peers do** (only what I could check):
- **GTA:** the leads have real, plain names that fit where they come from. Niko Bellic is an Eastern European immigrant who grew up in the Yugoslav wars. Carl "CJ" Johnson is from Los Santos. GTA V has Michael De Santa (born Townley, hiding under a new name), Franklin Clinton and Trevor Philips.
- **Flavour comes from nicknames,** not invented names: CJ, and Big Smoke, whose real name is Melvin Harris.
- **The satire lives in brands and places:** Sprunk for Sprite, Cluckin' Bell for KFC, Los Santos for Los Angeles.
- **Pedestrians:** I couldn't find how GTA names them.
- **Watch Dogs: Legion:** every Londoner is generated. Its director says a person's background sets the name they show. I couldn't confirm it draws from real census lists.
- **Desperados III:** plain names that fit each person, plus one nickname: John Cooper, Arthur "Doc" McCoy, Hector Mendoza, Kate O'Hara, Isabelle Moreau.

**The principle:** real, plausible names that fit a person's origin, age and class. Flavour comes from nicknames and how people are introduced, and the satire lives in brands and places.

**The custom for us.** Real given and family names, drawn by rule from real name-frequency lists for each origin, and matched to the person's origin and sex. Age is also wanted, but the trial lists can't do it yet.
- The top 3 of each national list are skipped, so the city isn't all Smiths.
- Ban-list names are skipped.
- No model chooses: a person's draw comes from their id, as it does now.

# The three customs (A–C), for comparison

A–C each invent a custom. My pick among them is A, Card names: it's the one idea you can say in a sentence, and it says something quietly unpleasant about the place. For Sky's ask, D comes first.

## A. Card names

**The custom.** When the city first registered everyone, the card had five columns for a family name. Whatever didn't fit was cut. Three hundred years on, the cut name is the name. Grandmothers may remember the whole one. Given names were left alone.

**Why here.** Records are paper, order comes first, and the state shaped people to fit its form. The real precedent is the Philippines in 1849, where a governor handed towns a catalogue of surnames to adopt.

**Rule for the generator.** Take a real family name of six letters or more, with its origin. Cut it to five letters. Drop letters from the end until it can be said. Throw out banned names, English words and anything famous.
Today: 632 family names, about 779,000 full names.

## B. Joined names

**The custom.** Atomic power made travel cheap, and people came from everywhere. When two families marry, the Register writes the first half of one name and an ending from another. Every family name is a record of two peoples meeting.

**Why here.** It shows a mixed city inside each name, and only here could those names exist. Of the three, it's closest to "old languages drifted".

**Rule for the generator.** Take the opening of a real family name and join a known family-name ending from a different part of the world. Keep 5 to 8 letters. Throw out real surnames, English words and banned names.
Today: 6,042 family names, about 7.4 million full names.

## C. Trade names

**The custom.** The city was raised by crews. The Register wrote each family down by the work it did on the great projects, in the city's own speech. Your family name is your ancestor's job, the way Smith and Wainwright once were. Your given name is still your grandmother's language.

**Why here.** It needs no decoding, and it puts paper and atomic trades in every name.

**Rule for the generator.** Take a thing from the world's list (card, reel, lamp, pile, rail, dock, bell) and add -ward (keeper), -wright (maker), -smith or -man. A person's own job may never match their name, so no clerk is called Cardwright.
Today: 112 family names, about 138,000 full names.

**Cast**

## Two faults in today's names, whichever custom you pick

- **Given names ignore sex.** The people data has no such field, so the guard is Folake, a woman's name, and Vale is Efua, also a woman's name. The pool needs a sex tag and each person needs one. I didn't check how the driver's figure reads.

## Customs I tried and dropped

- **Named for your street** (Miss Lantern): collides with real places, and "Call Lantern off the door" reads as an object.
- **Generation sounds** (all children of one decade share a syllable): comes out as generic fantasy.
- **Phonetic spelling by the Register** (Voytek, Shavon): tidy, but reads as misspelled or just anglicised, with little that's unique.
- **Named after whoever signed for you** (the name shows who's tied to whom): a clever link to the game, but the endings blur together and it needs decoding.
- **Register marks** (letters and numbers): sci-fi cliché, and it breaks the no-digits rule.
- **Double-barrelled names for the cell:** Okonkwo-Reyes and Okafor-Voss are already on the lists.
