# Research notes for brief 062 (Glyph, 2026-10-24)

Web searches were thin: none returned a description of a briefing screen's layout. What each source gave, and what is from memory of playing and therefore not sourced.

## From sources
- **Phantom Doctrine**: the investigation board is the signature screen: documents pinned to a corkboard, connections joined by red string, words in notes highlighted as leads. Reviewers like it as the place where clues become a plan. https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2017/09/04/why-phantom-doctrine-was-my-favourite-game-at-gamescom-2017/ , https://techraptor.net/content/xcom-meets-cloak-dagger-in-phantom-doctrine . The Guardian faulted it for not teaching the player, so a board still needs a clear reading order. (search result summary: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_Doctrine)
- **Syndicate (1993)**: the briefing shows a scrollable map of the level; a 1993 Amiga review praises the briefing, hints and team-equip screens as "beautifully drawn", and a failed mission plays an animation in which the syndicate leader throws something through the hologram of the mission blueprint. The blueprint is the mission; the loss happens to it. https://tcrf.net/Syndicate_(1993,_PC) , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syndicate_(1993_video_game)
- **Hitman 3**: planning is its own phase: loadout and starting place chosen before the level, and you can re-plan. Targets are highlighted in the world with instinct. Planning is a place you return to, not a one-way card. https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2021/01/26/hitman-3-best-loadout-weapons-unlocks/
- **Shadow Tactics / Desperados III** (Mimimi): you can open the whole map at any time; each mission has hidden objectives found in play; Desperados ends a mission with an animated map of the route you took. The map is a tool during play and a memory after it. https://www.gamepressure.com/shadowtactics/ , https://www.gamepressure.com/desperados-iii/
- **Deus Ex (Human Revolution)**: a handler voice (Pritchard, Jacobson in the first game) gives the objectives and the entry options over the intercom while you are already playing; the opening introduces the HUD in the first room. Briefing is a person talking, and the first minutes are the tutorial. https://deusex.fandom.com/wiki/Francis_Pritchard , https://shacknews.com/article/67639/deus-ex-human-revolution-hands-1
- **Invisible, Inc.**: pick two agents before the first mission; the objective depends on the facility type (usually: get one item out, then escape). Few choices, shown as faces. https://support.klei.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029880011-What-is-Invisible-Inc

## Not found by search (from memory of playing; treat as unsourced)
- **XCOM 2**: the mission is a card on the strategic map with its reward and a clock; the squad select is a row of soldier portraits; the drop-in is a short cutscene of the dropship before control. Strength: the squad is faces, the pay-off and risk are two numbers, and the camera arrives with a shot, not a cut.
- **Hitman 3 / Desperados / Shadow Tactics** briefing: a handler or narrator over an illustrated map or still, then a fly-in. Not verified.

## What it adds up to (the rules the three directions follow)
1. The target is a face, never a paragraph (Syndicate, Hitman, Deus Ex all name a person).
2. The map is the briefing; routes are lines on it (Syndicate blueprint, Desperados route map). Ours is the real street, because our game is already a map seen from above.
3. The cell is faces in a row (XCOM, Invisible Inc).
4. The first seconds teach (Deus Ex): the camera arrives where the commander stands, and the HUD arrives after it.
5. A board that you can return to (Hitman planning, Shadow Tactics map): out of scope here, but Intel is that board in play.
