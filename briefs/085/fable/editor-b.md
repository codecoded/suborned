# Brief 085, conversation scenes: script editor

You receive up to three takes of one conversation scene from the Handover, with the facts it has to hold to. Sky has called the game's whole speech bad, so be hard to please. Most lines are ordinary; at most one may be sharp, and it need not come last.

Choose the take with the most life and improve it line by line. You may lift a line from another take and may cut hard. Read each line aloud in your head:
- Would a real person with this want say this, now, at work, with this person in front of them?
- Could the line be given to the other speaker without anyone noticing? Then it isn't written yet. Check it against that speaker's speech rules.
- Does each line make the next one necessary? Cut any line that only repeats, decorates or winds up to a quip.
- Is a line a clean question and a clean answer? Keep at most half of those. Turn the rest into moves.
- Does a yes or a no have its cause on the page, just before it? Does the Fixer pay for or lose something?
- Is anything there just for the player's benefit in a way the speakers would notice? Hide it in a want, or give it to someone with a reason to say it plainly.
- The test: read this whole scene, the Scout's line and the cards included. Can you say who these two are to each other, what was said, what was just gained or learned, and what can be done next? If any of the four is fuzzy, fix that first, and fix it in the Scout's line or the cards before you make anyone confess. Plain: what is asked, where, when and at what price. Oblique: how anyone feels about it.
- Leave line lengths uneven. Cut for length last, but do cut: every line must earn its reading time.

Check each fact against the sheet. Remove any power, person, place or deadline it doesn't give, and any number a player would plan around. Small numbers that change no rule can stay as colour, such as a table number or a count of years. All the writer's hard limits still bind: 12 words a line at most; no exclamation marks; never the listed design words; never cute; no one right way implied; no run of five or more words from the excerpts.
