Brief 099 voices, step 1 (Fable). Nothing below is in the game yet: text.json changes only after your yes. The new lines are mine, written to the new bar: they are its gold set, the level the loop's rewrite has to reach.

What changed in the bar

  • Worth leads. A line exists only if it tells you something new or lands a character at the right moment. Otherwise it's silence, and cutting it counts as a good result.
  • Voice is scored on its own. Each agent has a voice sheet (below). A line another agent could say unchanged fails. A line can be true, short and plain and still fail on voice.
  • People talking, not status codes. Whole thoughts and contractions. Variants are different takes, not synonyms. No seconds, counts or rule words, except a key event's time, as your rule on numbers has it.
  • Calibrated. The judge sees today's flat lines as failing examples and the gold lines as passing ones. Every rule gets its own score, and only a 10 on all of them passes.
  • Speak less. Agents speak on a decision, not a click (below). The old bar even nudged the Enforcer towards two-word codes, and that nudge is gone.

The four voices

Scout

  1. Quick, bright and talkative: the first to see it, and glad to say so.
  2. Leads with the detail she caught, in a couple of quick sentences.
  3. A little cheeky with you when it's going well; dead plain when it isn't.

Never: “Eyes on.”, “Copy.”, “In position.”: report codes · Anything she hasn't seen or heard herself · A joke you have to decode · The plan spelled out for you

Fixer

  1. Warm and unhurried, even when it's going wrong: the calm the cell steadies on.
  2. Says “we” and means it; courteous to everyone, the people she turns included.
  3. Steel under the kindness: she names the cost plainly, and keeps her word.

Never: A threat she wouldn't keep · Contempt for the people they turn · Jargon: “asset”, “target”, “leverage”, “proceeding” · Panic, or gloating

Tech

  1. Calm, patient and dry: nothing in this city's machinery surprises him.
  2. Fond of its clockwork the way you're fond of an old dog: sure of it, unimpressed.
  3. Exact about what's wired to what, never a number; one dry aside, then back to work.

Never: “Hacking”, “system”, “network”, “online”: words this world never had · “Logging.”, “Confirmed.”: a machine's report, not a man's · A time or a count (“two minutes”) · Technical babble

Enforcer

  1. Big, older and calm: says as little as he can, and makes it count.
  2. Few words, but a man talking, never a radio: dry, a bit tired.
  3. Gallows humour when it's grim; he weighs a kill before he does it.

Never: “Holstered.”, “In place.”, “Noted. Done.”, “Here. Watching.”: a status code is not a man talking · Bravado (“Let's do this.”) · A speech · Jargon: “hostile”, “engage”, “neutralise”
