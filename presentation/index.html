The Suborned

Suborn the people, control the city.
Single-player real-time tactics with social infiltration · PC · Unity
Sky Rumsey · built with a crew of 17 AI agents
  • Three weeks ago this was eight paintings and a design document. Today it's a playable mission in Unity, running on a deterministic simulation.
  • The tour: the idea, where it started, how it's made (the AI crew and the build-time pipeline), then the map, the people, the simulation, the capstone mission and the play.
The idea

The city carries out your plan without knowing it.

You command a cell of four. You turn ordinary people (a waitress, a guard, a driver) and time their small acts into one plan.

Subvert people

Knowing more about someone makes them cheaper, quieter or more loyal.

Orchestrate the city

Every system opens or closes a window that you can see coming.

Live with it

You'll meet every choice again: people remember, and the city follows the trail back.

The loop Watch→Learn→Turn→Time→Take→Fight→Inherit

Every order shows its full price before you commit: $, time, Trail (evidence) and Alert risk.

Scoutwatches and listens
Fixerturns people, makes calls
Techloops cameras, reroutes patrols
Enforcertakes and fights
  • The fantasy is commander, not avatar. You never shoot your way through; you arrange for the city to do it for you.
  • Three pillars test every feature. Tiebreakers in order: readable over rich, people over powers, systems over scripts, preparation over improvisation.
  • Trail is campaign evidence; Alert 0–3 is how security responds right now.
The world

A 1950s world that discovered atomic power centuries ago.

Everything physical is abundant: sky rail, skyports, towers, liners.
No thinking machines, no networks. Robots run fixed routines; records are paper and microfilm; calls are by voice.
So information moves through people. People are the weak point, and that's what the game is about.
An abundant city that fears disorder: optimistic surface, coercive underneath.
  • Sky's premise from 14 October: an alternate history, not a forecast. Culture and style are 1950s; technology is centuries ahead.
  • It's also a design rule: no internet, so every fact reaches security through a person. That's why turning people works, and why witnesses matter.
  • Design test for every object: 1950s style, centuries-ahead technology (crossings have floating signal eyes, never a zebra).

People · 3 · the lovers

A secret the city generated, and the mission casts.

The Handover names roles, not people. Rule M-2 casts each role from the generated city and pins it by id. Inez's need is her debt (Help clears it); her secret is the affair (Pressure threatens to expose it).

$ bun run people:graph query lovers a_name b_position secret at Freya Antoniou guard affair evening Inez Nikolaou guard affair evening

This cast made Inez Fearful, so Help works one step worse: two Helps, $80,000, not $40,000. The city set that price, not a script.

  • Story canon from 15 October: Inez and the door guard are lovers. His pre-shift café stop is to see her. The call that pulls him off the door is the capstone.
  • The lover tie is generated like any other (mutual, secret), so it shows up in the graph queries and in the simulation's witness rules: kin and lovers report at once.
  • The viewer flagged the side effect: Fearful Inez makes Help dearer, so route A costs $80,000. We kept it; it's a fair consequence of the cast.

Simulation · 2 · how the city finds out

A fact reaches security only through a person.

A takedown, a carry, a body→ Seen within 13 m, clear line→ Reacts by temperament→ Walks to a phone→ Report lands: Alert rises

Staff radio in 5 s · kin and lovers report at once · Loyal report · Mercenaries keep quiet until Alert 2 · the terrified flee · a turned witness says nothing.

Only acts seen or found move it. A public fight moves it once, by opponent. Between missions, word of mouth: each person you helped tells their ties.

Hero turns people slower and dearer but loyal; Menace faster and cheaper, with betrayal.

  • No networks in this world, so nothing is automatic: a silent act raises Alert only when someone's report lands. That gives the player a window to stop the reporter.
  • The response is a dial (S0–S4) kept as data per district and mission; Sky's lever for difficulty. The Handover runs at S2.
  • Standing starts at +2, leaning Hero (Sky: "if you're not seen then everyone assumes you're good"). Turning conversations are private, except Pressure.
