How the submitted run went (all unseeded)

  • Round 1: the checker failed N-R1, with four areas out of reach (docks-east 294 m, lantern-west 289 m, harbour-east 279 m, rocket-west 273 m).
    • Keel diagnosed it as a line problem and routed it to Datum: no stop on the existing lines could reach those areas.
    • Vantage raised the same point as a blocking hand-off to Datum.
    • Vantage moved Skyport to x 545 (gap 205 m) and answered Datum's spacing ask done: true; Keel confirmed it.
  • Round 2: Datum added T2 as a ring, which fixed reach. The checker then failed T-L1, because it read the ring's two ends as open with no turning loop.
    • Keel explained the cause and routed it to Datum.
    • Vantage answered one of Datum's asks done: false, which became a fault for Vantage.
  • Round 3: Datum made T2 an open U with both ends running off the sheet, which T-L1 accepts ("ends joined or off-sheet"). Vantage placed its 7 stops and answered every ask. The checker was clean and Keel passed: ACCEPTED.

Datum re-asked the Skyport move in rounds 2 and 3, although it was already done. Its task shows the input plan's geometry, not Vantage's placed stops, so two of Vantage's four done: true answers repeat an ask already met. The self-assessment lists this as a weakness.

In r008 Keel's two faults restate checker failures, with a diagnosis and the right owner, and it raised no fault of its own. Its independent catches are in earlier runs (see "Earlier runs, kept as evidence").

Retrieval: the GDD's boarding rule (§02 … › travel) reached Pulse at rank 4 and Keel at rank 8, with no rule text in the prompts. Pulse's and Keel's queries didn't change between rounds and nothing was pinned for them, so for those two this is one result seen three times. Datum's queries did change (4, 6 and 5 queries, with 2 chunks pinned in rounds 2 and 3). Vantage's were identical in rounds 1 and 2, and round 3 added one (the text of a hand-off). The natural question on its own ranks the rule 1st: run the level-2 retrieval command in the README. Pulse also cited §02, so the passage reached Keel a second way, as cited text. Source mix: Datum's and Vantage's top 8 were all plan chunks in every round; Pulse's and Keel's had 1–2 agent-GDD chunks each. No human-GDD passage was retrieved. The self-assessment lists this as a weakness. Keel judged the timetable against it ("time only, no fare").

Seeded critic challenges: r010 (uncited) and r009 (cited), both ACCEPTED

Brief 001 allows a seeded challenge, labelled as seeded everywhere. Each seed plants one false claim in Pulse's round-1 notes. The claim contradicts GDD v0.15 §02 travel ("Price = time only: wait for the next service + the ride"). No checker rule or code check reads notes, so only the critic can catch it.

What these runs do and don't show: in both, Pulse's own unseeded notes already state the true rule in the same list. r010 note 2 says "Boarding is fast travel priced in time only, with no fare (GDD §02 travel)", and r009 note 3 says "priced in time only (wait + ride, no fare)". So Keel could catch the planted claim as a contradiction inside Pulse's own output, without checking it against the GDD. The runs show that the critic reads the outputs and routes a content fault to its owner, who repairs it. They don't prove that the critic knows the GDD. A stronger seed would remove Pulse's own statement of the rule as well.

Every seeded run is labelled:

  • in run.json (seeded);
  • in round-1/pulse.call.json (seeded, seedNote, and original, Pulse's untouched output).

Neither Keel nor Pulse is told. bun run levels:replay checks that round 1 equals the original plus the seed, and reports in each later round whether the claim survived.
