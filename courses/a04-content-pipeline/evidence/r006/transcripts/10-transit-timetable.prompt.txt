# Objective · timetable (Pulse, brief 001 output 3)
You choose; code computes. Per line you state `speed` (m/s, required), `dwellSec` and `headwaySec`. Per journey you state the ordered `legs`: `{ from, to }` stops, one line per leg. Do not write run times or journey totals: they are not in your schema. Code derives them from the plan checker's stop table (the chainage gaps shown in your task), so they are always right for the stops as they stand this round, and your speeds, dwells and legs are carried into the next round unchanged unless a fault says otherwise.

The rule (one rule, used everywhere):
- run sec = ceil(gap ÷ speed), gap = the "to next" metres in the stop table, whole metres.
- journey sec = rides + dwells + interchange walks. Ride = the run secs between a leg's two stops (either direction on the line). Dwell = the line's `dwellSec` at every stop passed through inside a leg, not at the boarding or alighting stop. Interchange walk = ceil(straight-line metres between the two paired stops ÷ 1.4 m/s) [S]: the GDD states no walking pace.
- The wait for the next service is not in a journey total: it comes from the line's headway (GDD §02: boarding is fast travel priced in time only: the wait plus the ride). No fare, no cooldown, no other cost.

Journey legs. A new leg starts where you change lines: the previous leg ends at a stop that names the next leg's start stop as its `interchange` partner (or the reverse), and the two are on different lines. A change at the same stop, or between stops that are not an interchange pair, is a fault for you (rule `timetable`), as is a stop that does not exist. Route choice is yours: which journeys matter, and which interchange to use.

A run or journey names stops as `"<line>/<stop id>"`. Do not invent powers. Anything beyond time (whether boarding costs anything else, limits on who may ride) is not decided: put it under `openQuestions` for Motive, not in the timetable. State your chosen speed per line in `notes` with the reason for it (headways, dwell and speed are [S] starting values).
Hand-offs: optional `handoffs`: `[{ owner, blocking, ask }]` for what another builder must change (owner Datum, Vantage or Pulse, never yourself). `blocking: true` means your output cannot be accepted until they act: it goes to them as a fault next round. `blocking: false` is advisory and is only recorded. Leave it out when you need nothing.
